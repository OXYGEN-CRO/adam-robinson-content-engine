# Art direction and motion craft

Use this before composing scenes. Its purpose is to prevent a technically valid slideshow from being delivered as a finished launch film.

## Define the visual argument

Write one sentence describing what visibly happens, without marketing adjectives. Example: “A plain-language request becomes a usable lead list, and its cursor carries us into the brand.” The action must communicate the product promise without requiring a paragraph of explanation.

For a new direction, sketch three distinct approaches and select one against product meaning, brand fit and execution feasibility. Changing the palette or transition preset does not make a different approach. An 8–10 second brief usually needs one central action and a deliberate landing, not a miniature feature tour.

## Study a reference precisely

Choose a professional reference for craft and, when useful, an executable reference for implementation. Inspect the actual imagery and motion available; separate a studio's description, sampled frames, normal-speed playback and subjective listening in the evidence log. Do not claim a viewing or listening pass that did not happen.

Record the relevant moment and the mechanism: what the eye follows, which object persists, where movement starts and stops, how scale changes, and why the cut feels connected. Read the matching implementation rather than approximating an effect from its name. Treat exact durations and springs as starting points tuned to the reference's geometry, not universal laws.

Useful research entry points:

- [video-shotcraft gallery](https://vincentwei1021.github.io/video-shotcraft/): working shot previews and corresponding Remotion components. Select a mechanism, then design it around the actual brand assets.
- [product-launch-motion creative direction](https://github.com/AbubakrChan/product-launch-motion/blob/main/references/11-creative-direction.md): concept selection and deliberate departures from a generic house style.
- [animation-techniques-kit method](https://github.com/platan821/animation-techniques-kit/blob/main/METHOD.md): reference breakdown and a small motion prototype before a full sequence.

The research memo records pinned versions and qualifications. These are references, not automatic installation instructions. Follow the current user scope, local brand and applicable licences.

## Build a continuous action

Track a subject across the sequence: a cursor, selected record, line or real product element. Define its position and velocity on each side of a transition. A cut can preserve direction, shape or meaning; it does not need a crossfade. Camera movement needs a destination and a reason. Independent floating cards should not substitute for a spatial idea.

Use changes in pace intentionally. Give viewers time to understand the input, accelerate the transformation, then let the result settle. Constant movement and constant speed both weaken emphasis. A still ending is a legitimate design choice.

For MoltSets, preserve the pixel silhouette, square geometry, terminal typography and coral/mint roles. Deliberate visual roughness can coexist with precise spacing and timing. Mascot movement should express a specific reaction; an enlarged static mascot is not a performance. There is no quota for effects, layers, blur, glow, camera moves or sprite appearances.

## Prototype before extending

Render the hardest 2–3 seconds with the real production assets and exact intended framing. Compare it with the relevant reference. Inspect motion paths, accidental easing resets, subpixel shimmer, masking, collisions and the readable settled state. If the visual cause and effect is unclear, revise the concept or motion before adding scenes and aspect ratios.

Product captures must be authentic. A graphic interpretation can explain a concept, but must be identified during review and cannot substantiate claims about product speed or results. Do not use fictional names and counts to simulate proof.

## Compose the sound

Treat sound as a short phrase: preparation, event and resolution. Choose timbres that suit the visible material. A dry key sound can make a terminal action tactile; a restrained final tone can complete the brand gesture. Do not attach a whoosh to every entrance. Select an existing music phrase with a usable ending, or let sparse effects carry a very short film. Loudness measurement complements listening; it does not replace it.

## Review in this order

1. Meaning: can a viewer describe the product benefit after one muted viewing?
2. Identity: does the film's central action belong to this product and brand?
3. Motion: do attention, direction and pace remain intentional through each transition?
4. Sound: do the cues support the visible actions, with a convincing ending?
5. Technical delivery: fonts, clipping, colour, frame integrity, metadata and editability.

Record concrete defects and corrections. Avoid aesthetic scores unsupported by a reference comparison. If playback or listening is unavailable, mark that assessment as pending and report the narrower checks actually performed.
