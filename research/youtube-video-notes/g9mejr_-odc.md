## Tensions and corrections

- Kyle's headline argument is that enduring outbound fundamentals still work, not that a wholly new playbook has appeared. The title should not obscure this distinction.
- Kyle criticizes treating every signal as purchase intent, then explicitly endorses signal-triggered outreach for ClickUp's PLG users. His labor-intensive approach is specifically scoped to cold executive outreach.
- The research play still uses relatively broad outreach to employees to obtain a few conversations. Its objection is to indiscriminate executive pitching, not to all volume or automation.
- Kyle promises greater efficiency from intensive account research, but supplies no comparative cost, conversion, or pipeline dataset. The economics remain conditional on deal size, audience, and required wins.
- Kyle alternates between approximately 1% and 0.1% participation language; hosts repeat 0.1% as an achiever or reply-rate comparison. These are rhetorical and caption-sensitive figures, not reliable benchmarks.
- Kyle's example requires five deals per quarter; Pete later describes five deals per month. Neither is established as ClickUp's actual quota, and the periods should not be merged.
- RB2B's seven-day AI operation is a planned experiment. Pete's later shorthand that Adam runs the entire company with AI is broader than the documented staffing and future test establish.

- Retention's captioned '20 million AR' is not clean enough to silently normalize into a verified dollar ARR figure. It is not evidence of profit, cash flow, or annual recognized revenue.
- ClickUp's $100 million figure is Kyle's incremental-pipeline target, not achieved pipeline, booked revenue, or Adam's result. ClickUp's 40 SDRs and 70-person wider team are different scopes rather than an obvious contradiction.
- Clean Bill's $800–$1,000 tax-saving claim, 30%–75% savings range, and Adam's tentative $3,000 recap describe different or insufficiently defined quantities. The recap is not a confirmed correction.
- The Clean Bill poll shifts between an unexplained 84, 92 yes votes, 70%, and 94 willing buyers; demo interest is much lower at one point. This supports distinguishing stated interest from action, but not calculating a final conversion rate.
- Kyle explicitly qualifies his understanding of AI calling law and gives a broad claim about cold texting. These statements are not verified legal guidance, and no jurisdiction or consent conditions are established.

- Adam's public-transparency claim and the planned Warmly debate do not establish a funding history, a detailed bootstrapping-versus-VC position, or which business the audience ultimately preferred.
