## Summary

The webinar presents signal stacking as a process of defining customer fit, identifying meaningful behavior, enriching and scoring records, routing prospects into suitable outreach, and measuring funnel outcomes. Adam contributes unusually candid accounts of RB2B's initial pricing, conversion and customer-instruction mistakes, while advocating clear messaging and customer discovery before elaborate marketing systems. Heath supplies the operating framework and Mixmax case studies; a substantial live workshop with CJ distinguishes engagement from buying readiness and reserves human outreach for sufficiently qualified prospects. The final discussion addresses enterprise accounts, enrichment, rep productivity and Mixmax's decision to grow without further fundraising. The title's $100m/$1m framing is aspirational, not a demonstrated revenue result or itemized budget.

## Tensions and corrections

- The title's $100 million ARR/$1 million ARR comparison is positioning. The spoken material does not demonstrate either an ARR achievement or a costed stack delivering equivalent results.
- Adam explicitly changes direction from his 2024 mass-outreach messaging toward a 2025 message discouraging that approach. Preserve the temporal change rather than presenting either position as timeless.
- The strong ICP-first advice coexists with Adam's admission that RB2B launched without an ICP and his later agreement that free sign-ups can help discover one. The discussion supports a distinction between discovery and scaling, not a universal prohibition on acquiring users before defining fit.
- Adam's early RB2B pricing was an idea involving a not-yet-built CRM integration. The $495/$500 references should not be treated as current pricing or multiplied by the 13 paying customers to infer ARR.
- Adam's proposed CRM-and-TAM workflow is explicitly still a hypothesis, even though he believes it may be the best way to use RB2B.
- Heath reports one closed holiday-campaign deal; Adam immediately describes four days to a demo. Preserve that outcome mismatch. There is no basis to equate this deal with the separate $100,000 Mixmax deal.
- The spoken date is the 17th and publication is December 18, 2024. December 17 is the likely recording date; publication must not be substituted for all campaign events.
- Adam endorses job-change signals while acknowledging saturation. Heath's stronger use case is a former customer changing employers, not indiscriminate outreach to every newly hired executive.
- Heath's enterprise-never-self-serves statement is immediately qualified by his own examples of occasional self-service by individual enterprise users.
- A cheaper collection of tools is not necessarily cheaper overall: Heath favors Common Room for Mixmax after considering technical labor, while still using Clay for experiments.
- CJ and Heath distinguish behavioral engagement from budget, buying readiness and organizational approval. High scores justify attention, not an automatic quote or guaranteed sale.
- The live workshop produces a proposed flow and scoring outline. Integrations, calibrated scores and execution remain to be implemented, so it is not a completed production deployment.
- Caption uncertainty includes Joe's Meyer/Mayer surname, Digital Visor's exact spelling, and a frozen/audio-corrupted CJ passage around 00:34:45–00:34:54 captioned as '$80 a year.' That fragment cannot support a revenue or company-age claim.
- Mixmax's approximately 2018 Series B followed by organic growth does not establish profitability or a bootstrapped history. Adam's enthusiastic response supports approval of this decision, not a categorical anti-VC doctrine.
- No Adam-company ARR, profit, cash balance, FTE total or contractor count is disclosed. The $100,000 customer deal, guest client names and other guest achievements must retain their original attribution.
