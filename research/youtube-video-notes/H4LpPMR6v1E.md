## Tensions and corrections

- The community price is materially inconsistent in captions: $29.97, a one-time description, an explicit annual correction, 297 monthly thereafter, and approximately $3,000 per year. Preserve the correction and uncertainty rather than choosing a precise reconstructed price.
- The included software plan is captioned as costing 3.49 per month. Its exact price cannot be established from this text.
- Adam's opening 10% conversion language combines a claimed rate with a statement that they are not quite there. Treat achieved versus intended conversion as unresolved.
- Adam predicts a 75% founder audience, while the actual reported live poll is 58% founders. The poll categories total 96%, and the remaining category is not identified.
- Founder-led distribution creates an acknowledged dependence on Adam that could complicate a sale. He accepts that tradeoff for now; Dave proposes broadening visibility to other employees.
- Dave's claim that bootstrapping enables greater disclosure is an opinion about incentives and freedom, not a categorical restriction on venture-backed founders.
- Adam presents founder brand plus a valuable free product as highly durable. Dave qualifies the mechanism by emphasizing relevant traffic, while separately limiting webinar closes to products whose price and sales cycle support immediate purchase.
- Adam initially questions whether TikTok can aggregate a valuable B2B audience. Dave's examples prompt him to acknowledge the possibility, but no Adam TikTok strategy is demonstrated.
- Adam's difficulty writing for Shopify merchants does not establish that the audience was unreachable. Dave attributes it to the content approach, and Adam accepts that continued effort might have worked.
- Dave says the market now requires more deliberate differentiation than simply posting daily, yet still recommends daily publishing to learn. These refer to content quality and experimentation frequency, not mutually exclusive prescriptions.
- Follower counts are defended as potentially useful distribution assets, while unrelated viral content is criticized for weak business impact. Commercial relevance and conversion are the boundary conditions.
- Dave initially reports no automation or DM strategy but later says Sam sends his DMs. The transcript supports the absence of a systematic acquisition program more clearly than the absence of any assistance with messages.
- Dave's polished-versus-spontaneous content examples compare engagement, not business outcomes. They should not override his earlier warning that high views can fail to produce business.
- Adam's additional payment to Kyle is captioned only as 50. Its amount, currency scale and period remain unresolved.
- The 170,000 LinkedIn followers, 5,000 Exit 5 members, creator-video results and Drift career achievements belong to Dave or other third parties, not Adam.
