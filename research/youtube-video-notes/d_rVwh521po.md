## Tensions and corrections

- The title is stronger than the discussion: the guest's objection concerns bootstrappers entering mature, highly competitive categories without a meaningful advantage. He allows for disruption, subcategories, adjacent expansion and venture financing for capital-intensive businesses.
- RB2B's current ARR is not internally consistent: Adam first says $5 million, then discusses growth from $6 million to $12 million. Preserve both rather than silently choosing one.
- The support arithmetic is approximate: 258 requests, 59 escalations and about half of escalations subsequently resolved do not exactly produce the stated 85%. The earlier 70% is a separate baseline.
- The AI sales categories are ambiguous: six clearly incorrect tickets, three white-label errors and four pricing overquotes may overlap. They cannot safely be totaled as independent failures.
- Adam describes letting AI run the business, but Rob handled escalation messages and email-to-phone fallback remained possible. This supports an AI-supported operation, not proven zero-human operation.
- The three-person RB2B and six-person historical Retention.com figures lack contractor/shared-support detail. They should not be converted into fully loaded FTE productivity ratios.
- Adam favors pricing that errs below an excessive quote, but does not describe a tested pricing safeguard or resolution of the AI quoting problem.
- Adam's willingness to accept churn is tied to small customers, low pricing and a deliberately limited business model. His expectation of better conversion from a product redesign remains untested in this source.
- Adam claims investor preference protections usually let investors recover money while founders suffer; the guest disputes preferred-stock prevalence and says investors can lose too. The disagreement remains unresolved, and neither statement should be treated as verified financing guidance.
