## Summary

Jeremy distinguishes list-based marketing from resource-intensive ABM chiefly by investment per account. He recommends defining and validating a market list, proving individual channels, and reserving intensive ABM for situations where a small number of valuable accounts justify the cost. Adam supplies a concrete Retention.com example: an enterprise-retailer deal below seven figures and a shift toward highly targeted events and partnerships. Their most revealing exchange concerns Retention.com's difficulty sustaining agency attention and Jeremy's account of Gorgias building reciprocal lead sharing. In the final third, Adam acknowledges uncertainty about RB2B's product ICP and a mismatch between its content audience and strongest product users. This is primarily go-to-market research, with some SaaS-building and candid operating context; it does not establish a bootstrapping-versus-VC philosophy or an approved brand voice or mission.

## Tensions and corrections

- Jeremy's opening dismissal of ABM is qualified throughout: it can work for high-value contracts, a concentrated remaining market or a company whose entire market consists of major enterprises.
- The repeated market-based readiness test conflicts with treating Jeremy's later $20 million ARR caution as a fixed rule. His $500,000–$1 million guidance concerns list-based foundations, despite a later verbal switch to 'account based'.
- Sales owning target selection does not mean marketing must wait for sales to create the underlying data. Jeremy explicitly assigns research, TAM analysis and recommendations to marketing, with sales validating and choosing.
- Adam suggests Gorgias used seat-based pricing; Jeremy corrects the relevant pricing mechanism to usage-based.
- Adam's speculative 50,000 Gorgias paying-customer figure is not confirmed. Jeremy's 10,000–12,000 figure refers to an earlier program-development stage, so it is not necessarily a same-date contradiction.
- Retention.com's roughly $20 million scale reference is guest-led and briefly affirmed; the local exchange does not clearly label the metric as ARR.
- The live role-poll percentages sum to 103%, and a later attendance caption is garbled. Neither should be silently repaired.
- Jeremy uses 'ACV' with '$500 a month'. Annual and monthly units must remain distinguished.
- Jeremy's customized account report is explicitly an undeployed example. Named enterprise event attendees are not identified as Retention.com customers, and Jeremy's hypothetical event budgets are not actual expenditures.
- Adam says Retention.com attracted agency attention easily after its late-2022 brand surge but could not sustain it. Jeremy's reciprocity explanation prompts Adam to acknowledge an execution gap; it does not establish that Adam adopted or proved the proposed solution.
- Jeremy presents the webinar polls as exemplary ICP research; Adam immediately explains that RB2B's content audience and software power users are misaligned. Content engagement should not be treated as product-market-fit proof.
- The question at 34:00 appears to concern when a company should start list-based marketing, but Jeremy answers about estimating prospects' revenue. The response does not resolve the original threshold question.
- The Shopify Plus agency count is explicitly uncertain and caption-corrupted. Jeremy's claim linking a partner strategy to Klaviyo's IPO is a guest causal assertion without supporting evidence.
- A host, likely Adam, speculates at 46:27–46:38 about HubSpot's profitability and motives for partner charges. This is unverified commentary, not evidence about HubSpot's finances or a bootstrapping-versus-VC position.
- The final promotion describes a forthcoming AI SMS demo-scheduling session as improving conversion and speed, but gives no numerical outcome or evidence of Adam's deployment. It should not become company social proof.
