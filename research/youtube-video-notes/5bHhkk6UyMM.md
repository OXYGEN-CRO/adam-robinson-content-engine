## Summary

Adam and Nathan discuss founder control, personal liquidity, media-driven acquisition, and financing choices. Adam favors building a lean, efficiently acquired business to $10 million ARR before deciding whether to raise, sell, or continue operating. Nathan connects his own disappointing VC experience to his preference for preserving founder equity, and explains how podcasts, proprietary data, paid webinar materials, and audience assets support his businesses. The final third adds important qualifications: recurring revenue can conceal churn, debt requires a proven investment motion, founder secondaries can provide liquidity even on a VC path, and distribution channels lose effectiveness as adoption increases. The discussion is strongly pro-bootstrapping but supplies opinions, anecdotes, illustrative arithmetic, and promotional guest claims rather than a controlled comparison of financing outcomes.
