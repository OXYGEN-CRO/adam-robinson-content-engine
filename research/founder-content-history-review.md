## Alec Paul episode — April 9, 2025

Adam and Pete host Alec. Advice is often collaborative, but individual contributions remain distinguishable from undiarized conversational context. The “99%” title is promotional framing, not a demonstrated study of posts.

- **Adam's concrete writing preparation:** he captures events/ideas through the week, meets Alec for about an hour mostly to develop hooks, and wants roughly ten developed hook/topic choices when sitting down to write three or four posts. The mechanism is an option bank that lets him move past an idea he does not feel ready to write. This is his dated workflow, not a required weekly post count or prescribed Notion setup. [08:31–09:41][A]
- **Earn a new reader's attention with attributable credibility.** Adam explicitly credits Alec's advice to treat every hook as if nobody knows him. He suggests being a broker of information when the writer lacks a large personal result: interview relevant operators and explain what was learned. The example of ten startup interviews is hypothetical; it does not establish that those interviews occurred. His polarizing bootstrap hook is also qualified by his admission that it is not universally true. [11:00–13:46][A]
- **Real activity precedes the writing.** Alec's recommendation is to build, speak with customers, consume useful information and document the experience; he rejects making up stories while sitting at home. He says this need not require disclosing every revenue/churn figure. Adam then describes his year of experimentation and growing understanding of his own words, while acknowledging that even an apparently strong post can miss. [16:00–19:08][A]
- **The company can carry an ongoing story.** Alec describes RB2B as a character with a launch, setbacks and changes; Pete distinguishes a product being the entire story from its being part of one. Adam directly says his monthly updates evolved from dry facts to the emotional experience of being stuck. The latter is reinforced in his March 7, 2026 post and already belongs in the existing guide. [25:40–28:00 and 32:25–33:50][A]
- **Reuse and feedback have limits.** Alec treats content as reusable intellectual property and suggests revisiting a weak post's hook or presentation. He favors quality and sustainable practice over an arbitrary daily quota. His “audience is not wrong” rhetoric, suggested engagement levers, link-preview effects and scheduling explanations are practitioner claims, not established algorithm mechanics. Adam's later uncertainty about results qualifies any deterministic interpretation. [19:08–23:10 and 47:18–50:53][A]
- **Resource requests require delivery and audience judgment.** Alec describes comment-to-receive offers as capable of driving reach and leads, while warning about poor fulfillment, operational volume, brand fit and people outside the ICP. His estimates of fulfillment and reach are unsupported. This is not permission to execute outreach or an endorsement of withholding a promised resource. [37:08–40:03][A]

The opening says Alec helps ideas, hooks, analysis and the calendar, while both men explicitly deny that Alec writes Adam's posts. Adam's account of early camera practice, a 2020 Facebook campaign and first social posting in fall 2022 concerns different activities; it should not become an exact LinkedIn start date or evidence that no earlier filmed marketing existed. [00:56–01:30 and 43:23–45:25][A]

## Adam's business/LinkedIn presentation — March 21, 2025 publication

Adam delivers the main presentation and answers most questions; Pete and Santosh contribute. The source is visibly retrospective: it describes the 2023 sales expansion and, at 16:27–16:41, says a January figure belonged to a February keynote. The exact keynote/recording year is not independently established here. Uploading the video in March 2025 does not make every dashboard figure a March 2025 result.

- **Find the overlap between lived experience, audience and platform.** Adam says content aimed at e-commerce created ecosystem visibility and partnerships without booking the intended merchant demos. Moving toward B2B SaaS founders and revenue people allowed him to describe a specific pain he knew from his own failed sales expansion. His conclusion is about a combination of author, topic, audience and channel, not that e-commerce founders cannot be reached through content. [17:04–19:04 and 32:16–35:49][K]
- **Pillars emerge through tests.** He wanted a predefined creator spreadsheet to tell him what to write. He reports coming to agree with Alec that the topics reveal themselves through publishing and audience response. He still studies others' hooks and templates, but his useful subject matter came from experiences he could articulate as one of the intended readers. This supports an adjustable taxonomy, not a manufactured fixed grid. [33:58–36:42][K]
- **Content can recruit product-learning conversations.** During the build, Adam says he and Santosh spoke with responders while Tate built the product. Soft calls to action and subsequent qualification routed people to calls or a waiting list. Content and buyer feedback then informed the move toward a free offer. His reported 2,000 waitlist people and five-to-seven daily calls are historical self-reports; a waitlist is not payment or PMF. The exact screening threshold and displayed CTA are not recoverable from the spoken transcript. [19:25–22:32][K]
- **The resource advantage matters.** Adam expressly says a large audience makes a free offer more valuable, and another cash-generating business allowed an unusual investment in content and free product distribution. His founder familiarity/outbound argument includes admitting that the older ad-awareness effect could not be measured. The useful lesson is complementary trust and a relevant offer, not additive channel attribution or a universal causal claim. [22:02–22:26, 37:06–40:38 and 44:40–48:58][K]
- **Demand and messaging remain prerequisites.** Adam differentiates learning how to book a demo from acquiring more visitor leads and frames the first million as a search for feedback rather than only a revenue race. His emphatic PMF/word-of-mouth statements coexist with pre-fit customer conversations and content. Pete, separately, advises a small experiment with a defined period and goals rather than promising that a channel works for every business. [29:07–31:33, 42:14–42:45 and 46:23–49:34][K]

The financial and staffing narrative contextualizes the content; it is not new current proof. Retention's ad-led zero-to-one, lean cold-email expansion, failed $50m target, churn and later larger team are different phases. The anti-privacy provocation story is a historical tactic he reports, not a new drafting requirement. Santosh's review-site opinion and Pete's G2/process recommendations remain their statements. [04:29–16:41 and 26:35–29:02][K]

## Dave Gerhardt episode — November 6, 2024

Adam and Santosh question Dave. Drift/Privy/Exit 5 experience, audience results, employee advocacy advice and most platform recommendations belong to Dave. Their presence on Adam's channel does not turn them into Adam's own evidence.

- **Adam identifies a reader need from his earlier frustration.** He says the operational detail he publishes and the community access he is offering are what he wished Drift had supplied when he was starting Get Emails. This is a concrete explanation of writing for an earlier version of himself. His certainty that he would have paid does not independently validate the community's value to others. [00:32–05:25][D]
- **Founder–buyer identity is helpful, not the only possible basis for useful expertise.** Adam asks about the objection that he is his ICP while many founders are not. Dave counters with Privy's founder teaching tactical conversion lessons from years of customer exposure and suggests different channels/angles where relevant. Adam agrees that his SaaS experience made the fit easier and says he might eventually have cracked the Shopify content approach. Dave's claim that the strategy suits almost every business remains his broad opinion. [26:53–31:50][D]
- **Keep a topic both resonant and authentic to the desired identity.** Adam defines pillars as repeated themes that shape how readers understand the author, then says they cannot be selected by the author alone. Dave recommends rough business-relevant topics followed by actual publishing tests. This is a difference in emphasis, not evidence that one supports a rigid taxonomy and the other rejects planning. [36:32–38:25 and 39:52–42:54][D]
- **Study a pattern, then create the author's version.** Adam describes listening repeatedly to Chris Walker, listing recurring topics and opposing positions, and drafting his own possible equivalents. He says these became his pillars. The spreadsheet is referenced and linked in metadata, but was not fetched in this review; the spoken account does not disclose its full contents. Dave adds that repetition follows finding what works and that only some followers see any post. Adam's “greatest hits” paraphrase is explicitly credited to another creator, not presented as an Adam-created framework; that creator's surname is unclear in these captions. [47:31–50:57][D]
- **A founder can begin a broader set of expert voices.** Dave describes willing Drift colleagues publishing because they cared about the company, then marketing helping them with technique. He opposes forcing reluctant staff and later recommends compensating a valuable employee brand. This complements the November 2025 “cast of characters” post, but Dave's employment advice should remain attributed. [13:57–14:53, 20:43–22:19 and 52:42–53:38][D]

Dave favors capturing interested traffic into an email audience and explains why he had not justified an expensive outbound setup for Exit 5's pricing. Adam describes paid LinkedIn help/automation in contrast. These are different business economics and dated practices, not a single approved operating policy. Dave also limits a sales webinar's urgency to suitable low-friction purchases, qualifying Adam/Nathan's broader webinar-sales thesis. [22:30–26:52 and 44:28–47:31][D]

## Pete / John Eley video episode — October 2, 2024

**Adam is absent on paternity leave.** Pete hosts producer John Eley after the planned guest cannot attend; the metadata description confirms Eley's spelling. All interpretations of Adam's motivation, results and practice are third-party reports. John credits a wider producer/editor team rather than claiming sole production. [00:00–03:08][V]

- **Production follows the kind of story.** John distinguishes the documentary's interviewed, followed and edited narrative from lower-lift talking-head observations and live/educational formats. He describes interviews revealing real story material, filming it, editing the narrative, then finding supporting footage. “Authentic” here concerns the underlying experience, not the absence of editing or production. Documentary production can take months; the one-person short-clip workflow cannot be used to estimate that effort. [04:58–06:02, 11:00–12:49 and 17:10–19:58][V]
- **The three video goals are a team proposal.** John names connection, education and inspiration, then explicitly says Adam still needs input. They are proposed purposes for video, not Adam's stated content pillars or approval of a new taxonomy. A later reported “cool, let's do it” message concerns live-event ideas and does not approve this entire strategy. [14:27–15:55 and 39:59–40:20][V]
- **Useful story and audio precede gear.** John recommends an editor before a shooter for a beginner and places story, then sound, then image quality. Pete suggests starting with text while learning topics and using video later if production is overwhelming. This is guest/host advice; claims comparing phone hardware to a film production or promising nearly all results are rhetoric, not tested equivalence. [16:06–17:03 and 19:48–22:07][V]
- **The AI demonstration is Pete's.** Pete describes talking into Gemini, supplying context, and iterating to organize his own material. He contrasts it with Adam's work with producer Christie. Do not use this demonstration as proof that Adam wrote with AI, that AI matched the producer's quality, or that no human editing was needed. [22:09–27:10][V]
- **Distribution and findability are part of the content problem.** They describe conversations being lost in a flowing social feed, fragmented videos and lack of direct contact with the whole follower audience. Their academy/community/replay ideas aim to make useful knowledge accessible over time. Much was prospective; the later note that material had been published does not establish completion of every proposed hub, show or integration. [27:35–35:30 and 40:49–42:32][V]
- **Retain an experimental fraction without promising a formula.** John proposes keeping what works while testing other ideas; his 80/20 split is a heuristic, and “always” improving is unproven. Pete says their current videos mainly served awareness and that more promotional content had coincided with weaker engagement. That historical funnel role does not forbid later signup-oriented videos. Their qualitative audience trust evidence does not calculate documentary ROI. [07:08–10:06, 42:32–45:42 and 47:08–51:59][V]

Case-study, G2, support-automation and follower counts in this episode are company/host reports with limited definitions. The documentary's billion-dollar name is a challenge, not an achieved valuation. No new Adam voice rule or company financial claim is proposed from this absent-author episode.

## Four proposed additions, maximum

1. **`voice/linkedin-voice.md`, stated method:** “In his April 9, 2025 discussion with Alec Paul, Adam described capturing ideas throughout the week and developing a bank of hooks/topics before a writing session. Having several viable choices let him write the ideas he felt ready to develop. His examples of ten options and three or four drafts describe that workflow, not a mandatory cadence.” [08:31–09:41][A] This adds preparation mechanics behind the existing hook rule.

2. **`strategy/learnings.md`, craft and topic selection:** “Treat topic choices as hypotheses. Adam says pillars must fit both audience response and who he wants to be; he found his own recurring positions after studying another creator's pattern and translating it to his own experience. Begin with plausible topics, publish, and refine the recurring themes from the response.” [33:58–36:42][K]; [36:32–38:25, 47:31–48:29][D]. This preserves the user's four wiki categories as proposed organization and avoids manufacturing a fixed content matrix.

3. **`strategy/learnings.md`, content as customer learning:** “Before RB2B's launch, Adam says content recruited conversations while the product was being built; subsequent discussion helped shape the audience and free offer. The useful mechanism is to build relationships with possible buyers alongside the product and stop or revise if the intended demand does not appear. His large audience and existing profitable business were material advantages, and waitlist interest was not payment.” [19:25–22:32, 39:43–40:38][K]; [March 13, 2026][P98]. This adds a concrete prelaunch mechanism to the existing postlaunch audience-feedback example.

4. **`voice/formats.md`, short historical production note:** “In the October 2, 2024 team interview, producer John Eley described deriving documentary stories from Adam's interviews and actual events, then filming, editing and adding supporting footage; informal observations and live teaching used different production effort. This is third-party process history. For the later YouTube workflow, use Adam's October 29, 2025 account with Samu.” [17:10–19:58][V]; [October 29, 2025][P195]. This explains how a produced story can preserve real author material while keeping credits and channel history intact.

These four are concrete proposed insertions, not approved copy. Existing emotional storytelling, credibility, borrowed technique, reusable formats and audience-fit guidance do not need wholesale duplication. No extra personal-value statement or new proof metric is necessary.
