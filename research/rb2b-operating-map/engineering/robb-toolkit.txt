We acquired a 6-year-old SaaS company and found out we need to rebuild the entire software. But instead of a manual audit, we used AI to reverse-engineer the product and create our roadmap. Here's our 3-step process:

For context, the company ticked all our boxes: 

• B2B SaaS at $100k+ ARR
• Sticky customers
• Proven product-market fit 

But the due diligence revealed a massive technical debt that we had to rebuild it from scratch.

A complete rebuild with an existing user base is a founder's nightmare. You risk breaking things and alienating loyal customers.

So before writing a single line of new code, we had to understand the old product inside-out.

Here's the 3-step process we ran:

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟭: 𝗥𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗲-𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗱𝗲 

We fed the entire codebase into an AI assistant (Claude Code) to extract a definitive, multi-page list of every single feature as it existed in the code.
_

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟮: 𝗖𝗮𝗽𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘂𝘀𝗲𝗿 𝗽𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 

We had the customer support team record an end-to-end video, walking through every feature and workflow from a user's point of view.
_

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟯: 𝗦𝘆𝗻𝘁𝗵𝗲𝘀𝗶𝘇𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗼 𝗮 𝗿𝗼𝗮𝗱𝗺𝗮𝗽 

We took the AI-generated feature list, the video walkthrough, and notes from the previous founder and fed it all into ChatGPT 5 Thinking and Gemini 2.5 Pro.

It all saved us an estimated 70% of the manual work involved.

From this process, we established 3 core principles for the rebuild: 

1. Aim for feature parity first

Replicate what exists before adding new features. Fix what’s broken, but don’t redesign the wheel.

2. Keep it simple *aka* don’t move the buttons!

Users hate having to relearn an interface. Keep the design and user experience as consistent as possible to avoid alienating your power users.

3. Future-proof the system

A clean rebuild is a chance to escape technical debt. We restructured the architecture to support compliance, scalability, and integrations that would have been painful (or impossible) to do later.

Have you ever built an entire application from scratch?

Did ChatGPT just kill Zapier at its Dev Day 20205? 🤯

Short answer:
Not yet.

But it just changed the table.

Yesterday, OpenAI shipped an Apps SDK + Agent Kit that turns ChatGPT into a visual, no-code automation canvas at its Dev Day 20205.

You can build agents and chain them, embed them inside apps with ChatKit, Call other services via MCP.

All from a single conversational surface.

That matters because Zapier’s core promise was always simple: connect tools and automate workflows.

Now that promise can live inside the chat where users already spend time.

It drops friction and improves discovery.

And prototypes can be shipped in minutes, not days.

But this is not binary. There are two very different moats at play.

One owns the UI and user attention, the place where people ask questions and discover automation.

The other owns connectors, depth, SLAs, governance, and hard-earned enterprise trust.

OpenAI may win UX and distribution quickly.
 
But Zapier still owns breadth, reliability, and provenance across thousands of apps.

Enterprises won’t move mission-critical workflows overnight without observability, error handling, and legal comfort.

So the real fight isn’t “who dies” but it’s “who doubles down where they’re strongest.”

Currently, OpenAI: makes integrations discoverable, secure, and enterprise-ready.

And, Zapier: become the invisible backend, the reliable plumbing that any UI can call.

If MCP standardizes, the winner will be the one who controls discovery and intent.

And if enterprises demand multi-model freedom and deep connectors, the winner will be the one who controls integrations and trust.

My take:
ChatGPT didn’t kill Zapier yet. It rewrote where automation should live. And Zapier’s survival depends on speed, depth, and adapting to a world where conversations are the new UI.
