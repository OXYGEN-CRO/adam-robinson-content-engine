## Corrections and interpretive limits carried into the playbook

- **Title versus content:** `S3fI3M3ckVE` says “4 Pillars,” but the opening and full discussion at 20:03 identify three: UI/UX, support documents and answer revisions. No fourth pillar was invented.
- **Documentation ownership:** `fZrJqB1xG4w`, 00:04–01:46 describes the operator's own article-editing GPT and refers to “Adam” in the third person. The page credits Robb's operating work rather than attributing the entire documentation system to Adam personally. The public/general and private/product-specific GPTs are distinct.
- **No quality benchmark:** The editing demo says its example was already optimized, so little changed. It supplies no quantitative before/after quality result. The FAQ's “80%” and “confidence” language are heuristics, not evaluation scores.
- **Support rates have different scopes:** The long video's description says 95% across sales, marketing and support, without a denominator. It is not a 95% ticket-resolution observation in this transcript. The later May 22 post says Fin handles 98.9% of all tickets and escalates 1.1%, with no measurement period or dataset supplied. Neither establishes independently verified successful resolution.
- **Daily question arithmetic:** In the full discussion at 19:12–19:31 Adam corrects “60 questions” to roughly 60 people, then estimates three questions each. Preserve the approximate 180 figure as clone conversations/questions, not support tickets or verified daily throughput.
- **Human work persists:** The July discussion includes Adam's daily review, Robb's continuing improvements and human escalations. The later post's “15min/day or less” concerns Robb handling escalated tickets, not every task required to operate the business.
- **UI outcomes are qualitative:** The credits-sidebar and visitor-list examples reportedly eliminated particular questions/confusion. No ticket counts, observation windows or experimental controls are supplied. Splitting person/company views repairs presentation; it does not show a change in identification accuracy.
- **Historical vendor comparison:** The July 2025 Fin/Delphi claims concern that setup. Adam explicitly says he lacks hard data for suspected conversation loss from Delphi's account-creation step. No current product capability or purchase recommendation is inferred.
- **Changelog boundaries:** The July 7 Apollo example explains how dated changes can answer a question; it is not independently verified release history. The source does not justify leaving inaccurate canonical instructions unchanged.
- **Escalation policy is incomplete:** Guidance names when to escalate and demonstrates a support-email loop fix, but does not specify a full decision policy, response SLA or proof that every exception can reach a human. The playbook labels its implementation implications as synthesis.
- **Caption normalization:** “Deli,” “Finn,” “RBDB” and FAQ rendered as “facts” occur in captions. The discussion/metadata support Delphi, Fin, RB2B and FAQ in synthesis. Misrecognized email addresses and promotional links are not reproduced as verified contacts. Raw text remains unchanged.
