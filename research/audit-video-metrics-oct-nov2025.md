## Findings and corrections

### Publication and milestone chronology

The November course starts with RB2B $7m ARR and Retention.com $23m, then explicitly sets a body section in **2024**, with RB2B at $3m after six months and Retention at $22m after four years. Its closing promotes a different video about RB2B at $6m. These are not a single November operating snapshot. January 29, 2026's LinkedIn statement that RB2B just crossed $7m remains in tension with the earlier upload's opening. The captured evidence cannot establish the opening's recording or editing date. Added the conflict without replacing the later milestone announcement or current figures.

The course's detailed first-startup account gives mid-2012 development and January 2014 launch. The AI video at 6:33 instead says his first business started in 2015 without naming it. The detailed chronology remains stronger, with the 2015 aside recorded as unresolved. The course also directly supports March 2024 RB2B public launch after October 2023 manual beta work.

The October 17 video's opening says six weeks after launch to $1m and rhetorically also says idea-to-$1m in six weeks. Its body already describes months of audience-building and discovery. The November course says 16 weeks, five people and zero ad spend after more than 300 discovery calls. These cannot be collapsed into a verified six-week standing-start result. The October video's two mentions of about 300 meetings may refer to the same work; do not add them to claim 600 meetings.

### Email attribution and conversion units

The October email video's opening attributes 32% of over $6m ARR to email, described as $1.9m annualized/$160k monthly. At 2:37, captions explicitly say the three email strategies generated 42% of total revenue **eight months ago**. The companion LinkedIn post says 42% **in eight months**, and its ARR hook is $6.5m. Thus the video supplies a different-period explanation for its own 32/42 figures, but cannot silently reconcile the post. The closing's at-least-20% figure describes **new revenue each day**, another denominator.

The email category includes a LinkedIn-built warm waitlist, genuinely cold prospecting and visitor follow-up. Founder demand and email conversion therefore overlap. The source credits Taylor (surname rendered “Heron” in captions; the LinkedIn post spells Haren) for infrastructure and copy. Neither channel attribution nor implementation should become a claim of Adam's unassisted work.

The 10.4% visitor-follow-up result is **email-to-signup**, not paid conversion. Its stated cold-audience comparison of one signup per 250 sends is 0.4%; their ratio is 26. The opening's 12.7% campaign is not established as the same campaign or period. Positive-reply rate lacks a denominator. These finer campaign details stay in research rather than becoming a broad performance promise.

Sending volume is inconsistent: the video says 160,000 per **month** at 2:48 and per **day** at 9:22; the LinkedIn post says 300,000+ per month. Preserve units rather than choosing the largest number or deriving a reliable daily operating volume.

### Community pricing and the monthly funnel

The LinkedIn video at 17:54 reports 3,000 free signups and 13 paying customers during an early pricing problem, then credits commenters with helping reach a 12.5% touchless conversion rate. Captions truncate “free-to” conversion; the surrounding discussion implies paid conversion, but does not disclose comparable cohorts, dates or a controlled before/after test. Added this useful story with those limits.

The AI walkthrough at 5:10 describes 30,000 unique visitors per month, 2,000 free signups and 250 new paying customers, with about 200 monthly cancellations. The 6.66% rate is traffic-to-free, and 12.5% is free-to-paid. Its separate 1,000 clone-chat users cannot be used as the denominator for 2,000 signups. Nor does the source establish that every signup resulted from a clone chat. Added a compact historical funnel row; do not derive net revenue, cohort churn or a reconstructed $6.22m ARR from a constant funnel assumption.

The same source says Adam does 40 customer calls, but does not clearly give that call count's period. “Wednesday afternoons” does not establish 40 calls every Wednesday. The asserted 15% denominator is also unclear. These call counts were not added as a new central metric.

### AI result versus ordinary operations

The challenge's opening says zero human intervention; the ending at 11:09 reports zero **critical issues requiring** human intervention, steady revenue and high customer satisfaction without measurement detail. Ordinary operations include Adam reviewing clone responses for 5–10 minutes daily and Rob reviewing Fin. This supports an assisted operating system and a self-reported limited challenge, not independently established permanent autonomy or an absence of human work throughout the business.

The video distinguishes 85% of customer interactions, roughly 1,000 monthly prospect questions, roughly 1,000 support tickets, and 75% ticket resolution. It recalls 25% resolution at launch. Those populations cannot be collapsed, or automatically compared with the later 98.9% ticket figure. The claim that AI improved by itself is unsupported by a described evaluation. “Last month” remains relative to an unknown recording date.

### Product scope and third-party attribution

The October product explanation gives 40–45% **US person-level** coverage and 70–80% **combined** coverage including international company data. The October first-million video and November course both advertise 70–80% at person level without that qualifier. The specific explanation supports the central scope boundary; this is not merely a discrepancy that first emerged in 2026. Coverage is not accuracy or a universal guarantee.

The product video's $25m Retention.com figure is expressly **annual revenue**, not ARR. Added that unit distinction. Robly's $10m exit is another direct Adam self-report, but does not independently verify transaction price or resolve the 2020/2021 year conflict.

Sendoso's subscription-relative ROI, First Touch's roughly three-to-five meetings monthly, and ColdIQ's fastest 15-minute meeting are presented as customer examples. They are not Adam-company revenue, independently verified causal ROI, or a representative result distribution. The First Touch introduction rounds to five demos while the quoted passage says three, four or five meetings. Guest attribution is contextual because captions are not diarized; names may be transcription errors.

### Conditions and changes of mind

The ABM video explicitly places its historical execution in 2020–21, credits strong product-market fit, receptive buyers and an experienced sales leader, and says response rates are now lower. At 10:40, Adam keeps manual research but switches from manual sending to sequencers. This is an explicit update of a tactic, not a permanent anti-automation principle. Its six-employee headline also sits alongside five Philippine execution staff; inclusion is unclear. The detailed $3,000 monthly payroll (one at $1,000 plus four at $500) does not establish total acquisition cost, and differs from the opening's “less than” $3,000.

The course's strong anti-VC language coexists with an explicit option to raise after profitable scale, for stronger negotiating power. Its imagined $25m ARR/$10–15m annual distributions path is counterfactual, explicitly where he thinks he could have ended up without overhiring; it is not actual income. The course's public billion-dollar valuation declaration likewise does not establish a financing or sale. These remain arguments or aspirations.

The October LinkedIn video says he excludes personal life and hobbies. April 2026's complete post discusses sobriety, family, Aspen and recreation. This qualifies the earlier rule; it does not authorize new private-life details or medical claims. Changed the interpretation boundary without changing the other identity/voice pages owned by colleagues.
