### 76: controversy, relationship cost and historical financial ambiguity

The title's $23M framing is not a reliable standalone ARR or causal growth claim. The host's money question and Adam's answer around 19:05–19:31 contain garbled numbers. In the same passage the host refers to being in March/April 2024; a question near 57:38 explicitly concerns inbound in 2024. A future July 16 debate is mentioned without a reliable year. These are substantial recording-date qualifications despite the February 2025 publication timestamp. Do not quietly date all “now” figures to publication or resolve the exact recording date from these clues alone.

Campaign periods also vary between weeks 12–43/44, 90 days and three to four months. GetEmails' early 200–250K plateau initially lacks a unit; the host later supplies MRR. The monthly 15–20% churn remark is generalized, without a verified logo/revenue denominator. A claimed roughly 100% monthly growth rate has an unnamed metric. The 100–150 daily signups and 8–10 paid conversions are recording-time self-reports, not September 2026 operating figures. At 40:07–40:22 Adam corrects which incident produced a signup spike: a critic's attack, rather than the 6sense post. No dashboard was inspected.

At 29:40–31:29 Adam describes taking a later post too far. At 32:04–34:38 he explicitly describes the effect of antagonistic online behavior on his relationships, reports Jess and Helen's feedback, and agrees with their criticism. This supports an acknowledged personal cost and receptiveness to feedback, not a clinical diagnosis or a claim that every controversy helped the company. His distinction between criticizing a company and attacking an individual executive at 43:04–44:35 is a personal boundary, not permission to attack anyone else.

At 56:42–56:56 Adam says he wrote a **fake** 6sense emergency-meeting script and Chris Walker talked him out of posting it. A possible newsletter appearance is a future possibility. The script itself was not inspected and is not evidence of a real 6sense meeting. The legal-spend phrase “a few hundred max” has unclear units; legal consultation and his risk tolerance do not establish general legal safety. Likewise, a proposed tripling at a future renewal is not proof of a universal implemented price increase.

The closing 58:26–58:43 engagement teaser repeats the Eric Siu material identified in the preceding batch. This note conservatively leaves the speaker uncertain/possibly Eric; it does not assign those figures to Adam. No correction is required merely to replace that safe uncertainty with the stronger cross-source identification already documented in [[research/audit-video-batch-70-75.md]].
