## Summary

The webinar explains how RB2B combines knowledge-base articles, deterministic self-service workflows, and Intercom's Fin AI agent with human escalation. Adam reports approximately 19,000 users, 700 paying customers, and almost $2.5 million ARR at recording; Robb supplies dated support figures showing low human involvement across the broader support system. The central lessons concern documentation quality, conversation review, modular workflows, testing after product changes, and limits on AI authority. The discussion also covers implementation staffing, resolution-based costs, estimated time savings, future image and voice capabilities, and the distinction between sales and support automation. The title's $2.2 million ARR and one-agent framing require qualification against the spoken numbers, shared human fallback, and an accepted additional hire.

## Tensions and corrections

- The title advertises $2.2 million ARR; Adam's spoken topline is almost $2.5 million ARR. The transcript supplies no reconciliation.
- The one-support-agent headline describes a moment in time and a principal operator. Robb has multiple technical-operations responsibilities, human fallback can involve other team members, and an additional hire has already accepted.
- Approximately 2% human involvement applies to the combined support system. Fin's 56–60% resolution rate uses a narrower denominator, and Robb says workflows resolve most support activity. These are not competing measurements of the same thing.
- The current approximately 2.0%, August 1–11 2.1%, and July 1–August 11 2.8% human-involvement figures have different specificity and periods. They should remain separate rather than being collapsed into one exact rate.
- Robb corrects the Fin comparison end date from August 17 to August 11.
- Improvement is attributed to review, documentation edits, and testing as well as the AI. The transcript does not establish autonomous learning as the cause.
- The team stopped explicitly introducing the agent as AI to reduce immediate human requests. Adam openly questions whether the resulting expectation could frustrate users; no controlled satisfaction comparison resolves this concern.
- Adam initially describes payment following affirmative resolution confirmation. Robb adds that user disappearance after an answer can also be billed as an assumed resolution.
- The $100–$200 AI-cost estimates, later $180 figure, broad support counts, narrower conversation figures, and July time-savings estimate are not presented with sufficiently aligned periods and definitions to reconcile them into a single cost model.
- Robb's intention to add read access to current account data coexists with a deliberate refusal to give the bot billing authority. More informative answers and greater transactional autonomy are distinct choices.
- Robb says withholding discount knowledge prevents that kind of hallucination, but the account does not demonstrate such a guarantee. The stronger concrete boundary is human control of billing and trial extensions.
- The co-host's Air Canada story at 00:29:48–00:30:37 is an unverified third-party anecdote about chatbot discounts and litigation. It should not be recorded as established legal history or Adam's own statement.
- The pricing example's $4.99, $3.99, and '177%' captions are not numerically coherent. The old '495 plan' also lacks a clear unit and billing period. None should be silently repaired.
- The June 6 pricing launch was revised again that day. This is a concrete change of direction, but the short interjection acknowledging 'twice' is not securely attributable to Adam.
- Robb recommends starting promptly and says development experience is unnecessary for the managed setup, while also warning that implementation needs meaningful dedicated effort. Ease of tooling does not mean negligible setup work.
- The Jolie figures are a third-party comparison from Adam, with an uncertain financial metric and undefined employee-count boundaries; they are not RB2B proof.
- The proposed live-data integration, image and voice capabilities, Aspen trip, and advertising-account walkthrough are plans or forecasts, not demonstrated outcomes.
