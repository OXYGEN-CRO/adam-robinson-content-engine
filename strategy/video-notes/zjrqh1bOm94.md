## Tensions and corrections

- Taylor, not Adam, supplies most of the episode's technical strategies and business metrics. His agency revenue, staffing, infrastructure and career history must remain separate from Adam's wiki proof.
- The title presents AI spam filters as established; Taylor initially labels his explanation a theory without proof. Later confident language does not supply the missing verification.
- The early 20% reply and 21.5% positive figures lack consistent denominators. Later references to 1.3% replies and 250 sends per positive cannot be silently harmonized with them.
- Taylor first describes roughly a year since contacting the list, then specifies a late-November-to-May pause of about six months. The original copy may be a year old even though the pause was shorter.
- The May restart, two-week infrastructure failure, two-week rebuild, two weeks of resumed volume and closing reference to last month's failure lack an exact shared chronology. Publication date is not a verified recording date.
- Taylor explicitly corrects the 8.5-million-email client from RB2B to Fixer. Elsewhere the discussion uses 8.2 million and a host rounds to eight million; preserve those variants.
- Fixer's stated 1.37-million-person TAM, monthly sending in excess of eight million and two-year full-market contact cycle do not reconcile as spoken.
- Historical one-per-nine performance is clarified as signups per sent email, although Adam introduces the question using positive-reply language. The current 38-to-1 ratio is not defined equally clearly.
- Taylor says the same copy still works, but later reports recent spam-word changes and an impending set of new variants. The unchanged-copy claim describes the restart control, not every subsequent campaign.
- Taylor doubts the conventional rationale for warm-up but still uses an initial precautionary period and continuous warm-up. He also supplies an Outlook-specific exception.
- The warm-up complaint example mixes total-message and outbound-message denominators. It should remain an unverified explanation rather than a corrected formula.
- Pete explains warm-up through simulated engagement; Taylor emphasizes complaint dilution. These are distinct proposed mechanisms.
- Taylor's lower displayed reply rate on the recording day is attributed to disconnected inboxes, demonstrating that observed metrics can reflect collection failures.
- Taylor generally rejects extensive follow-up sequences but tests a second step for each new client and retains a cybersecurity example where it performs better.
- Pete emphasizes precise pain-point personalization; Taylor limits his alternative approach to high-volume campaigns with broadly applicable products.
- Taylor's offer-first conclusion coexists with his requirement for working deliverability. The episode does not support ignoring infrastructure.
