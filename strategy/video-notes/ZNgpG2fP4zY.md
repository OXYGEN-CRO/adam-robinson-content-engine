## Summary

Richard explains how an executive-assistant agency supplied cash, customer relationships and task-level evidence for building Fyxer. The spoken $5 million milestone belongs to the agency, not an explicitly established SaaS ARR figure. The discussion covers advance-payment terms, enterprise relationship selling, workflow research, resisting customization, credibility-based distribution, founder succession and cross-application automation. Adam contributes his preference for bootstrapping, his unresolved hypothesis that his products should deliver value inside customers' existing systems, and his experiments with live founder pitches and educational content. The final third includes Fyxer's roadmap, an eXp contract, draft-email adoption and important limits on interpreting those numbers.

## Tensions and corrections

- The title can imply $5 million for Fyxer.ai, but the spoken $5 million ARR milestone is explicitly attached to the executive-assistant agency. No clear total SaaS ARR figure is stated.
- Richard initially describes reaching $5 million over six years, then twice says the milestone took four years. Six years may refer to the agency's broader operating history, but this reconciliation is not explicitly made.
- The host's $18 monthly seat-price statement is corrected by Richard to $30 per month.
- Richard explicitly corrects leaving agency operations from 'December last year' to December 2023, not December 2024.
- The transcript's $1.2 million eXp contract figure has no term length. It must not be treated as annual recurring revenue, recognized revenue or cash received.
- The eXp broker population is captioned as 880,000 and may be misheard. It remains uncertain rather than silently changed.
- Richard describes 53% draft send-through as being over halfway toward automating inboxes, but the stated metric only measures use of generated drafts, not the share of all inbox activity automated.
- The proposed 40–50% increase in house viewings is an intended future result, not an achieved customer outcome.
- The roughly 2% software-to-service price comparison lacks enough billing assumptions to reconcile with $60 per hour, 40 minutes per day and $30 per month.
- The pitch poll totals 101%; the demo count moves from six during the segment to seven at the end. Rounding and later interest are possible explanations, not verified corrections.
- Richard's resistance to folder customization coexists with acting on advocates' feedback. His principle is selective feedback interpretation grounded in workflow evidence and behavior, not wholesale refusal to listen.
- Adam criticizes his current interface while arguing that its intended replacement workflow may make interface improvements unnecessary. He explicitly leaves that product hypothesis open to disconfirmation.
- Richard's early agency growth depended on personal relationships and outbound sales, while software distribution included LinkedIn advocates. The discussion does not establish one channel as universally superior.
- Fyxer's four-person software team should not be conflated with the agency's 80-person historical team, nor assumed to include every contractor or shared service.
- The recording says publication will be 'tomorrow', suggesting a January 21, 2025 conversation, but only January 22, 2025 is supplied as a verified publication date. Relative event dates remain contextual.
- Names, domains and product references are inconsistently captioned. 'RBB/RvB', the email platform rendered 'clavo', and the recalled UGC contributors require verification before precise public attribution.
