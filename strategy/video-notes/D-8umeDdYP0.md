## Summary

Adam and Max compare how financing, personal ambitions and definitions of success shape two businesses that began as competitors and became partners. Adam favors small teams, simple products and recurring profit distributions, while acknowledging that personal capital, unpaid founder labor and a difficult early journey supported his path. He also describes an unsuccessful attempt to pursue growth equity in 2022. Max explains Warmly's venture financing, platform expansion and approaching choice between another round and profitability. The final third makes the strategic differences concrete: Adam tracks combined rolling profit and seeks a narrow, inexpensive website identification and outreach workflow; Warmly pursues larger sales-led contracts, integrations and services, increasingly incorporating RB2B data. All findings are internal research context; public reuse is not cleared.

## Tensions and corrections

- The title suggests a winner-versus-loser confrontation, but the spoken conclusion is that RB2B and Warmly increasingly serve different needs and can benefit from partnership.
- Adam's bootstrapping advocacy includes personal capital, family financial participation and years of underpaid founder work. It must not be retold as building with no money or outside resources.
- The three-person RB2B framing excludes acknowledged shared operations and agency support. It is not equivalent to three total people contributing work or a fully autonomous AI company.
- Adam's continued bootstrapping reflects both preference and financing constraints: he sought growth equity in 2022, but retention performance and market conditions worked against the plan.
- Adam endorses limited initial funding aimed at profitability. His position is more specific than rejecting every form of funding.
- The greater-than-$1-million monthly profit claim is not assigned to RB2B alone. The approximately $20 million and nearly $6 million figures are contextually ARR, while the $12–13 million portfolio earnings reference has an unclear captioned metric and period.
- The $10 million or $15 million ARR businesses with three people are ambitions or hypothetical choices; the latest RB2B scale claim in this source is nearly $6 million ARR.
- Max describes Warmly as similar in external scale to RB2B near the end, but gives no clear standalone Warmly ARR number. Do not infer an exact Warmly ARR from that exchange.
- Adam's willingness to tolerate high churn applies while combined rolling profit rises. He still wants to extend RB2B customer duration, and poor retention previously blocked his growth-equity ambitions.
- Warmly's hoped-for 3× annual growth, potential Series B, $30–40 million ARR ambition and January decision are prospective. They are not subsequent outcomes established by publication.
