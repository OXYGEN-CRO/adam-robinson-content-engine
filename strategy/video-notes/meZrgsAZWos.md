## Summary

Adam and Nathan discuss premium domains, the changing usefulness of public financial reporting, early SaaS churn, executive hiring, niche positioning, direct sales, referrals and founder content. Adam reports paying $800,000 for retention.com, questions whether his monthly updates still serve their purpose, and recounts a rushed leadership expansion after reaching $13 million ARR with six people. Nathan explains why Kit retired its public dashboard, how professional-blogger niches enabled early growth, and how affiliate distribution followed labor-intensive direct sales. The final third examines balancing company and founder brands, attracting established creators, evaluating content through valuable relationships and accounts, and batching production. Kit.com's purchase price is never disclosed.
