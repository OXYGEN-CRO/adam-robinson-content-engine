## Summary

Adam retrospectively examines two episodes in which provocative marketing generated attention and customer acquisition: early GetEmails Facebook advertisements made with Helen, and RB2B's public dispute with 6sense. He describes using hostile comments as creative material, evaluating controversy through signups and sales, and turning an incumbent's legal demand into publicity. He also explains why the Facebook campaign stopped working, how later posts became repetitive or mean-spirited, and how sustained antagonism harmed his relationships and emotional state. The final third adds boundaries around attacking individuals, legal downside, relying on negativity as a core strategy, and the changing economics of outbound versus inbound demand generation. The spoken evidence does not establish that controversy alone created a $23 million business, and several financial captions are unusable. Although published in February 2025, the conversation contains strong indications of a 2024 recording; its exact recording date is unknown.

## Tensions and corrections

- The publication date is February 22, 2025, but the discussion references 2024 as the present, describes a March/April 2024 episode and promotes a future July 16 event. Treat operational metrics as latest-at-recording, with the recording date unknown.
- The title's $23 million business claim is not substantiated as a precise Adam-confirmed financial measure. The host supplies $23 million without a clear unit; Adam's apparent response or correction is numerically garbled.
- The early ad-campaign duration is inconsistent: approximately week 12 through week 43 or 44, approximately 90 days, and three or four months are all stated.
- The account does not support a simple claim that social-media hostility alone built the full business. Adam also identifies ecommerce prospecting, a later outbound sales playbook, customer lifetime value and product quality as consequential.
- One-day ad payback coexists with a description of occasional $5,000 buyers covering two weeks of spend. Without dates, spend figures or accounting definitions, these should remain distinct recollections rather than one consolidated efficiency metric.
- Early claims that an undeveloped brand cannot be destroyed are later narrowed by warnings about persistent negativity, personal attacks, relationship harm and potentially business-ending litigation.
- Adam initially frames a visible signup spike as possibly connected to a large LinkedIn post, then identifies that particular spike at 00:40:07–00:40:22 as coming from the critic's attack. It should not be counted independently as verified 6sense-driven growth.
- The supposed 6sense emergency-meeting transcript is explicitly identified at 00:56:42–00:56:56 as a fake script Adam wrote. It is not inside information or evidence of actual executive discussions.
- Adam admits that a later 6sense post went too far: editing removed humor, a harsh closing risked making him look cruel, and attaching his own P&L confused the subject.
