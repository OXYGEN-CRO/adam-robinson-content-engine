# SaaS building

## Promise and boundary

Help founders find demand, build a simple product that earns revenue and keep operations manageable. This is the product/economics side of the freedom argument. A decision's emotional story belongs in building in public; its reusable mechanism belongs here. This fourth category is user-proposed, overlapping Adam's own three-pillar framework. [P46] [P65] [P98] [P129]

## Current thesis

MoltSets tests building for agents through data APIs. It evolved from an April test site to May price hypotheses, June's cheaper unlimited offer, delayed quality work and August beta. The constant is a narrow agent use case plus distribution/shared data economics. Prices, promised quality and launch dates changed; old screenshots are not current product documentation. [P85] [P67] [P57] [P34] [P37] [P20] [P18]

He frames simplicity as the prerequisite for AI customer interaction. Robb's documentation/escalation matter more than agent counts. Personal skepticism toward AI strategic advice coexists with company automation. [P56] [P64] [P90] [P91]

## Qualifications that affect the lesson

The recovered January28 continuation adds a manual-MVP example: RB2B emailed spreadsheets for five months before a website, and Adam uses a four-to-six-week core-build heuristic. He looks for weekly referrals, customer disappointment if the product disappears, and organic growth as PMF signals. December27's continuation lists opportunities in narrow suite alternatives, specialized verticals, manual-heavy software and accessible buyers. These are dated methods, not universal thresholds. [C7422337090329436162] [C7422337259011559425] [C7410750436858617856]

- “One person running RB2B” describes an operating allocation at a point in time. Founders, shared staff/data, contractors and content still matter. Dec 31 explicitly credits the 33-person organization. [P10] [P46] [P65] [P154]
- September 4 says RB2B is **about to** cross $9.6m ARR. Earlier predictions of $10m do not prove completion. [P2] [P61]
- July 31 proposes churn causes without settling one. The $79 cohort and OEM cannibalization remain hypotheses. [P23]
- He says churn matters after $5m [P147], yet diagnoses a $2.5–3m PMF problem through churn [P155]. Preserve context-dependent heuristics, not a universal threshold.
- “100% valid” data promises conflict with later poor-quality/delayed-rollout accounts. Assess quality through current evidence, not promotional confidence. [P45] [P47] [P37] [P20]

## Diagnose the stage before prescribing the move

The [January 2025 post](../../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2025-01-07-7282507888328167424.md) emphasizes manual customer discovery, clear positioning and a narrow initial use case. The [May 2025 post](../../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2025-05-31-7334641032967454724.md) addresses the later plateau: differentiation, messaging, customer focus, economics, word-of-mouth and retention. Their ARR bands define the situations Adam discusses; they are not universal stage gates.

For a product/operations brief, identify which constraint the evidence actually supports. The [February 2024 Retention update](../../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2024-02-06-7160748181696188416.md) shows that more focused selling can improve outcomes while the category's retention problem remains. More distribution, a narrower ICP and a better product are related interventions, not interchangeable explanations.
