# Financing decisions

## Current direction

Adam's latest inspected position is conditional: bootstrap when the founder can. His [September 4, 2026 post][L-Sep4] prioritizes recurring profit, control and freedom; his [August 14 post][L-Aug14] preserves a capital-intensive-business exception. These are his preferences and experience, not measured probabilities of financial success. See [[identity/proof]] for current operating figures and their definitions.

This map connects those newer statements to three fully read 2024 discussions and selected checked passages from August 2025. **Adam is absent from the September 25, 2024 Amit Vasudev session.** Hosting a guest on his channel does not establish adoption of that guest's position. [Source at 1:00][Amit-absence]

## What the history adds

In September 2024, Adam explains that much of his public writing speaks to his earlier self and advocates for early-stage founders. He distinguishes a founder's single long commitment from an investor's portfolio, and cash distributions from paper valuation requiring a transaction. He also admits that the early, lean phase of company-building suits him better than managing a larger organization. Those are personal priorities, not evidence that every small company wins. [Debate, 8:00–12:59, 34:00–38:59 and 52:00–54:59][Mark]

The October conversation adds an operating mechanism: Adam is attracted to media that earns trust and helps cover acquisition costs. He endorses Nathan Latka's approach and says he is experimenting with one-to-many selling. That supports his pursuit of efficient distribution; it does not establish literal zero fully loaded CAC for RB2B. He also confirms trying Founderpath's analytics. No loan, secondary sale or financing completion is reported. [October 2024, 15:00–23:59 and 42:00–50:59][Latka]

By August 2025, Adam articulates an objective across **two businesses**: increase six-month rolling average profit while enjoying the team and improving products. His willingness to tolerate one product's decline is conditional on combined profit rising; he still wants longer RB2B customer life. This is portfolio-level prioritization, not indifference to retention or RB2B-only profit. [53:27–54:47][Warmly-profit]

## Guest comparisons, separately attributed

**Mark Kosoglow, September 2024:** family obligations and insufficient personal runway made the venture-backed path attractive for him. He distinguishes building a successful company from realizing personal wealth, and agrees that a good smaller business need not pursue a unicorn valuation. Adam accepts the personal-context qualification. Mark's Outreach/Catalyst stories and results remain Mark's. [5:00–15:59 and 35:00–39:59][Mark]

**Amit Vasudev, September 2024, hosted by Santosh:** describes Clearbit seeking profitability even after early funding; later, its episodic data value made a conventional recurring-software growth model difficult. He stresses buyer behavior, strategic acquisition uncertainty and contractor support behind lean staffing. He also defends a role for venture capital while questioning its fit for many software businesses. These are guest lessons, not Adam's decisions or current Clearbit facts. [7:00–17:59, 21:00–32:59 and 38:00–46:59][Amit]

**Nathan Latka, October 2024:** recounts investor control blocking an early sale he wanted, then describes Founderpath's non-dilutive funding business. His debt criterion is an already working economic engine, with an approximate $500k ARR floor discussed in that historical session. This is a lender-founder viewpoint, not Adam's borrowing decision, current product eligibility or personalized financing advice. [3:00–7:59 and 49:00–50:59][Latka]
