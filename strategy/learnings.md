# Learning map

## Scope

A cross-source map of recurring lessons from all 200 selected LinkedIn posts and 100 full-transcript video research notes, with focused playbooks linked below. The captured posts span October 20, 2025–September 7, 2026; the selected videos span August 14, 2024–April 16, 2026. Detailed teaching from individual sessions stays in [[strategy/youtube-library]], which reports the current extraction coverage. This page selects reusable principles and their conditions; it does not repeat every video note. The cited passages were checked directly. Adam's reported experiences and opinions are distinguished from guest examples and proposed interpretations. Current metrics live in [[identity/proof]].

The four headings follow the user's proposed organization. Adam's own course-era taxonomy has three parts: building in public, the future of GTM, and contrarian takes such as bootstrapping versus VC. SaaS building is a useful additional wiki category, not his fourth declared pillar. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/r47Q4Cfit5s/r47Q4Cfit5s|December 31 video, 08:45–09:07]]

## Building in public

- **Describe the problem before the outcome is certain.** Adam connects candid Retention layoffs/churn disclosures to trust among founders experiencing similar problems. His July MoltSets post continues that practice with delayed shipping, poor data results and premature creator promotion. The durable lesson is visible learning under uncertainty; he also acknowledges that employees, cofounders and investors can experience disclosure differently. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/eUzqVJpZPP4/eUzqVJpZPP4|April 16, 01:16–02:42]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-07-06-7479937963578130433|July 6: MoltSets failures]]

  Reporting formats can evolve with their purpose. In May 2025 Adam credits Nathan Barry's dashboard as inspiration, then questions whether his own monthly format still serves readers while explicitly wanting to continue building in public. Nathan's separate explanation for retiring Kit's dashboard concerns calculation accuracy and revenue scope; it is not Adam's reason or proof he stopped reporting. His later emotional monthly updates remain evidence of the continued practice. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/meZrgsAZWos/meZrgsAZWos|May 20, 2025 release, 06:32–10:23]]

- **Use public discussion to gather useful hypotheses.** Later retellings credit commenters with helping RB2B reprice after weak free-to-paid conversion. An April 23, 2025 conversation gives a narrower account: Adam says the early discussion supplied substantial insight without, in his view, changing the intended decision, and that some subsequent posts presented him as more undecided than he was. Preserve that tension rather than claiming all decisions emerged from an open question. Audience feedback can inform research, but it does not prove representative demand or a single cause of growth. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/4Wyy1YiMWwo/4Wyy1YiMWwo|February 20, 2026, 10:19–10:54]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/bMm9bYTTeJs/bMm9bYTTeJs|December 3, 2025, 31:08–31:30]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/M0VvUsyJQ-M/M0VvUsyJQ-M|April 23, 2025 publication, 46:23–47:27]]

- **Improve the craft without claiming an algorithm formula.** His April process researches audience questions, establishes credibility, develops a precise pain into a takeaway, and ends with emotional resonance. His August admission that he cannot explain individual post results and September emphasis on continuing to publish qualify that process: consistency is a practice, not a reach guarantee. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/eUzqVJpZPP4/eUzqVJpZPP4|April 16, 02:43–06:50]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-08-27-7498815008437460992|August 27]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-09-03-7501296198624907265|September 3]]

- **Refine topics through publishing and real experience.** Adam says pillars must fit both audience response and who he wants to be. He describes studying Chris Walker's repeated themes, then developing his own equivalents, and learning that a fixed creator spreadsheet could not choose his subject matter in advance. The interpretation is to start with plausible topics and refine them through actual work and response; the user's four wiki categories remain an adjustable organization. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/H4LpPMR6v1E/H4LpPMR6v1E|November 6, 2024, 36:32–38:25 and 47:31–48:29]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/EqXag_z2OpQ/EqXag_z2OpQ|March 21, 2025 publication, 33:58–36:42]]

## Bootstrapping versus VC

- **Design for profit and the ability to choose.** Adam values surviving weak periods, declining unattractive deals and funding the next experiment from an operating business. His goal-letter account connects company size to independence and a better daily life. This is his preferred founder outcome, not evidence that every bootstrapped company will compound successfully. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/Xovf7vlPfOU/Xovf7vlPfOU|February 6, 01:58–03:14]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/AdFLL-bFEFI/AdFLL-bFEFI|January 14, 02:16–04:03]]

- **Preserve options when choosing capital.** His September advice is to defer fundraising and first solve product and distribution; his May Skio discussion allows raising when necessary while arguing that a smaller capital base can preserve desirable exits. The Skio results and Ryan Allis quotation are attributed comparisons, not Adam's financial achievements or a universal financing rule. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-09-01-7500601621890879488|September 1]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-05-13-7460353919353880576|May 13]]

- **Treat life design as part of company design.** The 2016 letter sought freedom, health, relationships and control over daily work; the March video favors a pace sustainable for decades. His newer August calendar retains concentrated meeting/content days and flexible experiment time. The interpretation is to protect energy and useful attention; neither the historical schedule nor family details become a prescribed routine or cleared public story. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/AdFLL-bFEFI/AdFLL-bFEFI|January 14, 03:00–08:28]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/y4R4zyOv2FE/y4R4zyOv2FE|March 6, 08:05–09:21]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-08-20-7496214966937530368|August 20]]

## Go-to-market

- **Connect attention to a complete acquisition system.** Adam's "magic triangle" combines useful founder content, relevant outreach and retargeting. Content builds familiarity; follow-up acts on existing interest; retargeting reaches people who were not ready. He reserves scaling this system for evidence of product-market fit, while still advocating discovery conversations and content before that point. Channel attribution claims can overlap and should not be added together. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/r47Q4Cfit5s/r47Q4Cfit5s|December 31, 06:00–09:57]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/sr9fzxRW0bA/sr9fzxRW0bA|November 19, 12:18–14:17]]

- **Give creators a reason to succeed with the product.** In Adam's account of Clay, agencies demonstrate workflows because that attracts clients; Clay supports them with ideas, visibility and implementation opportunities. His August MoltSets plan similarly wants a creator program that helps agencies, but explicitly puts a stable product first. Clay's history is a third-party example; MoltSets' program is a plan, not a measured repeat of Clay's results. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/_Rw7AazkHmg/_Rw7AazkHmg|December 11, 03:47–06:20]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-08-25-7498049303853191168|August 25]]

  A concrete guest example: Maor says Base44 removed the request to praise the platform when users shared their creations, emphasizing work they were proud of instead. Building credits still incentivized sharing; this account supplies a design choice, not a quantified conversion effect. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/1f19uvtzNds/1f19uvtzNds|July 20, 2025 publication, 50:24–51:08]]

- **Define the target accounts before acting on intent.** In the historical signal-stacking webinar, Adam calls it a product-education mistake to teach visitor outreach without first establishing the addressable accounts. He proposes matching signals to named accounts and their owners in the CRM, then triggering a suitable workflow. He explicitly describes this implementation as a hypothesis. It qualifies the acquisition-system lesson: a visible visit or enthusiastic free signup does not establish buyer fit or a useful next action. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/Y3xKljQlMzA/Y3xKljQlMzA|09:31–14:26]]

- **Retire tactics whose original conditions disappeared.** His historical ABM account requires product-market fit, receptive Shopify buyers and capable senior staff. He explicitly changes from manual sending to sequencers while retaining researched lists. His January "cold email is dead" video still recommends relevant prospecting and simple copy. These are conditional, changing playbooks; his broader advice is to learn patterns from other founders rather than copy their prescriptions. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/YNaPeDu4xo8/YNaPeDu4xo8|November 26, 09:50–11:41]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/P7dNHG1Xu6E/P7dNHG1Xu6E|January 21, 04:32–05:14]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/Xovf7vlPfOU/Xovf7vlPfOU|February 6, 07:48–08:33]]

  Revisit a segment when the conditions change. In the December 2024 Diana Ross conversation, Adam describes returning to publishers with a different offer and stronger brand credibility; Diana adds that buyer urgency had changed. Their account supports reassessing a previously weak segment, without proving that every larger buyer is easier to sell or less likely to churn. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/xwidBsviGII/xwidBsviGII|46:15–48:40]]

## SaaS building

- **Discover willingness to pay, then deliver the smallest useful experience.** Adam favors payment over polite interest, manual delivery before unnecessary automation, and observing what actually excites users. RB2B's early spreadsheet attracted little payment; live Slack notifications produced a stronger reaction. That reaction was useful product evidence, not itself proof of paid conversion. His March LinkedIn shorthand keeps customer conversations, product improvement and content as the pre-fit work. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/4Wyy1YiMWwo/4Wyy1YiMWwo|February 20, 06:20–09:36]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-03-13-7438221080412000256|March 13]]

  The historical prelaunch account makes the customer-learning mechanism concrete: content recruited conversations while Tate built the product, and the audience and free offer developed together. Adam explicitly says his existing audience made that free offer more useful. This supports building relationships with possible buyers alongside the product and reconsidering an idea when demand does not emerge; the waitlist itself was not payment or proof of fit. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/EqXag_z2OpQ/EqXag_z2OpQ|March 21, 2025 publication, 19:25–22:32]]

  The practice was uneven. In the Eric Siu conversation published February 28, 2025, Adam says he and Santos had stopped doing customer discovery for roughly nine months and connects that lapse to RB2B's plateau and churn. That is his retrospective explanation, not an isolated causal test. It adds a concrete reason to continue listening after launch and qualifies any impression that he always followed his own discovery advice. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/89Jl-njDguM/89Jl-njDguM|26:38–27:04]]

- **A narrow profitable product can be the deliberate destination.** His March reversal abandons widening RB2B into an outreach suite; July accepts a possible ceiling and favors another adjacent business. Simplicity reduces support and coordination demands. The boundary is unusually favorable existing audience/data infrastructure, which he explicitly says others cannot simply copy. April's AI discussion adds a caution: easy-to-copy features are a weaker advantage, and agent revenue can carry material variable costs. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-03-19-7440454271109836800|March 19]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-07-31-7488990110525353984|July 31]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-05-22-7463630759023529985|May 22]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/kS9ii5vYbOM/kS9ii5vYbOM|April 9, 02:33–03:55]]

- **Automate repetitive interaction while maintaining knowledge and human learning.** The February funnel walkthrough credits FAQs, troubleshooting, changelogs and daily corrections for better AI support. It preserves direct customer calls as learning that Adam does not want to automate. May's newer account still has Robb handling escalated tickets. "AI runs it" therefore includes maintained documentation, human exceptions and shared staff; it does not mean every customer interaction or all company work is autonomous. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/PCClwboo9ls/PCClwboo9ls|February 13, 07:48–09:46]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-05-22-7463630759023529985|May 22]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-08-25-7498049303853191168|August 25 staffing and support scope]]

- **Hire for demonstrated need and improve management as the team grows.** The retrospective hiring surge is a warning against treating exceptional early sales as proof of a much larger repeatable market. His April management account emphasizes clear expectations learned through coaching, stable quarterly priorities, strong compensation, recognition and real human connection. A small team still needs management; a larger team cannot follow every new founder impulse. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/_v-cZJc001s/_v-cZJc001s|January 28, 00:43–01:49]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-04-11-7448790811904868352|April 11]]; [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-09-01-7500601621890879488|September 1]]
