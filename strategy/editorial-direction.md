# Editorial direction

## Working brief

Help software founders make a better next decision when demand, growth or the operating model feels stuck. Use Adam's real operating experience, including corrections and emotional stakes, to teach something concrete. GTM practitioners are a second reader group when a piece explains an implementable workflow. This is a proposed editorial brief grounded in the archive, not an approved posting calendar or sales campaign.

[[audience/ideal-follower]] defines the reader. [[identity/positioning]] defines the promise. [[identity/proof]] governs metrics. [[voice/linkedin-voice]] and [[voice/formats]] govern the writing evidence.

## From an angle to a brief

Record one reader stage, one question, one pillar, one funnel job and the source passages that support the argument. State the observation, Adam's explanation and remaining uncertainty separately. Choose a format because it fits the argument: a confession for an emotional contradiction, a numbered explanation for a method, or a dated comparison for a changed decision.

For a commercial next step, define the actual use case and current destination. A founder interested in life design need not be an RB2B buyer. A workshop attendee need not be a MoltSets customer. Historical event links and prices should not become new offers by default.

## What a finished draft should accomplish

- The intended reader can recognize why the topic matters to their current situation.
- One source-backed event, method or distinction carries the argument.
- Numbers name the company, measure and period; targets and guest results remain identified.
- The reader leaves with a decision or useful question, not only a motivational slogan.
- Any invitation matches the reader's need and a real available destination.

No numeric growth target, posting time, channel allocation or outreach sequence is set by this document. Those are campaign decisions, not facts recoverable from old posts.
