# LinkedIn voice

## Evidence and attribution

This guide reflects all 200 selected LinkedIn posts, dated 2025-10-20 through 2026-09-07. It is an observed writing guide, not author approval. Profile authorship does not prove an unassisted writing process. Guest stories and metrics remain attributed to the guest; P 61 explicitly republishes Robb Clarke's words.

On December 1 Adam said he no longer used a ghostwriter and credited Alec Paul as coach/strategist/producer. On November 3 he credited original ghostwriter Tommy Clark for frameworks and the first 20,000 followers. His YouTube workflow uses Samu Kovács's scripts followed by Adam's light edits and recording. Keep channel and time distinct. [P171] [P192] [P195]

## Historical samples added to calibration

The expanded archive adds selected complete posts from 2023–2025 to the original recent sample; this is a focused qualitative extension, not a new statistical analysis of all 911 available bodies. [[research/context-expansion-review]] records the reviewed sources.

- **Useful contradiction:** the [May 2025 stalled-founder post](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2025-05-31-7334641032967454724.md) gives strong advice while admitting Adam himself is stuck. Preserve this tension instead of making the narrator all-knowing.
- **A story earns its audience promise:** the [November 2023 origin story](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2023-11-11-7129220348616593409.md) moves from desperation through discovery to why he writes for other founders. The emotional close follows the story; it is not a motivational line pasted onto unrelated advice.
- **Specificity makes a list useful:** the [January 2025 post](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2025-01-07-7282507888328167424.md) names recognizable mistakes and requests rather than abstract traits. Preserve the concrete problem while avoiding unsupported prevalence claims such as its “99%” framing.
- **Conviction can coexist with respect:** the [July 2025 CFO story](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2025-07-19-7352407363783438336.md) begins with an uncomfortable accusation and ends by respecting the colleague's challenge. Do not turn every disagreement into a simplistic villain story.

His [March 2024 audience strategy](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2024-03-28-7179219737141231617.md) explicitly values a strong hook, lines that sustain attention and an emotionally satisfying ending. That supports craft, not fake urgency, manufactured dialogue or guaranteed distribution.

## Core voice

The characteristic combination is a specific business fact, exposed emotion, blunt lesson and playful ending. He writes as a founder still inside the problem. Confidence and anxiety coexist: a successful business can produce a post about fear, churn, FOMO or failed expectations. A polished, omniscient management essay would lose that tension. [P2] [P23] [P37] [P142] [P185]

He addresses founders directly, often writing for a younger or stuck version of himself. Freedom, profit, simplicity and persistent learning recur. He attacks the financing model forcefully while showing warmth toward individual founders and respect for excellent operators. [P17] [P46] [P81] [P116] [P161]

## Vocabulary and rhythm

- Short paragraphs, standalone beats, numbered lists and dialogue. Some posts are a single sentence, others long lessons; no fixed length is supported. [P5] [P12] [P47] [P82] [P131]
- Concrete startup language: ARR, MRR, profit, churn, FTE, TAM, PMF, offer, demo and founder brand. A price, tradeoff or consequence usually explains the concept. Check [[identity/proof]] before using numbers. [P10] [P23] [P65] [P112]
- Selective ALL CAPS, repeated question/exclamation marks, ellipses, parentheses, arrows and occasional emoji. Numbering varies. Source spelling and punctuation irregularities exist; they are not compulsory affectations. [P31] [P39] [P63] [P144]
- Profanity expresses disbelief, frustration or conviction, but is not universal. Do not set a profanity quota. [P7] [P17] [P25] [P37]
- Boldness followed by self-deflation or qualification: the milestone is not quite reached, the idea may fail, the envy is his problem too. Preserve the caveat beside the claim. [P3] [P15] [P53] [P176]
- Recurring expressions include “Keep building,” “If you can bootstrap, bootstrap,” and “$10m ARR is the FU Money of SaaS.” These are observed phrases, not mandatory signatures. [P2] [P77] [P152]

## Hooks, arguments and endings

| Pattern | What earns attention | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Big result with surprising constraint | Revenue with unusually small staff, tiny cost or reduced effort | Aug 20 calendar [P14]; May 22 $9m milestone [P65] |
| Painful confession | Something apparently incompatible with success | July 31 churn [P23]; July 6 broken APIs [P37] |
| Dialogue | A founder/customer says something requiring an answer | June 23 beta customer [P47]; May 24 AI agents [P64] |
| Strong belief | A recognizable worldview with stakes for the reader | Apr 14 freedom [P82]; Feb 13 audience boundary [P116] |
| Named expert and useful result | A reason to learn from the guest | Aug 31 Michel's stack [P6]; Oct 25 Alina's events [P197] |
| Public challenge | A compelling uncertain outcome | Sep 7 content challenge [P1]; Apr 2 autonomy [P86] |

Do not invent conversations to fill a format or turn another person's result into Adam's credibility. A narrated reconstruction is not a verbatim transcript.

The common long-post sequence is hook → background/“to set the stage” → story/list/dialogue → lesson → close. `TAKEAWAY` is frequent. A specific decision, customer reaction or mismatch between numbers and feeling supplies the turn. [P10] [P37] [P59] [P101] [P112]

Educational endings often use encouragement, a joke or an earned question. Build-in-public asks invite criticism, grades or predictions. Commercial posts use event/signup/resource links or a comment/DM request, sometimes after `P. S.`. These are observed conversion patterns, not permission to publish or send messages. [P18] [P63] [P103] [P142] [P188]

He recommends coy endings yet frequently asks “Did I miss anything?” in practice. Preserve the variety rather than making either pattern an absolute rule. [P101] [P119] [P163]

## Adam's stated writing method

In the April 9, 2025 discussion with Alec Paul, Adam describes collecting ideas throughout the week and developing hooks/topics before sitting down to write. A bank of viable options lets him move to the idea he feels ready to develop. His example of ten options for three or four drafts describes that historical workflow, not a mandatory cadence or tool choice. [Source: 08:31–09:41](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/yMNyVxr4IAo/yMNyVxr4IAo.md).

His March 7 lesson explicitly prioritizes story, hook, credibility, pattern-matching strong creators, a takeaway, emotional payoff and timely narratives. Monthly reporting should convey his emotional journey, not just the changelog. August 27 simultaneously admits he cannot predict format performance despite years of posting. This is a writing method, not an algorithm guarantee. [P101] [P8]

In [How I Write LinkedIn Posts That Get 100,000+ Views](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/eUzqVJpZPP4/eUzqVJpZPP4.md), published April 16,2026, his stated guidance is:

- **00:00:29–00:02:42:** answer real audience needs, disclose hard truths, and find a polarizing position the intended reader recognizes.
- **00:02:43–00:04:02:** collect questions from recent strong creators, search and Reddit; create a swipe file. He names Chris Walker for hooks, Nigel Thomas for templates and his own posts for closes. Borrow technique, not their stories.
- **00:04:02–00:05:02:** the pre-“see more” space earns continuation; use credible, specific numbers.
- **00:05:03–00:06:00:** specific pain → solution/reframe → actionable takeaway. Lists and paragraphs both work.
- **00:06:01–00:06:50:** a coy close should create an emotional payoff. He suggests asking Claude for options and selecting by taste.

The final point differs from posts saying he does not use AI for writing. Keep it as dated guidance, not proof of day-to-day practice. Company AI operations, personal strategic judgment and authentic audience interaction are different subjects in his writing. Captions are automatic and contain recognition errors. [P25] [P27] [P90] [P91] [P97]

## Proposed drafting rules

Use one real tension and one central lesson. Let earned specificity carry the hook. Retain uncertainty rather than upgrading forecasts into achievements. Match emphasis and humor to the occasion. Repeated/reworked posts show enduring themes, not multiple independent confirmations of a number. No author edits, rejected drafts, fixed cadence or current CTA rules have been supplied.
