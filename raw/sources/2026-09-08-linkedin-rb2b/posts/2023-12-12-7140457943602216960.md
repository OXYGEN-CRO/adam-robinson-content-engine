## Exact post text

In the last 12 months, I bootstrapped my SaaS startup from $13m-$22m ARR. Here are the 6 biggest mistakes I made as CEO along the way (and the months I made them):


1. I got major FOMO

Dec 2022 - May 2023

I felt like if we didn’t go as fast as possible we were going to miss the opportunity. This led to panicked hiring and a horrible corporate spending culture as we tried to “be everywhere”.


2. We stopped acting like bootstrappers

Dec 2022 - May 2023

Related to FOMO, we went from making decisions like a capital efficient, lean-and-mean bootstrapped startup to a VC-backed unicorn whose only goal was to grow as quickly as possible. Not anymore. I know the type of CEO I want to be.


3. I didn’t insist on focusing on churn as the #1 priority

April 2023 - June 2023

Many of us hit a growth wall in April. Our management team convinced me that fixing our pipeline problem was the most important thing to do, despite very alarming indicators that foreshadowed vicious contraction and cancellations. We paid the piper and now reducing churn is our #1 priority.


4. We didn’t properly define our ICP

Dec 2022 - Aug 2023

We found that the best way to eliminate churn is to not onboard a customer who we know will likely churn in the first place! For us, a prospect below a certain size doesn’t work, and there are some complex tech stacks that require even more scale to have a high likelihood of success.


5. We oversold people and didn’t realize it for 5 months

Dec 2022 - June 2023

This was my fault for not seeing it coming. The consequences were horrible. It created a euphoria about the opportunity that led to over-hiring and overspending, then on the back-end led to a massive wave of dissatisfaction, contraction, and opt-outs at 60 days. We're doing the opposite now.


6. We scaled up a sales org before we had a repeatable sales model

Dec 2022 - April 2023

A combination of #5 and #6 led us to think we had a sales model, so we kept adding bodies, while seeing less results from the team. Bad signal was coming in, leading us to think that we could scale headcount when we weren’t ready, and it led to our VP sales resigning and a painful shrinking of our sales team. This was my biggest mistake.


TAKEAWAY:

Growing a startup in 2023 was hard.

I publicly set out to hit $50M ARR and fell way short.

Mistakes are inevitable, especially when you are going big.

But when I reflect on this year and I'm honest with myself...

I have to say I'm SO glad 2023 is over.

That may sound weird as we ended up growing 70%.

But the pain and suffering was immense.

Of course, I'm not complaining.

I wouldn’t trade my life, team or journey for anything.

But it's important to be honest about the process.

The path can be at the same time beautiful and brutal.

That's why it's important to take time to reflect...
