## Exact post text

We used to spend $875,000 every year on our BDR team. Here's the uncomfortable truth about that rate of spend:

BDR teams, scaled up “the old way”, have stopped working.

Our outbound response rates have plummeted over the last 12 months.

Just like everyone else’s.

Why?

Because buyers’ inboxes are ruined.

99% of tech companies:

- Use the same automation tools
- Send the same email cadences
- Contract the same data vendors
- Message the same prospects

Which is bad, but...

Then AI tools came and made it 100x worse.

Here's the “old way” of running a sales organization:

- Pick a revenue target for the end of the year
- Model out how much manpower it’s going to take to get you there
- Hire, hire, hire, and then... hire some more

I made this mistake in 2023 at Retention.com.

Then our VP of Sales resigned.

Of course, it was a surprise at first.

But it caused me to rethink how we run our business.

I had read a few posts from Sam Jacobs about the “new way” of running sales.

Where $30M ARR companies were doing MORE sales with 2 reps than 25.

I spoke to founder friends and investors who were seeing the same.

When it became time to design the GTM strategy for RB2B (a completely different product in a totally different market), we decided to create one of these “new way” super-light, super-lean sales machines.

What’s shocking about it is that we’re better on every metric than any business we’ve ever run.

Here’s what we’re doing at RB2B to generate pipeline: 

1. We’re leveled up our Founder Brand content.

- Our goal is to be undisputed #1 at LinkedIn thought leadership content 
- It’s creating a huge amount of trust in the market and informing buyers of our solutions
- It’s the most efficient awareness-generating machine that has ever existed.

2. We went with a low-touch “freemium” pricing model.

- A viral content strategy benefits heavily from a viral pricing strategy
- If traditional outreach doesn’t work, free (but valuable) software will sell itself
- Our price points are way too low - people buy without thinking about it

3. We’re keeping the internal team as small as possible.

- We have five team members and have gone $0-1.5m ARR in 19 weeks
- Robb has done a fantastic job at creating a self-serve support experience
- We want to scale to $15-20m with under 10 people, then see where we’re at

This isn’t “predictable”, but it’s the most efficient way to scale up a SaaS that exists.

Will this work for everyone?

Probably not.

But the next generation of unicorns will look more like us than Salesforce.

Running as lean as possible.

And leading with great content.
