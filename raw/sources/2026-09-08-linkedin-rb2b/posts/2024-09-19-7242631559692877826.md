## Exact post text

Two weeks ago Sam Jacobs said that “growth is worth 3x profit”. Here are 6 reasons why I think that’s the wrong way for early-stage startup founders to look at it:

1. Monetizing that growth number is difficult and low probability

I understand these numbers are market rates, but I speak for the founders here. It’s incredibly difficult (and low probability) for a founder to achieve a meaningful liquidity event. Market rates are true for investors, but they don’t handicap this dynamic for founders.

2. Monetizing profit is an absolute certainty

There is no market rate way to represent the value of an ongoing substantial cash flow and the effect that can have on a founder’s life over time.

3. Smaller, profitable companies are easier to sell.

If you generate lots of cash, you will not only bank money with absolute certainty, if you decide you want to sell, there are 100x more buyers who will buy something at $50m that is very cash positive than a unicorn, where there is either the IPO option or a handful of buyers.

4. You’ll own all of the equity of a smaller, profitable company

Some people manage to have massive exits and still have massive equity when that exit happens. We hear about them, and celebrate them. Most who go down that road DON'T. If they are lucky enough to have the billion dollar exit, they are usually diluted to a point where the cash to them at exit might end up being the range of the high-probability mid-8-figure exit of the profitable bootstrapper.

5. Most VCs don’t love the idea of large secondaries

Founders assign too high of a probability to selling huge amounts of secondary. Some investors will let you, especially if you have a 0.01% incredible startup, but most end up letting you take $1-2 million to help pay for food, schools, etc.

Do you know what type of investor WILL give you a large secondary?

A growth equity investor who invests in profitable founder-led companies who doesn’t want to put incremental capital into the business.

6. Valuation is temporary, control is forever

You also have control, and as Nathan Latka says, valuation is temporary, control is forever. Don’t underestimate the benefit that true freedom has on your wellbeing.

TAKEAWAY:

I get fired up about this topic because it’s a weapon that gets used against us.

Growth definitely IS worth more than profit…

On paper.

And, to investors.

Also to companies who are already public, or headed there with a high degree of certainty.

Here’s the problem.

If you’ve got meaningful traction as an early-stage founder, it’s SO hard to rise above the excitement and appreciate how valuable profitability - and control - actually are.

Because for the Founder, growth’s “worth” can only get monetized one way - with some type of low-probability exit.

As a founder, profitability gets monetized every single day.

With absolute certainty.

AND you completely control your own destiny.

What’s that worth?
