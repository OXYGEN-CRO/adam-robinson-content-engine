## Exact post text

Last week our VP of Sales resigned. 

Instead of replacing him, we made the difficult decision to scale back our sales team from 14 to 5. 

As a commitment to building in public, I'm going to tell you why:


Back in December, we decided to ramp up our sales team and “go for it”.

My vision was to have 3-4 AE’s that were flooded with demos.

But that's not how it played out...


PROBLEM #1: I got the TAM totally wrong. 

We were overselling in Dec-Mar.

And we had no visibility into our product’s performance.

We didn’t find out until months later we were hitting the wrong target.

I thought we could sell a $30k deal to a universe of 50k customers.

And because of that belief, I had the absurd goal of trying to get to $50m ARR by year-end (from $7m). 

I was way off on all of these assumptions, and it caused us to over-hire. 

This was totally my fault.


PROBLEM #2: On April 1, the music stopped.

We had just got done celebrating a $1m ARR week. 

The next week we closed virtually zero business. 

Meanwhile, we kept hiring AE’s and BDR’s, thinking it was temporary.

That adding sellers and making them "hunters" would get us to $50m ARR.

This was also wrong.


Our VP of Sales resignation was a huge blow.

It forced me to ask some tough questions:

"What type of organization am I running?"

"Why would someone so important just up and quit?"

"Is it me? Is it my exec team? Is there a long line of others waiting to quit?"

I came to the conclusion that the thing we needed to change most in our sales org was empty calendars.

Just not nearly enough demos were being set.

This wasn't why the VP sales quit, but it is, at this point, the quickest way to right the ship.

This was a real wake-up call for us. 

If your VP of Sales quits, what you're doing isn’t working. 

So, I made the case to the exec team that we should pursue our original vision. 

Build a sales team that is completely inundated with demand. 

Lean, mean, and high-velocity.

This meant we had to do the hard thing, and scale back the team from 14 to 5.  

I’m sure competitors are going to start talking about how the walls are on fire Retention.com.

They’re not. 

We're going to be an unbelievable financial position in a few months.

But startups are hard, and this is the hardest part. 

Letting go of incredible team members.

It's hard sharing this failure publicly. 

"What will this make future candidates, prospects, investors think?!?!?"

But I’m going to keep building in public. 

I’m going to share my biggest L’s, in addition to my biggest W’s. 

Sharing that video about how we hit $1m ARR in a week felt so good. 

Sharing this feels terrible. 

But, it’s the journey we're on.
