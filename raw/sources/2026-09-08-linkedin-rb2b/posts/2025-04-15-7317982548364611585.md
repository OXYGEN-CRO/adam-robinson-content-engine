## Exact post text

Last week RB2B was accused of downloading CRM data without warning, being spyware, and then I was accused of not owning up to our terms of service. All of this is completely WRONG. Here’s what happened, and my response: 

The allegations: 

- RB2B downloads Hubspot CRM data without warning
- I am passing the buck and don’t stand behind our terms
- RB2B is spyware and we are reselling data to your competitors

The Facts: 

1. Our Hubspot integration is opt-in, tells you exactly what it’s doing, and has a cancel button if you don’t want to do it. 

The reason we created this Hubspot integration is that customers asked us to tag CRM users in the app and in Slack. In order to do that, you need to know who the contacts are. When you enable the integration, it shows you exactly what it’s doing, then gives you the option to cancel if you would rather just upload a file. This is a similar flow to every company that does anything like us (every data company period) that has CRM functionality within their app.

2. I stand by our terms. They are in no way egregious.

We hired the most reputable privacy attorneys in data to create our terms, and modeled them after who we considered the data industry standard when we launched, Apollo. They still very closely resemble Apollo’s terms. I stand by them as reasonable for what we are doing and who we aspire to be, do not believe they are in any way misleading or inappropriate.

3. RB2B is “spyware” and selling your data to customers

I can’t believe I have to respond to this, but NO we are not “using your deal data to show investors that your ‘fast growth’ is artificially inflated” or “using internal notes about renewals and red flags as a hit list” or “stealing your ICP.” Those are the ravings of an unwell marketing influencer with an axe to grind. 

What ARE we doing? 

- We’re downloading CRM data (with full disclosure and the ability to cancel)
- In the exact same way everybody else is
- For the exact same reason everybody else is (the customer asked)
- With terms of service that are materially identical to the industry leader

It begs the question … 

Why am I getting all of this heat?
