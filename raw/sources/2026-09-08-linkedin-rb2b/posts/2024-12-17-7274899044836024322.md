## Exact post text

As I look at 2025 for RB2B, I hate all my options. If you’re an early-stage SaaS founder, and you're being 100% honest with yourself, you probably do too. Here’s why:

1. Anything we build (that works) will get copied in 1 second

I’m not saying we’re great at product, but if we were, and we did what it took to figure out how to actually get the person-level ID to work, people would just copy everything we did immediately, at a WAY faster pace than ever.

2. It feels like every SaaS is a race to the bottom price war

What’s the result? “More for less” offers, tangential competitors/partners all building everything, trend towards the marginal cost of distribution, which isn’t zero, but it’s still much lower than here.

3. I don’t like the idea of building everything under the sun

Everybody brings up Rippling for the example of what to do in this market, but I don’t think they realize how absolutely incredible the execution has to be across the board to pull Rippling off. Very, very few teams can actually do this. 

4. I don’t think users actually WANT another platform

There is a narrative that you need to be a supplier or a platform, even if workflows that you are enabling could be done in platforms that the customer already has. I don’t like this path, but VC-backed companies are forced down that road.

5. I haven’t spent enough time w/ AI to understand how it can help

This is my fault. I am a late adopter. We use Fin for support, and I get why LLMs are incredible at doing research, but outside of that, I really don’t know the use cases for AI, and as such, am probably being lapped by those who do.

TAKEAWAY

2025 will be a tough year for early-stage SaaS. 

On one hand, it’s amazing that it’s easier to start a SaaS than ever. 

On the other hand, that means you have 100 competitors the second you get something working.

What’s my plan for 2025? 

I’m trying to focus on things we can do that other people can’t. 

Can I get better at content? 

Can I get better at community? 

Can I build a brand that actually matters?

I’ve been thinking about these things a LOT recently. 

If the answer is “no, no, no”… 

Then here we go 2025. 

Let the Rat Race begin.
