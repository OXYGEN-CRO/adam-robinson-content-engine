## Exact post text

Just 12 weeks post-launch, RB2B crossed $600k ARR. Sounds great, right? Tomorrow we're going to make a move that will cut our revenue by +50%. Here's why I couldn't be MORE EXCITED about it:

BACKGROUND:

- We “accidentally” launched our person-level identity product on March 1
- We tried to give the leads and a push to slack away for free
- We wanted to charge $495 for integrations, but you can parse the slack block
- Our free-to-paid conversion rate is only 1.3%

I have written a few posts about how our pricing is broken. 

- It’s broken for smaller businesses because it’s too expensive 
- It’s broken for larger businesses because we don’t have an ICP filter yet
- It’s broken for everybody because no one knows what to do with the leads

The solution (thank you LinkedIn) is:

- Give the product away for free to 85-90% of the market
- Cap the free plan, force larger businesses to pay for leads
- Build the ICP filter as quickly as possible

Here’s the first PROBLEM with that plan:

We currently have 110 paying customers.

In order to avoid confusion - and do the right thing - we’re going to:

- Drop people down in price who fall into a lower plan
- Not jack the price up on people who JUST started paying us $495

Here’s the second (much LARGER) problem:

- 35% of our 110 customers are going to drop from $495 to $119/mo
- Another 30% are dropping from $495 to $249/mo

That’s going to decimate our ARR.

Especially because I don’t have the confidence to raise prices at the high-end yet, even though I have a very strong feeling that most of our highest-volume customers are getting value well beyond $495.

So why am I excited? 

1. We’ve spoken to 100s of users. I believe this pricing change, combined with the two-touch sales motion we’re putting in place could 5x our conversion rate.

2. I think we can implement PLG for low-value users at these lower price point and get touch-free conversions (but … they will be very high churn)

TAKEAWAY:

$600k ARR in 12 weeks has been nothing short of amazing.

Having to then make a move that will take revenue down by 50% feels terrible.

I’m not sure my younger self could stomach a move like this one.

(For anybody $500k-$1m ARR - could YOU chop it 50% and sleep at night?)

Early on Santosh and I declared that at RB2B we would not make decisions optimized for revenue.

Instead, we would optimize for: 

1. User growth 
2. Low time-to-value
3. Eliminating friction

Looking back, I think my younger self was playing not to lose.

No I want to play to win.

When you have the ability to look past revenue and optimize for user base and viral expansion…

That’s the global domination mindset.
