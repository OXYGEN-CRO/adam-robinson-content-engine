## Exact post text

Here’s RB2B's most recent Churn Numbers - they’re not pretty. Why? Because when we launched 9 months ago I was WRONG about 3 major assumptions:

1. I thought that the “little guy” would get value from the product.

I thought the B2B market would differ from ecomm in two major ways - there would be less price sensitivity (even at the bottom) AND low volume outreach would still be effective. It turns out that people need traffic to make this work and there’s a pretty big “what now” problem.

2. I thought larger businesses would be happy using Clay. 

Many are, but we still find most aren’t, and in B2B, large companies can have a surprisingly small amount of traffic if their ACV is high. We are now filtering and routing through the CRM, which we should have done from the start.

3. We still don’t know who our “dream customer” is. 

If you look at our highest paying customers, the persona doesn’t match the B2B SaaS buyer that we thought we were targeting. We’re 9 months old and we need some major product improvements and we need to target and onboard a different workflow than we’ve been selling. 

TAKEAWAY:

These churn numbers are interesting. 

But I want to make sure I’m looking at this the right way....

Here’s my Q to all you product nerds out there: 

1. What changes do you think we should make in 2025?
3. Why do you think the low plans churn more?
2. Who do you think our ICP is?

Let me know in the comments!
