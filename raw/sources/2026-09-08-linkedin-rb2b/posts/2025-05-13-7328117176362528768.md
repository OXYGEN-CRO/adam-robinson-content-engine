## Exact post text

Agency CEO: I’m thinking about building a SaaS product. I’ve been talking to people about what I want to build, and a few investors have offered seed funding. Should I raise $1-$2m to start building it? 

Me: Absolutely not.

Agency CEO: Why not? 

Me: Can you fund development from your agency profits? 

Agency CEO: It would be tight, but yes, we could. 

Me: Do that. Period.

Agency CEO: But we’ll probably get good terms. Like $10m valuation or something. 

Me: You should never ever EVER raise money for a product that doesn’t exist yet if you don’t have to, ESPECIALLY if you are building SaaS for the first time.

Agency CEO: Why not?

Me: Product creation is incredibly risky, mostly because human intuition is a horrible indicator of market viability. You will burn through the money and be left with nothing.

Agency CEO: Should I just bag the idea and keep running my agency? 

Me: I didn’t say that… What I DID say is that you should not raise money, at least until you have product-market fit, preferably longer. 

Agency CEO: How do I get product-market fit?

Me: In an ideal world, before you even start building, you start running a proper discovery process. Talk to hundreds of prospects about their days and their workflows, and get a real sense as to how painful the problem is that you’re solving. If you can sell it before you even have it, that’s a very strong sign that you are on your way to PMF. 

Agency CEO: Yeah but where do I even start? How do I find these people?

Me: You dial your messaging in and start doing outreach to your network and to your networks’ network. I’ve had success asking people for feedback. My friend Andy Mewborn gets great traction from offering a $100 Amazon gift card in exchange for that feedback.

Agency CEO: But WHEN? I’m super busy running my agency. 

Me: You need to treat this discovery effort as a side hustle and start working on nights and weekends. Trust me - if you do not do this first, your SaaS will fail.

Agency CEO: That is far less exciting than raising $2m. 

Me: That may be true - but most startups fail. If you actually do it, you’ll have a far greater chance at actually succeeding. 

Don’t raise $1-$2m. 

Run a proper discovery process before you do anything.

You’ll thank me in a few years.
