## Exact post text

Two weeks ago I was having a drink with John Mackey (Founder of Whole Foods), and I nearly spit out my drink when he turned to me and said, “VC’s RUIN great businesses.” Wait… WHAT?!!?

Me: Tell me exactly what you mean by that, John.

Mackey: VC’s need to shoot for the moon. There are a LOT of great businesses that aren’t meant to be trillion dollar company.

Me: But didn’t Whole Foods do $22b in sales last year? 

Mackey: I’m not talking about Whole Foods - I’m talking about the MANY other Founders I’ve watched get on the VC treadmill, only to waste money trying to push a business farther than its natural state, ultimately destroying a huge amount of value.

Me: Funny enough - I see the same thing all the time. With software in particular.

Mackey: Tell me what you mean by that.

Me: I always thought that software businesses had the characteristics to be INCREDIBLE small-to-medium sized cash generating businesses. I’m talking - $50m ARR with $25m profit. We don’t have to move physical goods, so with a good enough product, we should be able to be WAY more profitable. 

Mackey: I agree with you there. 

Me: Yet, I don’t see anybody doing that. I only know of a few examples - Ross Andrew Paquette from Maropost being one of them, who has been generating over $20m profit for more than a decade. Nobody in our ecosystem even knows it’s possible, so they all head down the VC route as soon as they get any traction. Most fail, but even worse, some spend a decade or two creating businesses that are quite valuable, but not actually valuable to THEM.

Mackey: That’s one major problem. The problem I had at Whole Foods was that I needed VCs to fund my acquisitive growth strategy, but after seven or eight years they needed OUT. It forced me to IPO well before I otherwise would have.

Me: You’re a billionaire now - would you ever use VC money again? 

Mackey: If I get Love Life scaling, and I’m trying to ramp thousands of physical locations in a short period of time, I definitely will, because I don’t see a better alternative. 

Me: What will you do differently? 

Mackey: I’ll be planning for their exit from Day 1.

TAKEAWAY

John Mackey thinks VC ruins great businesses. 

Yet, he’s very open to using VC money again. 

But only using it under the following conditions: 

1. Proven product-market fit of his retail business
2. He’s ready for rapid, international expansion
3. The exit (IPO) is planned from Day 1

Until then… 

He’s a bootstrapper.
