## Exact post text

The past 4 months Retention.com has shrunk from $22m to $21.2m ARR. 

Here’s how bad things are inside the company: 

THE BAD: 

1. Retention.com shrank ARR another 2.3% in March, mostly churn related
2. RB2B: 0.6% free-to-paid conversion rate, and getting worse

I’m dreading posting this, filled with guilt/shame about our performance.

Looking at this graph (see picture) hurts me emotionally.

So much of my identity is tied to that topline ARR number.

If it’s going up, I’m happy and feel like THE MAN.

If it’s going down, I’m miserable and inadequate, like I’m letting my team down.

I want to post that we’re crushing it.

I want our employees and prospects to see it.

I want other founders to respect it.

Posting -2.3%, down for the 4th month in a row is the opposite of that.

I now have a zero-growth (worse… SHRINKING) EBITDA business that according to Jason M. Lemkin ISN’T WORTH ANYTHING (quoting him from CEO Summit in AZ).

Here’s the crazy thing about all of this:

Despite feeling like dogshit, if I make a T chart, there are far more positive things going on than negative.

On the Retention.com side:

- Our engineering team figured out how to boost resolution by 10-20%
- We made huge strides in understanding why we are losing to competitors
- We are identifying inefficiencies and understanding how to operate better
- We are (finally) implementing EOS as an operating framework
- The team crushed ShopTalk, booked over 40 demos
- Our internal NPS on an employee engagement grew from 41 to 72 in Q1
- We’re printing money - generated $1.67m free cash THIS QUARTER

On the RB2B side:

- We got over 2,800 free signups 30 days since launch
- We have over a 78% install rate (WHAT?!)
- The product feedback is incredible
- Our team of 5 is improving everything by the second (product, support, etc)
- Our LinkedIn community is providing an insane lift to our product and brand

There’s more GOOD at our company than any time in my career.

Incredibly exciting, long-term growth potential.

Still, when I look at that shrinking ARR chart, I feel terrible.

Why?

Two reasons:

1. I’ve been programmed by the VC/PE world to think up-and-to-the-right ARR is what makes a “good” company. And by association, a “good” founder. Shrinking ARR makes me feel like a "bad" founder.

2. As we keep posting down months, the unreasonable expectations I (stupidly) set for our ARR growth last year are hitting me in the face.

The most painful part?

A year ago I thought that we’d be a unicorn by now.

In hindsight, that is a completely absurd.

The sad thing is regardless of how stupid we are when we extrapolate what we think our businesses will be worth in the future, it becomes real to us.

And we experience a feeling of loss as that value evaporates.

I know I’m not the only one around here feeling this way.

Fortunately, the way out is simple.

We just keep building.

P.S.
