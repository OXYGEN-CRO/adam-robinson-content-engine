## Exact post text

Last night I had a chat with a SaaS founder who raised $60M for his startup. The latest round was a $40M Series B at a $400m valuation at the end of 2021. 
He thought he was on the unicorn path, but now it's a VC nightmare.

After the latest raise, he went on a hiring and spending spree, built out a half-baked “platform”, spent boat-loads on go-to-market.

In the beginning, it all looked like it was working. 

For six months revenue spiked and they were closing deals left and right. 

But the last 18 months have been brutal.

Massive layoffs, horrible churn, sprinting to profitability.

Before that series B - even though they were venture backed - they were focused, lean, profitable, and actually had decent retention.

Here’s the bombshell...

What’s their ARR 3 years after the deal?

About the same as it was BEFORE the Series B.

Except instead of being profitable, now they are burning.

Instead of a core product suite of high-retention products, they have a leaky platform.

And the STRESS LEVEL of the last three years… 

He didn’t even want to talk about it.

The problem is, for this founder, there’s no way out.

It’s a zombie company that raised (and burned) way too much capital. 

There’s not a clear path to an exit with a 1-3x return for investors.

The truth is I’ve been stuck like this a bunch of times.

In fact, I did something very similar in 2023… 

I hired up for massive growth, and spent a boat load on GTM, and instead of 9-figure ARR, I got a bunch of churn.

But unlike this founder, who is stuck trying to move this zombie forward …

As a bootstrapped founder, I was able to pivot, and focus on efficiency.

We got a lot smaller.

We started a new business (RB2B), and found two new TAMs in two totally different markets. 

And even though we also failed at the unicorn dream, we fell back on a massively profitable $25m ARR company.

So what’s next for my founder friend?

Thinking about it is anxiety-inducing.

What’s next for us??? 

Anything we want.

If you can bootstrap… 

Bootstrap.
