## Exact post text

Last week the legendary startup founder Hiten Shah said, “The Celebrity Founder is a Modern Illusion”. Here’s why he’s wrong:

I not only think it’s a modern REALITY.

I think it will soon be a REQUIREMENT for getting a startup off the ground.

Why are Hiten and I on opposite ends of the spectrum?


1. Hiten’s main argument is that it’s impossible to have a personal brand AND relentless focus on improving your product

I disagree.

I’m a living example of the opposite.

In fact, to flip the argument COMPLETELY…

My personal brand is the MAIN DRIVER of the feedback that leads to product improvement.

- We had 0.1% free-to-paid conversion rate. LinkedIn told me what to change. Our free-to-paid conversion rate is now over 10%
- We had 17%/mo revenue churn rate after 6mo. Linkedin told me how to fix it. It dropped to 10.5% (still bad) but I know how to get it to reasonable levels

We have a ton more product improvements coming, which are a combination of conversations with LinkedIn and us eating our own dog food…

We're building something that our team can’t live without.

So, you can build a personal brand and be product-obsessed.


2. The Value of Personal Brand is up-and-to-the-right

I am 100% certain response rates to cold email will be lower in 2025 than in 2024 (and every year thereafter).

I am equally certain that the trust built from organic social media content will be higher in 2025 than 2024 (and every year thereafter).

This is a mega-trend.

Harnessing the power of social and directing that toward your company is the most efficient way to get a startup off the ground.

No, you can't do what I have done without a great product ($0-4m ARR in 8mo)

But you could not have done it without a large personal brand either.

If you would have given me $100m to spend, it would not have matched the reach that the last 12mo of posting created.


3. Hiten says “Fame is expensive”

I didn’t want to be famous.

Until I was famous to my prospects.

And saw it help EVERY PART OF OUR BUSINESS.

Fame has made it so:

- I can work with people I have no business working with
- We have enterprise customers we have no business selling to
- I get invited to speak in front of 100s of people (for free)

I am now trying to figure out ways to grow this affinity.

Will it cause me to lose my focus on product? 

I don’t believe so, because I have spent 5 years on either side of product-market fit, and I know that with a great product everything works and with a bad product nothing works.

Do you know what would have been expensive? 

Growing RB2B like we did if I WASN’T famous.


TAKEAWAY

Relentless focus on product is a requirement for a startup. 

Perhaps young founders who don’t realize that this is their #1 priority as CEO would benefit from reading Hiten’s post.

Because you’re going to fail without relentless product focus.

But, once you internalize that focus… 

There is ONE THING that will move you faster than ANYTHING.

And that thing is “Founder Brand”.
