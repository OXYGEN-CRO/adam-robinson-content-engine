## Exact post text

I’ve been unfortunate enough to have to let go of some very senior people in my last decade. There’s a right way and a wrong way to do it.

In my experience, I’ve learned a lot about it. So, God forbid you have to fire a senior employee, this is how you should go about it:

1. Be definitive.

When you decide to have that conversation, you need to be extremely precise with your wording.

It’s like a breakup.

If you plant a seed in someone’s head that there may be some opportunity down the line, it’s much worse than being upfront about what you mean.

There can be no door left open. Just be straight up and tell them what YOU would want to hear so that this senior leader can move on.

2. Be kind and empathetic.

Even if it’s clear that the employee and the company are not a good fit for one another, and you both *know that,* you still need to absolutely be kind to the person you’re letting go.

I’ve been fired from some high-paying jobs. It sucks, even though I didn’t like the job.

They’ll feel blindsided, but you need them to know that it’s not them, it’s you.

3. Be generous.

This is absolutely crucial if you value your reputation as a company.

Again, to go back to the breakup analogy, if you end things terribly with your partner…word will spread.

You’ll get bad reviews on Glassdoor (the group chat) and nobody else will want to work with (date) you.

It’ll cost you some time and money to be generous enough to give them some leeway, but it’s worth it.

For example, work out a deal where the senior employee can actively search for other positions while they’re working for you.

Or, you could say that they resigned instead of saying that you canned them. That’s nice.

And obviously, a severance package can be negotiated if you’re up for it.

Some companies have paid up to 24 months of compensation for employees that have spent more than 5 years working there.

That’s a little crazy, so hopefully, that doesn’t happen to you. But, it is what it is. Reputation is everything.

4. Help the person find another job.

I wouldn’t make this a LinkedIn post about this.

Maybe go direct to your network and see who’s hiring so you can make an intro.

That’s likely the best way to go about this to eliminate any sort of shame or embarrassment for the employee.

This will go a long way if you want to create a positive culture inside and outside of your company.

If you’re on either end of this exchange, I’m sorry.

And my DMs are open if you have questions.
