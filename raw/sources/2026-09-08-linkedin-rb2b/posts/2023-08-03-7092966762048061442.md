## Exact post text

If you're over 40 you might not recognize this guy, but every serious entrepreneur needs to study him.

Here's 4 lessons you can learn from a group of college-age friends who turned a video game YouTube channel into a $1 Billion brand listed on the NASDAQ.

Let’s take a look at the backstory...

In 2010, three gamer kids with the nicknames “CLipZ” (pictured), “Timid” and “Resistance” started a YouTube channel showcasing their Call of Duty skills.

They called themselves FaZe Clan.

Through a mix of engaging content and the force of their personality, they reached a million subscribers within 2 years.

After expanding into other games and winning tournaments under the FaZe brand, they attracted outside investors like Pitbull, Ben Simmons and Offset.

They acquired teams to participate in other competitions, increasing their brand recognition and presence across the Esports world.

In July 2022, FaZe Clan went public in a $725 million SPAC

Today, they have top level teams in ten of the hottest games on the market and continue to be leaders in the world of not just Esports, but youth culture. 

Here's the 4 lessons from their entrepreneurial journey:


1) Find your niche.

In 2010, the Esports streaming world was filled with players posting their winning shots. FaZe focused on trickshots that distinguished them from the bunch. Winning a tournament? Easy. "360 noscope headshot jumping off a ledge"? Unforgettable.


2) Expand on brand.

FaZe’s mission has always been to recruit talented and engaging personalities who build relationships with the audience. When adding teams for Counter-Strike, Fortnite, or FIFA, the goal remains the same. Expand vertically or horizontally, but always on brand.


3. Skate where the puck is headed.

When FaZe’s YouTube channel gained traction, the world of Esports was wide open. People were hungry for competitive video game content presented in a fun way by relatable creators. FaZe saw the opportunity ahead and invented the branded Esports team


4. Protect your brand.

In 2021, members of FaZe used their platform to push Save The Kids token, or $KIDS, a pump and dump cryptocurrency, to its audience under the guise of raising money for sick children. The offending members were immediately removed from FaZe and an apology was issued.


Today, FaZe Holdings is a publicly listed company on the NASDAQ and had $70M in revenue in 2022.

Not bad for three kids playing video games.
