## Exact post text

Me: I’m killing all our paid spend. Cold email, ads, UGC, SEO, everything.

CFO: WTF. What??? That doesn’t make sense. Didn’t cold emails and Linkedin ads acquire a ton of customers last year? 

Me: Our reporting said it did. And it may have helped… a little. But look at what happened as the year went on. The more we pushed - on ads, email, etc - the more money we spent and we got back less for every dollar. Diminishing returns. It’s not sustainable.

CFO: But if we turn paid off, won’t our growth hit a wall?

Me: In the short term, maybe. But maybe not. Nobody in B2B has the answer because everybody is terrified of turning anything off that “says” it’s working. Personally, I want to find out. And, I also want to spend ALL my marketing time and money creating a deep emotional connection in the mind of the market with the people who AREN’T in the market to buy right now.

CFO: That sounds great in theory, but how will you know it’s working? 

Me: Day to day we won’t. But here’s the thing - at any given time only 20% of our prospects are in market. If our stuff is good enough, it will constantly be in front of the 80% that aren’t ready to buy. We’ll know it’s working in a year or two… If we’re still growing faster than everybody else in our space with a fraction of the team size and zero paid spend (while they’ve ALL burned $10’s of millions of VC).

CFO: So your plan is to create organic social content for the 80% who AREN’T going to buy? 

Me: Exactly. The downside is we don’t have last-click or multi-touch attribution to tell us it’s “working”, but all that is BS anyway. Just because you can measure a click doesn’t mean that was why someone booked a demo. Most of the time it’s just tech getting in the way of something that was going to happen anyway, with vendors claiming credit to justify high pricing.

CFO: I still don’t understand why we wouldn’t focus on people who are ready to buy. 

Me: When people are in market, they are rationally shopping vendors for features and pricing. When they’re not, our organic content becomes an automated trust-building machine. When it comes time to buy, we’ll be who they think of when they are ready to add Person-Level Website Visitor Identity to their stack. Most of the time they’ll come straight to us, at the very least we’ll be in the deal.

CFO: So it becomes a type of moat...

Me: I watched 37 vendors whip up a similar product to ours in the 12 months since we launched last year. With AI, things are only going to move faster. In the future, brand will be this type of trust, built up over months if not years, one post at a time.

CFO: Alright, I’m starting to see where you’re coming from. 

Me: I forgot one more thing... If it all works out, RB2B will be WILDLY profitable. 

CFO: You know I love that. Spoken like a true bootstrapper!
