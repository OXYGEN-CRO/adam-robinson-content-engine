## Exact post text

I wanted to share something that I used to be deeply ashamed of.

Truly one of the worst things I’ve done as a founder, and I hope that no one has to go through the same thing…

In my early days as a founder of another startup, I’d gotten my hands on a list of leads from a data mining company. A *lot* of leads.

We knew the quality would be pretty close to 100% trash, but we didn’t know how close to 100% trash they were.

In order to figure it out, we had 3 of our best sales guys go through some of these leads and see if they were workable.

After a brief testing period, it seemed like they were qualified enough that we could scale this thing as fast as possible.

We were foaming at the mouth to get a ton of sales talent on these leads.

So we hired a head hunter to get us a bunch of top salespeople, trained the heck out of them, and put them on the new lead list.

Very quickly, we realized that these leads were 99.99% trash. As close as you can get to what we hoped would *not* be the case.

Crickets.

Not only were these leads not converting… they weren’t even answering the phone when one of my 25 new salespeople would call them.

It wasn’t a problem with our scripts, our product, or our talent. 

It was a lead problem.

I’d made a massive mistake.

I’d just incurred the expense of bringing on some expensive salespeople, and they suddenly had *nothing* to do all day. We were burning daylight trying to get this lead list to work.

The day we realized this, it was a nightmare.

I’d spoken to some of my mentors at the time, and they all told me what I already knew.

The next day, I took 25 of my 32 employees into a room, and I said:

“If you’re in this room, you no longer have a job with us.”

I had wasted the salespeople’s time, the recruiter’s time, the people that trained them, etc.

I felt horrible. Leading on 25 people who thought they had an opportunity, and I blew it.

Firing ONE person is hard.

Firing 25 is a tragedy.

Hopefully sharing this helps you learn from my mistakes, so you don’t have to go through the pain that this hard decision caused.
