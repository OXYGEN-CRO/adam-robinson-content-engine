## Exact post text

Last year I spoke to 50+ top-tier VC firms about investing in my bootstrapped (profitable) $25m ARR SaaS. Here are 7 reasons why I walked away and decided NEVER to take VC money:

1. Dividends are good

If you create a successful startup, you should be rewarded by getting paid millions of dollars every year… Not just some *potentially* huge number at some point MAYBE in the future.

2. The investor value prop is weak (if you don’t need capital)

I listened to pitch after pitch of investors saying their “GTM advisory would be game-changing” for us. This pitch was weak and designed to get founders like me dreaming big dreams that if they panned out would largely be my team’s doing.

3. A new seat at the table is a net negative. Period

At best, investors will slow you down. At worst, they will ruin your life AND destroy your common equity if your business slows down for macro reasons outside your control.

4. Investors are in the portfolio game. You’re not.

Situations emerge - FREQUENTLY - where what is best for your life and company can be usurped by a group of investors wanting a quick 2x. Even if they have a minority position, there are SO many ways they can exert influence and drive the outcome that is best for the fund.

5. I want to do things investors would not tolerate

My strategy for 2024 is to give away a super-valuable product to millions of people using viral distribution strategies and do a lightning-fast PLG. With a team that is unreasonably lean. While continuing to add value to the free offering. THE SECOND any of this plan went sour, I would be told to stop, and if I didn’t, I’d be fired.

6. I don’t believe in measuring things or spreadsheets

This is controversial. The only thing I measure is ARR and churn… and the one and only Nathan Latka and the Founderpath team created a new revenue dashboard that is pretty cool (see below). Unlike other dashboards, they help you estimate what your business is worth (both in a raise and sale) based on relevant deals in the market.

7. Large carrots dangled in front of us lead to bad decisions

An investor’s job is to figure out what number will motivate the founder to transact and get them to attach their identity to that number. We love validation that our beautiful business IS worth $100m, even if it actually lowers the probability-weighted financial outcome for the founder.

TAKEAWAY:

Last year I was seduced by the idea I could take millions off the table and move even faster.

After speaking to an investor a week for all of 2023, I realized those millions would likely change many of the things about my life I value the most.

I’d sacrifice independence, freedom, and it would slow us down.

That's why I'm a bootstrapper at heart.

And I always will be.
