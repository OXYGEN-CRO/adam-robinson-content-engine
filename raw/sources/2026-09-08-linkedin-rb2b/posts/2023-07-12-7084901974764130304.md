## Exact post text

I’ve been an entrepreneur for over a decade, I started one company in 2012 and sold it for 8-figures, now I have a bootstrapped SaaS for DTC companies that does $20M in ARR.

Here are the 10 things I wish I knew before I started my businesses:

1. Spend as little money as humanly possible until you have product-market fit.

2. It’s never going to get easier. The secret is to fall in love with the grind.

3. It’s as easy (maybe easier) to create something big as it is something small, and the rewards are so much larger. Don’t waste your time on small opportunities.

4. There is an area of study called “product” that you are unaware exists, but you will have a knack for and it is the single most important determinant of success. Go all-in on becoming an expert at it.

5. If the product doesn’t make your buyer’s face light up when they hear about it for the first time, don’t build it.

6. Chop your expectation of the outcome of your first venture by 90%. If you are still excited, pursue it. 

7. Lengthen the time you think it will take you to achieve an outcome by 10x. If you’re still excited, pursue it.

8. It’s all about the quality of talent that you can attract to work on your project. 

9. Make sure you like working with that talent, because the outcome will be a crap shoot.

10. Spend as much time as possible around entrepreneurs who are a few steps ahead of you. Draw inspiration from their wisdom, but do not treat it as prescription.

Bottom line:

Double-down on product and frugality. 

You’ll crush it in the long run.
