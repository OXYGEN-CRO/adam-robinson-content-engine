## Exact post text

When you’re as obsessed with building a company as we are, you begin to find new ways to measure success.

For DTC, your main metric is ROAS.

For SaaS, it’s CAC:LTV.

That’s a great way to know how good your ads are, but it’s not a great way to determine how fast you’ll grow in the long term.

After running Robly and Retention.com, I’ve realized there’s a better, more important metric that people don’t often use.

Velocity.

To illustrate this, let me give you an example of what velocity is NOT.

When I was running Robly, we had a very slow onboarding process that was nowhere near scalable.

If someone joined our $30/month plan, we’d have someone get on a call with them, transfer their contacts from our competitors, upload them into our software, and then have a designer create a template that looked similar to the one they were using in the other ESP.

It was kind of a nightmare.

Very, very slow and inefficient to get people into our product.

Or, a more recent example of what velocity is not, with Retention.com.

When we started, we were a team of 6 highly-efficient people. We scaled to $12M ARR in 2 years like this.

The main roadblock was that my co-founder Diana was in charge of all things related to sales.

She was really the only person who had any real information on how to make decisions that everything had to go through her.

This slowed us down a ton.

Beyond that, our plans were confusing for most of our customers.

It was a number of different monthly tiers based on traffic, contacts, etc.

This led to a low conversion rate and a lot of churn.

To eliminate that friction, we now have 3 plans that make complete sense to someone that sees them for the first time, the pricing is better, and we have annual deals that’ll reduce churn and allow us to collect more cash upfront.

Great example of increasing velocity.

Just that metric alone lets us evaluate our business in a different way.

Santosh will shout this from the mountaintop.

I listen to everything Santosh says. You should, too.
