## Exact post text

I’ve been in the world of entrepreneurship for over a decade

It has been the craziest roller coaster I’ve ever been on. Both emotionally and financially…

After this amount of time doing various projects, making money, losing money, meeting people, and learning from all of it, I’ve grown a lot.

And now, at age 43, only NOW do I feel like I’ve finally gotten to a place where I’m genuinely excited about the future.

This is all congruent with a quote attributed to Tom Clancy that I absolutely love:

“An overnight success is 10 years in the making.”

It resonates because it’s true.

It’s the reason I decided to call my podcast ’10 Years in the Making.’

We think of success as a thing that happens to us — not a thing that we do to ourselves.

We make it sound like a completely random event that appears on our doorstep at a certain point in time when we’re deemed lucky enough.

A major fault in this line of thinking is that we refer to success in “outputs” instead of “inputs”.

When I had my friend Chase Dimond on my podcast, it just solidified this idea for me

Chase is one of the top experts in eCommerce, email marketing, and DTC copywriting

He has 100K followers on Twitter, over 150K followers on LinkedIn, and he has over 50K subscribers on his personal newsletter

He owns an 8 figure digital marketing agency, working with some of the biggest names in DTC, and consults for public companies on a monthly basis

For someone with this much success, you’d be surprised to know that he’s only been “famous” in the industry for 2 years

And you’d be even more surprised to know that he was almost COMPLETELY unknown for 5 years before that

Tweeting and posting to an audience of 5, and 4 of which were related to him

An overnight success, though, right?

Chase was not successful because he had a viral moment and rode the wave for as long as he could. That’s “output” success

He was successful because he posts multiple pieces of content per day that are valuable to hundreds of thousands of people. And he never took a break. That’s “input” success.

The inputs were very calculated and measurable.

In the short term, you are guaranteeing “input” success by contributing a penny every day to the platforms you want to grow on.

In the long term, you are guaranteeing “output” success, because the rest of the formula is already complete. Good content + posting consistently + time = output success.

This is true in Chase’s case, and it’s just finally become true in my case.

I have spent the last 10 years fighting for “output” success, frustrated because I thought all of the inputs were there.

It was only when I began putting 110% of my time into Retention.com that I realized that I actually had a shot at output success.

The lesson here is that success is an equation.

And we almost always forget time.

Time allows us to compound knowledge, experience, and reach.

And it’s the only true way to become an overnight success.

🤝
