## Exact post text

Ekster spent $500 in total on their launch and made $350k back.
$350,000 on launch, from scratch. How?

It wasn’t some super sneaky, scrappy early-stage founder strategy.
They used Kickstarter.

Olivier and his co-founder had studied Kickstarter for years beforehand.

The science behind the biggest launches, how they went viral, and all of the ingredients that went into a ton of eyeballs for little-to-no money.

The $500 was spent on a video, shot and edited by Olivier’s sister.

To turn that into $350k in sales, the key is to get to the “Taking Off” section of Kickstarter.

These are the 5 things they did to get there.
1. Nail PR.

Reach out to journalists who can cover what you’re building in advance of the launch.

Do a social media campaign to collect emails of people who are interested in the product or the brand.

Kickstarter launches don’t operate in a vacuum. Just because you’re on Kickstarter does not mean that you’re going to make guaranteed money.

You still need to build an audience and a reputation beforehand.

2. Get as many pledges as possible.

A pledge on Kickstarter is a way of demonstrating support with a quantifiable number.

Every project creator sets their project's funding goal and deadline.
If people like the project, they can pledge money to make it happen.

If the project succeeds in reaching its funding goal, all backers' credit cards are charged when time expires.

Make sure that you have a low pledge AOV so that more people will be able to participate.

3. Engage with the people commenting.

This is where you start to get a little scrappy.

One of my favorite startup motifs is “do things that don’t scale” from Paul Graham.

As the founder, make sure that you interact and engage with every single person who comments on your video so that you can build that authenticity with your audience.

These people will be crucial to the success of your business down the road.

Your first 1000 customers are the most valuable thing to a launch like this. Make sure they know that.

4. Update your audience regularly.

Once you have someone’s email, or their phone number, or they follow you on social media, etc, update them as frequently as possible.

Brands often fizzle out when they have a great launch and forget to keep their leads excited in the time it takes to create and ship the product.

Communication is key.

As long as you’re in contact with them and letting them know where you’re at in production, no timeline is too long.

5. The first 24 hours can make or break you.

A while back, I wrote about BarkBox and how successful they were when they launched.

The founder of BarkBox literally went up to strangers in a dog park and sold them on preorders for dog toys.

His first 24 hours were indicative of how well the company would do years later, and now they’re worth $1.6B.

If you can nail the first 24 hours, you’ll be golden.

And if you have a product like Ekster, maybe you’ll get to $50M a year like they did, too.
