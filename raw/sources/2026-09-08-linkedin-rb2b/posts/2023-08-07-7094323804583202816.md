## Exact post text

Building Products in 2012 vs Building Products in 2023:
 
Building Products in 2012:

- Let’s save money and get v1 built overseas
- Let’s just copy [insert market leader]’s UI/UX
- Let’s build it first and once it’s almost done let’s find our customers
- We should build a market-leader’s feature set before we launch
- Doing the same thing as the market leader for a cheaper price will work
- Let’s try to under-cut a $30/mo SaaS by 50%
- Let’s go into a super-mature, super-commoditized market
- Let’s have 25 salespeople and 1 engineer and zero product people
- Let’s build a small lifestyle business

Total minimum viable product build time = 18 months
 
Building Products in 2023:

- Only entertain ideas that are “money for a discount” and show quick ROI
- Stay away from any idea that is less than $1,000/mo SaaS
- When you come across an idea, talk to 100 people in the ICP about it
- Watch their facial expressions, see micro-reactions to judge how good it is
- Make sure 80% of people have “holy shit, how do I get that right now” face
- If so, figure out if MVP is possible with zero engineering resources expended
- It’s ok to do everything manually with VA’s until 10-20 cases are proven
- If dev work is required, do as much as possible outside of core team
- Create so few features that the experience is “borderline unbearable”
- Gather feedback for 60-90 days of the initial cohort of users
- See if they still consider your "unbearable" experience a high NPS product
- If so, team up with product, figure out how to make the experience delightful
- Once UX is done, cut the line in the dev queue and insert yourself at the front
- Do whatever intricate go-to-market motion makes sense for the product

Total minimum viable product build time = less than 8 weeks

Every single thing that I did when I built my first SaaS product I now think is wrong. 

In fact, I’m proud of myself for how far I’ve come.

Not just as an entrepreneur, but how careful I am with engineering time.

I messed up everything I could the first time around. 

Don’t make the same mistakes I did.
