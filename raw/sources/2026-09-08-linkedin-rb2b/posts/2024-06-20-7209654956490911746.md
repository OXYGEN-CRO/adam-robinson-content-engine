## Exact post text

Marketers love saying B2B is years behind B2C. I don't disagree, but I think their reasoning is wrong. Here are 5 things that B2C nailed that are INEVITABLE in the B2B world (and sexy branding isn't one of them): 

BACKGROUND

When people make this declaration, it’s often about style over substance.

They mean things like:

1. Salesforces’ branding will look like Athletic Greens
2. White papers will become sexier and have more functionality
3. We’ll see high-quality, aspirational, video-based imagery on boring sites

That may be true, but B2B is going to follow B2C in more important ways.

The entire Go-To-Market motion will change to look more like B2C.

Here are 5 things that B2C has nailed that are inevitable in B2B:

1. More influencers

I’m living it myself. If you’re not selling sales tech, it will look different than how I’m doing it, but no matter what you're selling, the power of the influencer is undeniable. Influencers wield power over B2B buyers just as they do in B2C.

2. More UGC

My favorite DTC example is Ryan Babenzian and Jolie, who sells a shower head. 0-$50m revenue in 18 months with 3 employees. The secret? They harnessed  TikTok influencers better than anyone. 30k pieces of UGC created in 2023 for $0.

3. Less friction

DTC loves making it easy to try something. To make a tiny initial sale and once there is a deep understanding of LTV to widen the purchaser pool.

As outbound response rates plummet in B2B, companies will do anything to get people into the top of the funnel, including making some (or all) of your product free.

4. Leaner teams

At Retention.com, we sell into the the Shopify ecosystem. They are some of the best entrepreneurs in the world. It’s SO much harder to create and ship physical goods than it is to build SaaS, and they are doing it on smaller margins.

In B2B, as CAC keeps going up and LTV going down, doing more with less will become essential. A natural consequence will be leaner teams.

5. More brand investment

The Shopify stores learned long ago that anything can (and will) be made in China by the factory that is making your stuff, and sold for 10% of your price. The only protection you have in the marketplace is your brand.

AI is going to create a similar dynamic in SaaS.

Anything you create that works will be built by 1,000 other people overnight.

What’s the only way to win in that environment?

It’s all about building a brand.

Creating trust and affinity, and selling products into it.

TAKEAWAY:

Everybody says if you want to see the future of B2B, look at B2C.

I think they're talking about sexier websites, cooler UX, aspirational imagery.

To me, B2B will look like B2C for different reasons…

Outbound is dying, SaaS is being commoditized, CAC is rising, and LTV is shrinking.

We’re going to have discipline forced on us the way it was forced on the Shopify ecosystem long ago.

That sounds bleak, but it’s not.

Early adopters of this playbook will grow faster than ever.

Don’t be left behind.
