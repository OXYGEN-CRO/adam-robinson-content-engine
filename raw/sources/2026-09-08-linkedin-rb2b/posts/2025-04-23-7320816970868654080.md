## Exact post text

Last month I spent $30,000 to share a room on Sir Richard Branson’s private Caribbean island. No oceanfront villa. No private suite. Just a twin bed… in a billionaire’s house. And the craziest part? I thanked him for it. Here’s how Branson turned Necker Island into the best “community business” I've seen:

1. Make a personal paradise that is so beautiful and inspiring that people are blown away by it.

- The topography, balinese architecture, chic interiors, views, food, service, and every water sport imaginable are this man’s paradise
- (Richard Branson’s paradise is paradise is ALSO your paradise)
- It’s also his home… And he and his staff makes people feel like they are guests at his home
- He has lemurs and baby kangaroos that I got to bottle feed, and over 3,000 flamingos
- You can drive golf balls into the ocean or sit in a hot tub on top of the great house, which is the highest point on the island

2. Make it so expensive that inspiring groups are the only people likely to go there (and so expensive that it funds this paradise).

- At $150k/night for 48 people max, I believe most people find themselves there with a group of very interesting people doing very interesting things
- I was invited by Brett Hurt, and we attended Alexander McCobin’s Principled Business Summit, put on by his fund, Liberty Ventures
- I met people doing all sorts of wild things like making tunnels with plasma guns, cross border payments for agriculture, building up a TopGolf empire
- I also got to have great conversations with John Mackey (founder of Whole Foods) and Brock Pierce (a true crypto OG who was literally involved in every single project) … Is anybody aware that Tether (he co-founded) generated $13b of profit in 2024 with 50 employees?!?!

3. Physically BE there - and ACT like you’re part of the group (even though you’re clearly not).

- Richard was on the dock when we arrived and pulled us off the boat from Tortola
- The second morning itinerary started with “Optional Hike with Richard Branson”
- He spent time sharing his epic stories - hot air balloon near death experiences, ALMOST avoiding the first gulf war entirely, virgin galactic and the dream of space
- He played chess during lunch with whoever wanted to take him on

TAKEAWAY

Sir Richard Branson has created a personal paradise. 

Not only that… 

People line up to pay him insane amounts of money to visit…  

And those people are the very people he’s interested in meeting anyway!

I don’t think I’ve ever seen a more incredible example of a community business.
