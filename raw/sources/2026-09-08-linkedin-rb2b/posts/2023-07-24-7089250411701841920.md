## Exact post text

Last year I was in Aspen with a startup CFO friend who ended up selling her fintech company for $250M after 7 years of hard work.

As part of the deal, she got a cushy desk job with 3x the salary she had before and a guaranteed 3 year deal with rolling equity.

She had made enough money to retire. 

More than most will ever see.

She was *literally* set for life.

When she told me all this I was expecting her to be ecstatic.

Being THAT cash rich is what we all strive for, right?

Wrong.

Turns out, the exact opposite was true. 

She was bored, burned out and in a post-exit depression that I often hear about.

What are the lessons here?

I’m not sure I have all the answers. 

But it does confirm a feeling I’ve had for a while...

That if I sold Retention.com tomorrow for $1 Billion… 

I'd probably end up deeply depressed.

Because startups aren't just about BIG exits.

Building a startup is about the journey.	

And the struggles that come along with it.

It gives life purpose.

So don't forget...

No matter how rough it gets out there.

Enjoy the ride while it lasts.
