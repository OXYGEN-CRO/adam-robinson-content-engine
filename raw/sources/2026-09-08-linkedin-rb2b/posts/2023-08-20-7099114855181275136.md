## Exact post text

99% of startups brag about scaling headcount.

Instead, they should brag about revenue per employee:

At Retention.com we learned this the hard way. Here's the story:

Last December I decided we were going to “go for it”. 

We identified an ICP (large Shopify stores) and wanted to grab market share as quickly as possible.

At the time, we had 6 employees, and $500-$700k free cash flow. 

So, we had lots of money to spend.

We hired 50 people in 60 days. 

Everything was great for a while, but then we started having problems with communication, people issues, and above all, we weren’t going faster even though we had so much more manpower.

Not only that - the ferocity (and desperation) with which we grew them team gave the impression that we were trying to spend as much $$ as possible to grow the business. 

This is a catastrophic position to put yourself in as a Founder/CEO. 

I felt it every day, but I didn’t know what to do about it.

There was no obvious path to getting smaller again.

A month ago, we reach a turning point.

Our VP of Sales resigned.

So, we had a choice to make.

We could replace him and keep the whole team...

Or we could downsize and have our CRO assume the VP of Sales role. 

We decided to get smaller.

Now, 8 months later, I have the answer.

Here are 3 lessons on how to run a hyper-lean, efficient business

1. Be the “most lean” version of whatever state you are in

- Obviously as you get better you need more people, processes
- There is a “lean” and a “bloated” way to do this
- Fire unreasonably fast, and wait unreasonably long  to hire

2. Always make money, and have a culture of frugality

- Hold everyone accountable for generating return on money spent
- Have people optimize for profit, in addition to growth
- Things can get out of control quickly if you aren’t running a tight ship
- When you have a business that makes lots of profit, you win no matter what

3. Don’t let yourself get desperate for growth. FOMO is an illusion

- Calm and measured decisions always have better long-term results
- It’s better for everybody to get there slowly than to go too fast and have to cut
- It’s easy to get caught up in the excitement of super high growth. Don’t!

Stop bragging about scaling headcount. 

It's a vanity metric.

Start bragging about revenue per employee.
