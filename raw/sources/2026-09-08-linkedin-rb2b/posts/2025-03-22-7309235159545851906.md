## Exact post text

The Two Opposing LinkedIn Audience Building Strategies (explained in 60 seconds):

THEIR WAY: 

- Create posts around generic topics that LinkedIn loves…
- Work-life balance, hiring, mental health, LinkedIn leadgen, AI, checklists
- Ghostwriters are used (most of the time)
- Join engagement circles or hire engagement service to “get in feeds” 
- Post viral content over meaningful content (think memes)
- Have no intention about who the actual members of your audience are
- Do not reply to comments on your own post
- Stay away from work in public, share only wins but not losses
- Use someone else or AI to reply to other peoples’ posts
- Do not analyze quality of your own audience
- The goal is to grow followers to the highest number possible

OUR WAY: 

- Create the best hook possible for that given post
- Write every line so that the reader will be compelled to continue
- Wrap up the post with something coy to create a dopamine hit
- Zero involvement in engagement pods or artificial post inflation
- Affinity is built through true thought leadership and meaningful content
- Know exactly which audience each post will reach (Founder, Sales, Marketing)
- Create talking head videos for most posts to build a stronger connection
- Be wildly transparent about the journey, share numbers and mistakes
- Answer every single comment yourself
- Have high percentage of your audience in the ICP (75% for me)
- Goal is to drive quantifiable sales outcomes (booked demos, signups)

TAKEAWAY:

Agencies tell you they can ghostwrite and grow your followers with just 1 hour of your time a week. 

They can, and they will. 

It’s one way to start building your brand, and you can see SOME benefit.

Here’s the problem... 

The best case outcome is that you’re “well known”. 

I believe the power of LinkedIn is the opposite. 

If you create the best content, and figure out how to reach your ICP, you can become “known well”, which is a far more powerful level of trust.

Trust = brand. 

In 2025, brand is built one post (and one comment) at a time.
