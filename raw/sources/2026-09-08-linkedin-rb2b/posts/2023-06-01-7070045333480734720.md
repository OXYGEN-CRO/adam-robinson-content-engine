## Exact post text

Confession: we got absolutely crushed in April.

I talk a lot about everything we do at Retention.com.

I talk about when we crush our goals and surprise ourselves, but I should probably talk more about things we’ve done that aren’t ideal.

It paints a more realistic picture of what growing a business looks like.

It’s not always sunshine and rainbows.

Before this past period, we were crushing.

A $1M ARR week, we were growing super fast, we hit all the targets, etc.

But that was short-lived.

We recently hit a wall.

We stalled completely.

We were hitting targets at 50% of what they should’ve been.

We tried a price experiment that took our average contract value from $30k to $12k…

Our top-of-funnel tracking got scrambled, so we have no idea what’s happening.

And lastly…

I got hit with a random tax bill for $300k while in a break-even period.

It would be easy for me to be down in the dumps. But I can’t do that.

We’ve got mouths to feed.

So here’s what I’m doing in light of this “L” to fix this mess and learn from my mistakes.

1. Roll back pricing.

The pricing experiment was a failure. We tried to raise prices because we felt like we were undercharging…

But you shouldn’t mess with what’s working.

We’re going to go back to what our prices originally were, because it’s just such a no-brainer for the customer.

Our most stacked plan is still cheaper than our main competitor’s entry-level plan.

The way we have it currently is way more approachable. We’re not gonna mess with it.

2. Add. More. Value.

We’re probably leaving 60% of the value on the table of what we can offer to merchants.

We need to do SMS.

We need to do back-in-stock flows and price-drop flows.

We’re building out integrations to other apps that will make the experience better for the customer.

It’ll just continue to add more buzz to what we’re doing and demonstrate just how much of a necessity our product is.

3. Collect more data.

For the points mentioned above, we want to get a ton of data about exactly how much value we can add with SMS, back-in-stock, and price-drop flows.

Get solid numbers on what our TAM is, and how much revenue we can drive for the companies we work with.

4. Be grateful.

I need to remind myself sometimes that the problems we experience as a company are good problems to have.

A year ago, I would’ve killed to be in this position.

Regardless, this is still a ride I wanna be on. It doesn’t matter what happens in the day-to-day, I’m so grateful to be working on this company with some wonderful and talented people.

I wouldn’t have it any other way.

As a CEO, your job is to solve problems quickly.

Unfortunately, you don’t have time to sit around and feel sorry for yourself.

Not if you have a team that’s relying on you, and a mission to conquer.

Nowhere to go but up.

In 3 months, you’ll forget all about these problems as long as you take action on them today.

Thanks for reading.
