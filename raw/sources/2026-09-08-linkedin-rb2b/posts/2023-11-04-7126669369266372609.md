## Exact post text

We used to spend $875,000 every year on our BDR team. Here's the uncomfortable truth about that rate of spend:

BDR teams have stopped working.

Our outbound sales performance plummeted over the last 12 months.

Just like everyone else’s.

Why?

Because buyers’ inboxes are ruined

99% of tech companies:

- Use the same automation tools 
- Send the same email cadences
- Contract the same data vendors 
- Message the same prospects

Which is bad, but...

Then AI tools came and made it 100x worse.

Here's the “old way” of running a sales organization:

- Pick a revenue target for the end of the year
- Model out how much manpower it’s going to take to get you there
- Hire, hire, hire, and then... hire some more

Retention.com was guilty of this.

Then our VP of Sales resigned. 

Of course, it was a surprise at first.

But it caused me to rethink how we run our business. 

I had read a few posts from Sam Jacobs about the “new way” of running sales.

Where $30M ARR companies were doing MORE sales with 2 reps than 25.

I spoke to founder friends and investors who were seeing the same.

So we shrunk our sales team from 14 to 4, eliminated the entire BDR team, had our CRO become VP of Sales, and decided we were going to create one of these “new way” super-light, super-lean sales machines.

What’s shocking about it is that we’re better on every metric than we were before we made the move.

Here’s what we did after we downsized to generate pipeline:

1. We DRAMATICALLY stepped up the Founder Brand content

- I used a ghostwriter which was building an audience, but not generating leads
- I now use a consultant, which is more work, but building the right audience
- I use some templates, but lots of vulnerable stories and personal experience
- We did a targeted connection request campaign to get the audience going

2. Grew an overseas team doing “spray and pray” cold email

- We build lists using tech lists on BuiltWith and have the team find the email
- We have the team manually send emails from multiple inboxes on our Gmail
- I don’t even think about this as direct-response… It’s more just branding, like an “ad unit” in inbox

3. Created more content and hosting events that cuts through.

- We’re making a super-high-quality docuseries called “Billion Dollar Challenge”
- We’re recording podcasts to write Linkedin posts that we know do well
- We’re targeting guests who are big names for our target audience
- We're hosting large and small events for our ICP and influencers

This isn’t predictable, but it’s creating 3x the pipeline with 20% of the sales org we had 120 days ago.

Will this work for everyone?

Probably not.

But the next generation of unicorns will look more like us than Salesforce.

Running as lean as possible. 

And leading with great content.
