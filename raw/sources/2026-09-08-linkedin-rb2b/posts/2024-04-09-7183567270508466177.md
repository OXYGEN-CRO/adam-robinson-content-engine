## Exact post text

Last year I spent $100,000 on Data Privacy lawyers. Today I want to clarify my thoughts on Data Privacy for the angry CISOs and B2B Marketers who keep showing up in my LinkedIn comments:

1. A consensus view does not exist (despite what the loudest activists think)

There is a group of cybersecurity professionals who believe that there is a consensus that Data Privacy is unambiguous and clearly expressed in the GDPR. If you suggest that there are people in the world who believe differently, they say you are a dumb SaaS Bro and a sociopath (!).

2. There is no moral obligation for an American company to adhere to GDPR

I live in the US, vote in the US, and pay US taxes. I comply with US law, so do my customers. Many CISOs believe that I should follow regulation by other countries, instead of regulation in the country I live in. I disagree.

3. People (incorrectly) think the US is headed towards GDPR

State-level regulation is fast-moving and confusing. There is no question that regulation is tightening in the US, as it should. What I advocate for (and what is playing out in California) is disclosure, consent (through cookie banners with conspicuous statements), and opt-out. That’s very different from GDPR, which is a 1st party opt-in for data collection. The US is not currently headed in that direction.

4. Cookie banners are our friends

All anybody is asking for is disclosure, consent capture, and opt-out. The cookie banner, if the right language is used, can achieve all three. That will all but eliminate legal risk not only for our tech, but all types of pixel retargeting. US regulators aren’t trying to stop these practices - they are trying to make the consumer aware and give them an opt-out.

5. A website visit isn’t that special. 

If I have BuiltWith alert me when a competitor script goes on my site (behavior) and look in Apollo for the CMO’s contact info (PII) then connect with them on LinkedIn and send them an email (action), I don’t see the major difference between a website visit (behavior) and us providing the website owner the CMO’s contact info from Apollo’s API (PII), then the site owner connecting on LinkedIn and sending an email (action). 

If anything, we enable more targeted outreach than today’s accepted standard.

TAKEAWAY:

Some think every word of the GDPR is a human right. 

Other's believe the natural outcome of digital life is being tracked.

There’s one thing I know for sure. 

If you’re a company in the US, if you simply:

- Implement a cookie banner
- Have the proper language
- Provide a conspicuous opt-out

You lower your legal risk of using our tech (and others) to damn near zero. 

But don’t say that on LinkedIn… 

You might just get your teeth knocked out!

P.S.

*I am not a lawyer. This is not legal advice.
