## Exact post text

I bootstrapped my startup to $22m ARR in 4 years. People tell me they would LOVE to bootstrap rather than take VC money, but they don’t know how. If you're at $0 MRR, here are 4 ways you can fund your startup:

1. EXCLUSIVELY Friends and Family.

The key here is to not let any institutional money into your angel round, and explain that you are intending to grow a solid, profitable software company. Go to the people who would fund a restaurant if you opened one. People who are betting on YOU as a person, not the idea or company.

2. Start a consultancy or agency to fund your SaaS.

SaaS is hard because you (usually) have to start at SMB and work your way up over time. It takes a LOT of $15/mo payments to pay salaries. Small consultancies or agencies are much easier to get to cash positive and will help you acquire some of the basic skills you need. 

Mailchimp, my favorite SaaS ever, started as an agency. Nathan Barry funded ConvertKit by selling eBooks. Russel Brunson funded Clickfunnels with InfoProducts. The list goes on.

3. Keep your job.

This is a little Gary Vee of me, but you can work nights and weekends to get an MVP and some initial customers off the ground. I would recommend not cutting off your income and going all-in on something until you see signs of product-market fit. Remember, it will take longer than you think!

4. Fund it with your (or your rich spouse’s) savings.

I happened to save a couple million bucks in my 20’s trading credit default swaps at Lehman Brothers. I realize that this didn’t happen to most people, but some people do have rich family members!

🚨 A word of warning... This is the MOST dangerous option, because however much money is available for a startup will get consumed by the startup (and more). If you are a first timer, there will likely be little to show for it in the end.

TAKEAWAY:

Building a SaaS startup is HARD.

It’s better to start your career as an entrepreneur selling something else.

Services and info products can help you hone basic skills like providing and charging for services, keeping customers happy, and collecting payment.

Once you crush another space, it may be time to turn to SaaS.

When you do, I hope you remember you wanted to bootstrap.

Because once you get on that VC treadmill, there is no getting off.

Kiss profitable growth goodbye.
