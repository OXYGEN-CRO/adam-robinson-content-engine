## Exact post text

Most marketers would say I’m an idiot, but I don't believe in attribution. It forces you to focus on the WRONG things. Here's 5 reasons why I don’t measure any marketing activity I spend time on:

1. The most important things in business can’t be measured

The two things that come to mind when I say this are “brand” and “demand creation”.

I can’t measure the direct effect that ANYTHING I spend 80% of my time on has on RB2B. I have no idea how many signups came from this post, and I don’t know what the 3 guest slots I did today produced.

Yet, I know it’s working better than anything I’ve done before, but if I were optimizing for things I could see with my own eyes (beyond MRR), my days would look totally different.

2. The least important parts of business are what you CAN measure

You can only measure demand CAPTURE - when someone actually clicked on an ad, booked a demo, hit your site from google, or responded to a cold email or LinkedIn DM.

3. Once you can measure something, you start optimizing for it and scaling it

How do we respond to #2? More paid ads, SEO, cold email, and LinkedIn messages, which are only CAPTURING demand, but not creating it.

Executive teams don’t have the patience to scale demand creation because 6 months in the CEO asks how many leads we got from the podcast (or organic social). When the response is “I don’t know”, the budget goes to paid search.

4. Tech platforms take credit for things that were going to happen anyway

This bothers me more than anything else. So many of our customers have made up their minds to buy before they were shown a retargeting ad.

But, Linkedin still claims the retargeting conversion, but the buyer came from listening to me on someone’s podcast, or from reading my posts.

Am I supposed to believe that our ROAS is positive? I’m really not sure.

5. Measuring leads to models in a spreadsheet that almost never happen

“If we hire X people in Y department, we’ll grow by Z.”

I've made this rookie mistake and I think most CEOs are right there with me.

I believe it never works because we don’t appreciate how important the unmeasurable, TRUE components of business growth like Brand and Demand Creation activities are. They don’t fit anywhere on that spreadsheet.

TAKEAWAY:

It’s 2024.

As outbound continues to die, the unmeasurable becomes ever more important.

The quickest way to grow a SaaS is by building a brand.

The best way to scale a brand is by building a personal (not company) profile.

Demand today is actually created through human-to-human connection.

Social media platforms facilitate this connection at a massive scale.

I’m creating human-to-human connection by:

1. Spending tons of time pouring my thoughts (and heart) out on Linkedin
2. Doing as many podcast guest slots as I possibly can 
3. Responding to every SINGLE comment someone leaves me

But I can’t measure what any of it is doing to our business.

Except for the most important measure of all…

ARR…

Up-and-to-the-right!
