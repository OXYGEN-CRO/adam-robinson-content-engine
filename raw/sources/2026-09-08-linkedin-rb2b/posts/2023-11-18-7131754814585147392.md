## Exact post text

Over the last two weeks I’ve spoken with 25+ founders, marketers and sales leaders about 6Sense and Demandbase. Why?

Because Retention.com is about to launch a new product into the ultra-competitive and noisy $500M+ intent data market. Here's what I learned (and why founders must do their own research):

To set the stage:

- We are creating a “person-level identity” product for B2B SaaS companies
- Rather than account/company level ID, which is standard today
- We are using the same core identity tech we sell to ecomm
- We are going to build the entire business in public

Here are the 6 biggest things I learned from my deep-dive:

1. People actually DO want to know the individual website visitor (rather than the company)

- 6Sense and Demandbase give you a company and geolocation, then guess
- This isn’t actually that valuable, especially when selling to enterprise

2. 6Sense is pivoting towards pushing an easier-to-use tool for sales teams, Demandbase is staying true to their original tool

3. I never heard that the ABM/advertising part of the platform was a resounding “slam dunk”

- This could have been adverse selection… 
- Maybe I just got people who wanted to vent?

4. The value of 3rd party intent data varies wildly by use case.

- Is someone reading an article that mentions Apollo.io really in market for a sales intelligence tool?

5. False positives from intent data can be a major problem for high-ACV SaaS companies.

- If you’re playing a low-volume, high-ACV game, you can burn accounts by putting them into an ABM campaign based on a website visit that didn’t happen

6. There is demand from salespeople/growth hackers at smaller companies (sub-50 employees) and marketers/rev ops at larger ones (250-750 employees).

- I also heard many times that marketing will have the budget for this, even if sales has to implement it

TAKEAWAY:

Launching a new product into a new market the RIGHT way is a ton of work.

My calendar is so full when I look at it I want to vomit. 🤮

What I thought our MVP (minimum viable product) was has changed once a week for the last two months.

Who I think our ICP is -- meaning what size company and what department we’re going to try to sell into -- has changed three times in the last two weeks.

The thing that keeps me going is this: 

1. My current business is $21M ARR after 4 years
2. It didn’t have 1/10 of what this had going for it when we started
3. Our team has learned so much building that business
4. I am so much better as an entrepreneur
5. I think this product has the potential to be so, so, so much bigger

And of course, for those who follow me...

I’m going to be building the entire thing in public.

With radical transparency, right here on LinkedIn.

Stay tuned.

P.S.

If you're a revenue leader, tell me...

If you could get the Linkedin URLs of 30% of your anonymous website visitors (and what pages they viewed), would that be valuable?

We're looking for some beta testers.

DM me if you want to try it.
