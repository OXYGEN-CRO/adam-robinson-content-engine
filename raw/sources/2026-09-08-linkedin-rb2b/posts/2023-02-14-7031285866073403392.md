## Exact post text

You might not know this about me, but I worked as a trader on Wall Street for 10 years…

Somebody recently asked me what I learned during that time that’s applicable to what I do now as an entrepreneur. Here are 5 lessons I still use today as I scale to $50M ARR:

1) The relationship between fear and greed.

As Warren Buffet says, “Be greedy when others are fearful and fearful when others are greedy.”

2) It’s a waste of time to try and outperform the market.

Betting on individual stocks is a huge distraction and a waste of my mental energy. I primarily only buy indices now.

3) How to talk like an adult and act like a professional.

This is super valuable and something I wish I learned at a younger age.

4) I acquired insane Excel skills. Seriously.

It seems silly — but those skills have allowed me to convince incredibly smart people to give me insane amounts of money.

5) Money ain't a thing! It comes and it goes.

On Wall Street, I went through a ton of P&L volatility, and it increased my risk tolerance.

As I build and invest in this company, not everything is going to go as planned, but I know I’ll be fine in the end.

And there’s one more thing. Doing this thought exercise made me realize that there was a lot of value in working a ‘regular’ job before jumping into startup land.

Social media makes you feel like you need to go ‘all in’ as soon as you graduate college (or drop out).

Sure. That might work for some folks. But it wasn’t my jam.

And I say my approach worked out just fine 🙂

PS - if you want to watch me scale Retention.com to a $1B valuation — follow along ✅
