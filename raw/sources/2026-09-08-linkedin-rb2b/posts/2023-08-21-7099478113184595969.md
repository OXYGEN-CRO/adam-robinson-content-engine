## Exact post text

2 years ago, GetEmails was stuck at $8M ARR and I was burnt out.

But now, we’re at $20M, on the path to $50M ARR.

Here’s *exactly* what I did to turn things around:

First, I hired Santosh Sharan.

He came on after having built multiple HUGE data companies.

He was the catalyst.

Within 2 months, we were selling all annual deals at high velocity.

Here's a few of the changes we made:

- We rebranded to Retention.com (this was BIG)
- We built out an influencer/affiliate channel
- We build out an agency partnership team
- We built a more robust product suite
- We built out an events channel
- We built out a BDR function
- We only did demos for ICP

Need I go on?

“What got you here, won’t get you there.”

That's one of my favorite quotes.

$50M ARR is solid goal, but...

In order to hit it, we need to have our sights on $250M.

Here are 3 tangible lessons for startup founders from our journey so far:


Lesson 1: What got you here, won’t get you there

I mentioned that already, but it's worth repeating

Newer entrepreneurs don’t believe this. 

They think that this only applies to *some* companies.

I am telling you: it applies to everyone.

Certain channels will only work up to a certain threshold.

Same with talent. Same with products.

You need to keep iterating if you want to grow.


Lesson 2: Think WAY ahead.

I’m talking years down the line.

Not just about how to solve problems that’ll arise a week or a month from now.

You ever wonder why people describe entrepreneurs as having “great vision”?

It doesn’t mean that they’re visionaries, even though some are.

It means they can literally see into the future.

To see what’ll work and what’ll be relevant.

Do your best to think about what you need to do way down the line.

Prepare past the next two quarters.


Lesson 3:  Stack S-curves.

An S curve is exactly what it looks like.

You bang your head against the wall for months and months, and then you hit a curve. You grow like crazy for a few months, and then you stagnate.

Newer entrepreneurs think that when this crazy growth happens, it’ll keep going forever.

And then it throws you into a depressive episode when you stagnate.

Guys like Santosh have seen this time and time again, and he knows that every growth spike is temporary.

You need to be working on your NEXT S-curve while you’re in the growth phase of your current S-curve so that the stagnation is temporary.

Stacking S-curves means you’re having a crazy growth event more frequently than if you just had one.

By the time the first S-curve flattens out, the next S-curve will be doing its thing and keeping you growing.

That makes a hockey stick.

Vertical growth.

For us to achieve this, there are 2 options:

We can either add more features for the same users…

Or keep the same features for new users and a different audience. 

This usually means going up-market.

P.S.

We're quietly rolling out a product for B2B sales team. 

If you want to try the beta, DM me.
