## Exact post text

When scaling a startup, every CEO should focus on 3 things (and it's not revenue, churn, or hiring great talent):

BACKGROUND:

Over the past 10 years, I read "The Great CEO Within" by Matt Mochary 4 times.

Each time I was at a different part of the scaling journey, so each time something different resonated with me.

When I read it recently, one idea stood out WAY more than it had in the past …

The idea that the *emotional burden* of scaling a startup is substantial.

And it's not fair to put the weight of that burden on your family.

You need a support system to deal with it along the way.

That's why I think every CEO scaling a startup should do 3 things:

1. Get a therapist

I finally found a therapist and now we meet regularly. I have been seeing her regularly, and interestingly, my wife thinks I’m less of an as*hole now. Every week my therapist makes me “bring something in” to talk about, and that alone is making me more mindful of the little things on a day to day basis.

2. Get a mentor or coach

Santosh Sharan has served as my main mentor figure for the past 20 months. He was a huge help with some glaringly obvious shortcomings I had around instilling processes.

A while back, I was explaining to Brian Lee🏌🏻 that I was looking for a mentor or coach, and he connected me to Matt Blumberg, who found Jonathan Shapiro. Jon worked with 8 of our executives, and we've rolled out Entrepreneur Operating System. I’m VERY happy I pursued the mentorship path.

3. Get a network of peers.

I was really sold on the idea of meeting with a group of CEO peers regularly. One of my friends joined YPO and said the forum was excellent, so I applied, went through that brutal screening process, and didn’t get in!

I then decided to join Pavilion and Sam Jacobs' CEO group. It’s definitely doing what I was looking for - a group of people at a similar stage to me experiencing a similar pain in a slightly different way.

TAKEAWAY:

Startups are hard.

Maintaining good mental health throughout the journey is even harder.

It’s not fair for your significant other to bear the burden.

I found a therapist, a mentor, and a group of peers to help me along the way.

Being a CEO can be pretty lonely.

But it doesn’t have to be.
