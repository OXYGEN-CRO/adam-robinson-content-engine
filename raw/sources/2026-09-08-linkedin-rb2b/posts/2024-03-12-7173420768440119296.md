## Exact post text

I bootstrapped 3 startups in 13 years. Here are the 3 BIGGEST failures of my startup career:

1. “Robly Authorized Partners” (2017)

My first startup Robly was email marketing software. 

We were stuck at $3m ARR for 5 year and I had no idea what to do. 

Our original GTM motion was marketing to Constant Contact customers. 

In 2017, Constant Contact was acquired by Endurance and they killed a boots-on-the-ground program they invested 10’s of millions of dollars in called their Authorized Local Expert program shortly thereafter. 

Soon after, one of the senior leaders reached out to me with an idea.

We attempted to absorb the program and failed miserably. 

It turns out it was a great program as a branding play for a company as large as Constant Contact, but didn’t make sense from a unit economics perspective for a company as tiny as ours. 

To add insult to injury, I got sued for hiring the former leader during his non-compete. 

2. Lead(.)com (2018)

I hired my now co-founder Diana Ross to do some consulting for me to figure out how I might get my $3m ARR SaaS un-stuck. 

She believed that there was space above Mailchimp and below Salesforce Marketing Cloud to create and market a mid-market Email Newsletter app. We spent a year building it, and I did a deal with a friend in the domain space to lease the domain Lead(.)com. 

We were going to “launch” at the Traffic & Conversions Summit in 2018, having almost finished the platform. We pulled a big stunt that involved promo girls in Lead(.)com clothes walking around to everybody at the conference (a mostly male demographic) asking them, “How big is your list?” 

No one cared. 

I was devastated, having wasted a year and a million bucks of salaries building software that we literally just threw away.

3. Retention.com and the “Billion Dollar Challenge” (2023)

In late 2022, we honed in on our ICP. We started scaling up our go-to-market engine and everything was flying, WAY faster than expected. I was confident that we had a unicorn on our hands. 

So confident that I made a 2 season docuseries called “Billion Dollar Challenge”, declaring that we would hit $50m ARR (up from $15M) by the end of the year. 

We oversold, over-hired, and our churn ended up being WAY worse than I thought. Instead of $50m ARR, we ended up hitting $22m.

You may say that growing from $15-$22m ARR (bootstrapped) is still really good, especially in 2023, and you would be right, however...

When you publicly call your shot at $50m…

And you confidently make a docuseries about the journey… 

And you end up with UNDER HALF the ARR… 

That’s an epic fail. 

But, you know what they say. 

What doesn’t kill you makes you stronger.

Keep building.
