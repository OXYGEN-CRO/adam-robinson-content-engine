## Exact post text

We just spent $150k to fly 38 employees out to NYC for a 72 hour AI hackathon. Why? Because I’m terrified the AI-native 2027 version of Retention.com ($20m ARR, 35 FTE) looks nothing like the business today and if I don't build it we're screwed.

As Diane from ApplyAI so elegantly put it: 

“If you do not build the AI-native version of your business, some young, hungry founder will… and they are out there trying as we speak.” 

Yikes.

———

I believe all mature SaaS with people and processes have to completely transform themselves, else they will die a slow death (as MANY SaaS are right now, some know it, some don’t).

I also believe the effort has to be grassroots. The totalitarian approach won’t work because people at the top are out of touch with the actual work that gets done. 

I thought going into this that a year ago most of our team probably wasn’t using AI for anything. 

By mid-2025 they had become fluent with ChatGPT, but most weren’t building agents to automate repetitive work. 

And most of us are unaware of what’s possible… me included. 

I’m not sure exactly how long we all have, or if there is a definitive moment where if you haven’t achieved transformation you will suddenly implode. 

The more likely scenario is the mature companies stall-out and enter a slow melt phase, while super-lean, AI-native startups solving overlapping problems flatten every market with point solutions that collectively solve the incumbents whole problem for 1/50th of the cost (and 1/1000th of the team).

———

How do you level up your team? 

I’ll tell you how we’re trying to do it. 

BEGINNER PHASE: 

- Understanding how to prompt effectively and the contrast between good and bad prompting
- Familiarity with types of work LLMs are well suited for and what they’re not
- The limitations of LLMs, how confidently they can be wrong, how to check them

INTERMEDIATE PHASE: 

- Time audits identifying work that is both repetitive and aligns well with LLMs strength
- Familiarizing team w/ workflow builder that meets their technical appetite (zapier/n8n)
- Getting them comfortable trading imperfection for 100x efficiency

ADVANCED PHASE: 

- The design (but not necessarily development) of increasingly complex workflows
- Every task and process gets the same scrutiny - “can AI handle this”
- All roles evolve from doing things to monitoring and improving machines

Most people get tripped up between intermediate and advanced because they go down rabbit holes of a 20 part course on how to use n8n rather than understanding what’s possible, designing, and getting someone else to build.

———

TAKEAWAY

If I don’t build the 2027 AI-native version of my business, I’m screwed. 

If you don’t do the same with yours, you’re screwed too. 

What’s the only difference between you and me? 

I realize there’s a dozen YC kids working 9/9/6 trying to eat my lunch. 

Do you?
