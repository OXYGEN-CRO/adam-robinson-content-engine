## Exact post text

For 7 months, my Glassdoor approval rating has been under 40%. Our company is rated 2.3 out of 5 stars. So last week I finally sat down and read them. Against advice of counsel, I recorded the painful experience. Here's why:

To set the stage: 

1. In the summer of 2022, we had six employees and zero Glassdoor presence
2. We hired over 50 people in 60 days
3. It was a complete disaster, which culminated in our VP sales quitting
4. We downsized our sales org from 15 to 4
5. Our understandably upset former employees let us have it on Glassdoor
6. Those were our first reviews, with no positive reviews to offset them

CURRENT STATE:

My goal on LinkedIn is to show the emotional journey of being a founder.

Nobody has ever done it with the level of transparency I aim for:

Real financials, big wins, big losses, embarrassing moments, and set backs.

And this is my most transparent moment yet....

Despite some laughter out of disbelief, it was an incredibly painful experience. 

You might be asking yourself:

"Hey Adam, why I haven’t you read your Glassdoor reviews?"

The answer is simple:

I hate Glassdoor. 

We scaled up our sales org too fast, had to do a layoff, and we got blasted.

My People Ops team thinks managing your Glassdoor presence is inauthentic.

So we have refused to use the strategy most companies use which is "highly suggesting" their team and new employees leave glowing reviews. 

Would this balance out the bad ones and give a more representative picture of what it’s like to work at Retention.com?

Sure, but I don't want to play that game.

My team told me the substance of the reviews when they were posted.

Much of it we deserved, and what we deserved, we’ve taken action on.

Scaling a startup is hard.

Making the tough decisions required to run a great business, and knowing that you can’t please everybody, is one of the hardest parts.

Letting people go is always terrible.

It's difficult to make decisions that impact people's lives.

But as CEO, that's your job.

And when you make those decisions quickly you end up with an efficient, productive team… 

Which is what you need to survive in 2024.

Unfortunately, Glassdoor may destroy you for it.
