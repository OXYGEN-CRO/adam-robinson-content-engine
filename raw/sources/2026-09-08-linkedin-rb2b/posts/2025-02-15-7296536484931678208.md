## Exact post text

I binged 50 episodes of the Founders podcast. And I noticed something WILD… Every single world-changing founder—Disney, Jobs, Jordan, Napoleon—had the same 3 traits. The craziest part? Their lives were completely different, but the patterns never changed.

BACKGROUND: 

- Founders by David Senra is a podcast I discovered thanks to Brett Hurt
- Senra reads biographies of the most successful people in the world
- He condenses them to 1hr episodes and is a brilliant orator
- It’s not just business people… it’s artists, athletes, historical figures

So what do all of the most successful people in history have in common?

1. The all have an intense, lifelong focus on their craft.

David loves repeating that “people become known by the public for things they spent thousands of hours doing in private”. 

Michael Jordan said that everybody is fascinated by the shoe deals and commercials, but don’t realize that the reason all of that happened is because he worked harder than anyone he had ever met on the fundamentals his entire life.

2. They all have an undying commitment to excellence.

In a way, these biographies are simply collections of unique stories about lives dedicated to the pursuit of excellence. Steve Jobs was about tech and film, Michael Jordan was about basketball and fashion, Sam Walton was about retailing.

But what unified them was their extremely high bar for quality and their pursuit of excellence.

Steve Jobs said “Quality is so much better than Quantity. One home run is so much better than two doubles.”

3. They put the best teams around them they possibly could.

The emphasis on collecting “A players” and constantly trying to upgrade talent comes through in every episode. Especially in engineering - Steve Jobs said a great software engineer could be 100x more productive than a good one. 

TAKEAWAY

It has been years since I’ve been this addicted to a podcast. 

Here’s the crazy thing… 

I’ve listened to so many that they have started sounding the same. 

Each episode a totally different time, life, and set of circumstances.

But when it comes down to WHO the people are that have shaped our world…

1. They all had intense focus for decades (and loved the game)
2. They all had an absolutely psychotic commitment to excellence
3. They built the best teams they possibly could

This reminds me of an article I read about Nick Saban—legendary Alabama football coach—who has a statue in front of the stadium for winning a national championship (he won 7), along with five other coaches who won national championships.

Cedric Burns, who has been an assistant for 33 years, dropped some epic, generational wisdom that pretty much sums up this entire Linkedin post:

“There are two types of coaches at Alabama. 

Ones with statues, and there’s one without. 

The ones without statues are all different. 

But the ones with statues are all the same.”
