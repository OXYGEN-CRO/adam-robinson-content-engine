## Exact post text

Koala shut down and Unify raised $40m at $260m (on $5.6m ARR) in the SAME WEEK. Two opposite outcomes for two companies trying to solve the exact same problem. How could this happen? Here's one man’s opinion — who is also in the “signals” space 👇

You don’t shut your business down because you are frustrated with how fast it’s growing.

I don’t know the Koala team, nor exactly how they were doing. I can imagine they were struggling with a lot of the same issues we are at RB2B.

Here are 3 big reasons the signal space hasn’t lived up to the hype:

- For MOST B2B companies, there isn’t enough signal for meaningful pipeline
- You can’t just spam a website visitor and get anything back from it
- Once you combine OTHER signals with the site visit, the number of signals drops even more…

Which leads to incredibly high expectations coming from this inspiring narrative in an era where entire channels were being lost (and not replaced) and CAC is headed to the moon in all others.

Not to mention a handful of vendors who were NOT doing signal - ai copy generation, omnichannel outreach - all jumped in because the demand was so high…

All to converge with a very similar product offering solving a nearly identical problem, onboarding hopeful marketers whose expectations become shattered when they realize only ~10% of the expected juice came out of the lemon when they squeezed it.

A reality that is lower than expectations = churn.

Welcome to The Painful World of Signals that I know so well.

That ROUGHLY explains my hypothesis about Koala.

But what about Unify?

How does a 2.5yr old startup doing the exact same thing raise at 42x revenue?

I have a couple theories there, too.

My observation is that “signal stacking” crushes for sales-assisted PLG dev tools and security platforms … Explosive AI tools are the hero users.

1. OpenAI is an investor, which helped them bag other AI decacorns after PMF

2. Everyone wants to be AI decacorns (perplexity, cursor), insane social proof dynamic

3. Late stage venture SaaS have high traffic & growth teams that can make this type of thing work (justworks, sofi)

These three factors - all relatively large deals - plus a semi-credible AI narrative are showing high early growth rates which VCs can spin each other up about.

What are investors missing here?

1. They are so young that churn hasn’t actually happened yet

2. The TAM of the PLG dev/security space isn’t huge, rest of b2b is “meh” with this category

3. This product category doesn't inherently expand in state over time - NRRs will be HARD

To expand, Unify will need to add features to sell into other parts of the org. Signal activation isn’t like a CRM - it’s not a database that people are comfortable paying more for over time.

The biggest problem of all is that prospecting tools like Unify have a “reverse network effect”…

The more adoption they get, the less effective they will be.

Here's what I think two "heroic" outcomes look like 👇
