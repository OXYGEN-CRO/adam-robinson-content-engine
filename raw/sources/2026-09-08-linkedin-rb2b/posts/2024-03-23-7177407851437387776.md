## Exact post text

99% of startup founders THINK they know what their business is going to be when they start. They're almost always wrong. Why? Because human intuition is a terrible indicator of market viability. Here are 3 examples from my life:

1. Robly Email Marketing in 2014

What we thought it would be when it started: 

- Merchant-side customer review platform with email newsletter app for VSMB
- Inbound marketing strategy led by direct mail (this was completely insane)
- Value prop was strong enough to gain quick and large market share, 9 fig sale

What it ended up being: 

- Email Newsletter app ONLY (turned off the reviews app after 6mo)
- Stuck at $3m ARR for 3+ years
- Great lifestyle business, sold for $10m

2. GetEmails (now Retention.com) in 2019

What we thought it would be when we started:

- Thought large affiliate senders (credit card, solar, finance) would be market
- Thought 3rd party cookies were ending in 18 months, tech would die too
- Thought it would be a way to make a quick few million bucks and move on

What it ended up being: 

- Bootstrapped $22m ARR/$6-8m profit 4 years in
- Tech no longer uses 3rd party cookies (we help extend 1st party cookies)
- Have a killer team of 55 who is working on continuing to grow this fabulous business

3. RB2B in August 2023

What we thought it would be when we started:

- Person-Level Website Visitor Identity for mid-market B2B rev teams
- $30-50k ACV deals, entirely sales-led
- Goal was (relatively) low-churn, quick ARR

Where it is today: 

- Massive freemium offer (unlimited LI profiles to slack/teams $0)
- Underpriced, self-serve PLG ($495/mo, one plan, for all features/integrations)
- Goal is a massive user base, global domination play

TAKEAWAY:

In the startup game, you rarely end where you thought you would.

Why?

Because our gut instinct about products is (most of the time) pretty bad. 

Luckily there’s a proven and fairly simple way to build great products. 

1. Talk to prospects before you start building, make sure they want it
2. Build the smallest product that meets their needs to see if they use it
3. Keep talking and iterating until they have something they are raving about

In 2024, once you have word of mouth... 

Everything else becomes 10x easier.
