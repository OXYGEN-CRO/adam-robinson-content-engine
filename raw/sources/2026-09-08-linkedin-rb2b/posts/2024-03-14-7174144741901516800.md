## Exact post text

Last week Clark Barron wrote a hit piece about rb2b.com, making four serious accusations. Here's why he's wrong and my point-by-point response:

1. This is a wholesale assault on data privacy and will lead to a dystopian future

2. Our technology skirts existing privacy legislation in the US

3. That by operating exclusively in the US, we are doing something wrong by not subjecting our technology or its users to GDPR

4. I am preying on “SAAS bros that are too naive and dumb to have any ethics yet” who are “growth obsessed lunatics”

This is one side of the data privacy argument, and while I believe that they are untrue, I respect those who hold these beliefs.

Here are my responses:

1. This is a wholesale assault on data privacy. 

Google and Facebook know who is on your site - the exact individual - and charge you for the privilege of targeting them. Why should small business and startups not be able to make money off the same information? This is what our customers think. It’s hard for extreme privacy advocates to believe this, but it’s true.

2. Our technology skirts the existing privacy legislation in the US. 

This is factually untrue. Our technology is 100% compliant with all federal and state rules and regulations. Clark states that it’s “technically” legal in the us. That concept doesn’t exist. Either something is legal or it isn’t. I am a law abiding citizen, so are our customers. If this becomes illegal, we will stop doing it. If it were to become legal in Europe, we would do it there too. 

3. We are doing something wrong or exploiting a loophole by not operating in Europe.

We don’t operate in Europe because GDPR says that we cannot. If GDPR said we could, we would operate there. If the US, or a state, says we can’t do this, we will stop. This is not exploiting a loophole. We are operating legally in jurisdictions that permit it. 

4. I’m preying on “SaaS Bros” who are too stupid to know what I’m doing.

First of all, I am a SaaS bro. I created a product for myself. It’s valuable to me and my business. When it started working, we gave it to other people. They thought it was valuable also. Our customers aren’t naive. They just believe something different. This is a political debate, akin to many hot button issues in the US.

TAKEAWAY:

We sell polarizing technology. 

The very definition of “polarizing” is that it divides people into sharply contrasting sets of beliefs.

And like with anything polarizing, there is a loud vocal minority who disapprove of what we're doing.

That's OK.

Because one fact remains unchanged...

Our technology is 100% compliant.

The rest of the heated debate is just that... debate.

Which I welcome.

New tech is bound to attract detractors.

If you do not agree with me, I respect that. 

I’m not trying to change your mind. 

I just want you to know that many, many people disagree.

And that is their right.

Calling them idiots isn't going to win you any friends.
