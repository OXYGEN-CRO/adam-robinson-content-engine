## Exact post text

I’ve been selling software for a little while now.

I’ve sold $15 software, and I’ve sold $30,000 software.

For a myriad of reasons (some more obvious than others), I enjoy selling the $30,000 software much more than the former.

In the early days of Robly, we ran an experiment.

We had one price for the premium version of our email marketing software: $15 a month.

Most people will know how impossible it is to be profitable on $15 conversions.

Instead, we decided to have 3 price options instead of just one.

A $15 plan, a $22 plan, and a $60 plan.

We said that the $15 plan had limitations, the $22 plan was unlimited, and the $60 plan was white glove.

We also highlighted the $22 plan and said it was the “most popular” plan.

There were hardly any limitations with the $15 plan, and the $60 plan took hardly any time to quickly onboard people and provide some extra assistance.

Guess what happened?

Our conversion rate stayed the same…

65% picked the $22 plan…
20% picked the $15 plan…
And 15% picked the $60 plan.

We did nothing different other than make our current plan look cheap and less useful than two other plans that we pulled out of nowhere.

It took our average price per customer from $15 to $27. Up 75%.

This can be a bit trickier if you’re selling a DTC product — but if you’re in the B2B world, it’s probably a sign to raise your prices 😉
