## Exact post text

“It's never going to work”. That's what a billionaire CEO told me as I was explaining my vision for the next 24 months of my startup. And not only was he right, but it led to me getting sued.

BACKGROUND:

In the spring of 2017, I was sitting on the couch in my New York apartment, sending emails to wrap up my day.

My startup, Robly Email Marketing, had grown from $0-$3M ARR, but got stuck, and at this point, had been stuck for a year.

Looking for answers, I popped over to LinkedIn.

I saw a message that was so intriguing I read it twice.

An employee of the pioneer in Email Marketing messaged me…

This company HATED me because the way I got to $3M was by calling their customers and offering them a better deal.

What intrigued me so much about the message?

The end of the message was this:

“They put $50m into their in-person selling program, and when we got acquired they just chopped it. You’re the perfect guy to swoop in and take it over.”

This was it!

This was how I was going to get unstuck!

I agreed to wait until his non-compete expired and hire him.

___

A few days later, one of my mentors connected me with a billionaire CEO, who agreed to meet with me as a favor.

I was excited to bound my new idea off a legend.

But half way through our meet, the CEO stopped me.

“It's never going to work,” he said.

“Why not?” I asked, flustered.

“I’ll show you.”

He then started writing numbers on a legal pad.

“How many customers do these agencies have, on average, and how much would they charge?”

“Maybe 10-20, and they charge around $1-2k/mo” I said.

“And what does your software cost?”

“$60/mo,” I replied.

“And what affiliate commission do you pay?”

“40% for life.” I proudly said, because it was HIGHER than most.

The CEO laughed and shook his head.

“If you get 100% penetration into the agency’s accounts, you will make them a total of $24 for each account they bill $2k to their agency for. They aren’t going to care about you or your software.”

What did I do?

Did I listen to the most successful founder I had ever spoken to?

Of course not.

Not only did I proceed, I ended up hiring the guy early because the perfect time presented itself to strike, and “non-competes aren’t enforceable in California”.

Fast forward 12 months...

After the initial excitement, the program got zero traction.

Then the competitor sued me and dragged the lawsuit out for a year.

Eventually we settled, and the employee informed me that by California law, I was obligated to pay his legal fees too.

What a kick in the nuts.

TAKEAWAY:

Should I have listened to the billionaire CEO?

Maybe.

Maybe not.

I think many first-time founders fail because they haven’t learned what only being in the game can teach you.

Those hard lessons shape the founder you become.

Total failure, wasting a year of my life, burning several hundred thousand dollars, and a lawsuit were awful…

But I’m a stronger founder for having gone through it.

The key is living to fight another day.
