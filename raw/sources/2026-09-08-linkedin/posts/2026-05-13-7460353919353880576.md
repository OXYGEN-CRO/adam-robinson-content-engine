## Exact post text

Last week Recharge acquired Skio for $105m cash. This is an incredibly important acquisition for founders to pay attention to. Here's why:

What is not said in the headline “we only raised $8m and sold for $105m” is the little-known fact that the reason they only raised $8m was several years ago nobody would do a Series A for Skio. 

Which is why their founder Kennan Frost replaced himself as CEO and moved on to build Icon. 

Why is this important for Founders? 

1. Selling a business for $105m when you only raised $8m is LIFE CHANGING

2. For the same reason he couldn’t get the Series A done, I’m not sure a business like that (sticky but sells to small TAM of elite Shopify users at $500/mo) is saleable at $500m+. 

3. Had Kennan been “lucky enough” to get the Series A done and raised at $200-$250m valuation, the exit hurdle would have been raised to $600-750m

4. Recharge likely wouldn’t be a buyer there ($2.1b at absolute peak valuations), not sure if anybody would be … because if you’re buying Skio at $600-750, you think it’s going to $2b.

5. Skio - which would still be the same, GREAT business - would be stuck in a zombie state, pushing for a valuation that is larger than it was meant to be (like so many others).

Ryan Allis once said, “If you have to raise money, keep it at 1x your ARR at the time”. 

Why? Because it maximizes your optionality. 

You can sell a great biz for $100-$150m, then go do whatever you want. 

Or, you can keep running it. 

Or, you can keep the equity, and hire someone else to run it, and go bigger (like Kennan). 

If you’re a founder, keeping a low 9-fig exit on the table is an incredibly smart move.

Kennan did by accident. You should do it on purpose. 

You only need to get rich once. 

My advice is to have as many paths to that place as possible.

Oh and PS… The other reason he was able to sell besides not raising lots of dough??? 

THEY WERE A GROWING, PROFITABLE BUSINESS … 

Why do more people not live life this way!?!?! 

Congratulations, Kennan!
