## Exact post text

7 reasons why you should never take VC (explained in 60 seconds):

1. Dividends are good
2. Investor value prop is weak
3. A new seat at the table is net-negative
4. They’re in the portfolio game, you’re not
5. I want to do things investors won’t tollerate
6. I don’t believe in measuring things or sreadsheets
7. Large carrots in front of us make us make bad decisions

———

1. Dividends are good

If you create a successful early-stage startup, you should be rewarded by getting paid millions of dollars every year… Not just some *potentially* astronomical number at some point MAYBE in the future.

2. The investor value prop is weak (if you don’t need capital)

I listened to pitch after pitch of investors saying their “go-to-market advisory would be game-changing” for us. This pitch was weak and designed to get founders like me dreaming big dreams that if they panned out would largely be my team’s doing.

3. A new seat at the table is a net negative. Period

At best, investors will slow you down. At worst, they will ruin your life AND destroy your common equity if your business slows down for macro reasons outside of your control.

4. Investors are in the portfolio game. You’re not

Situations can emerge - FREQUENTLY - where what is best for your life and company can be usurped by a group of investors wanting a quick 2x. Even if they have a minority position, there are SO many ways they can exert influence and drive the outcome that is best for the fund.

5. I want to do things investors would not tolerate

My strategy for 2025 was to give away a super-valuable product using viral distribution strategies and do a lightning-fast PLG. I did it with a team that is unreasonably lean. I even let AI run RB2B for 7 days when the team went on vacation. THE SECOND any of this plan went sour, I would be told to stop, and if I didn’t, I’d be fired.

6. I don’t believe in measuring things or spreadsheets

I think almost all measurement (aside from top-line ARR) is garbage and leads to bad long-term decision making in favor of optimizing in the short term for whatever metric you’re going for.

7. Large carrots dangled in front of us make us make bad decisions

An investor’s job is to try to figure out what number will motivate the founder to transact, and once identified, get them to attach their identity to that number. We love validation that our beautiful business IS worth $100m, even if it actually lowers the probability-weighted financial outcome for the founder.

TAKEAWAY:

Last year I was seduced by the idea that I could take millions off the table.

After speaking to an investor a week, I realized that those millions would likely change the things about my life that I value most.

I’d sacrifice independence, freedom and it would slow us down.

That’s why I’m a bootstrapper at heart.

And I always will be.
