## Exact post text

145 slides of artisanal, hand-crafted data on US startups and venture capital. Here are the top 5 trends Peter Walker says founders need to know as we get into 2026:

1. AI ate 44% of invested capital (slide 10)

And that share climbs to 61% if you just look at software startups. If you are building a startup and not using AI, you should have a damn good reason why.

2. Founders sell wildly different amounts of equity for the same $ at pre-seed (slide 32)

Lots of capital sloshing around at the earliest stages of company building - but that capital is mostly chasing the same deals/founders. Meaning those in demand get to set their own prices and those in the cold scrap for attention. 

3. ARR at Series A is ~3x higher than in 2021 (slide 52)

Call it a $3M median for Series A B2B companies these days. And $7M is now 75th percentile. These are not inflation adjusted, but even so the pace seems to be getting faster by the day. 

4. The average new hire receives 50% less equity today than in 2022 (slide 84)

Startups are building with fewer employees than ever - meaning the leverage in hiring conversations is on the side of the employer. Salaries haven't kept pace with inflation and equity has been halved in 3 years. The only employee group bucking this trend are AI/ML engineers. 

5. Some venture funds can get better terms than the typical 2 and 20 (slide 134)

About 10-15% of the funds raising last year received carry of 25%-30% and some of them also grabbed management fees above the blended 2% benchmark. Just like with founders, certain GPs have a special allure. We'll check back in a decade to see if those higher fees resulted in better returns. 

----------

Now more than ever, Peter says (not me! Peter) founders should do some SERIOUS INTROSPECTION about whether to take VC money at all. If your ambition requires a $1B exit or an IPO — go meet some VCs. 

But you can build a fabulous business without any venture money. Picking the right capital game can make all the difference!
