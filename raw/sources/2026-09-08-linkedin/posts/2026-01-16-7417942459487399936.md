## Exact post text

If I were a 22-year-old Stanford grad who wanted to work 9-9-6 in the hopes of making life changing money, I WOULDN'T sell my soul to Y-Combinator and try to create the next OpenAI. I would do the opposite: 

1. Before I founded ANYTHING, I would first achieve mastery at engineering or product or sales and marketing. 

You could either do this by getting a job at high quality startup where you will both acquire skills AND learn what excellence truly is, or…

2. Start a company, but if it’s your first swing at it, AIM LOW.

Try to get something small and profitable working, where you gain the skill of creating and marketing a product, billing customers, and making them happy. 

On one end of the spectrum is a one-man consultancy or agency, on the other is sending humans to the moon. SaaS is in the middle.

Start simple.

3. Learn from your mistakes in a low-stakes, low pressure environment.

Entrepreneurship is a skill like anything else, and there are fundamental things like hiring and managing that are critically important to making something succeed that you need to master. 

It really helps to not have VCs breathing down your neck as you mess everything up the first few swings you take.

4. Start aiming higher as you become more successful and gain entrepreneurial prowess, and keep growing that stream of profits.

Take that slowly growing stream of profitability and start making larger, more complex bets that have asymmetric payoffs. 

Think SaaS, marketplace, vertical AI companies. 

5. After a decade or two, take the tens of millions of profits you are making, and go BIG.

At this point your skill as an entrepreneur is so much greater than when you were 22 and wanted to 9-9-6. 

In fact, you can probably execute a vision of the scale you wanted when you graduated from Stanford with a fraction of the capital and a normal work schedule. 

———

But you probably won’t do that. 

Why? 

Because it feels too good to go big (even though the stats show you are more than 80% likely to either fail or make no money).

And it feels better to send your mom a TechCrunch article about how your fledgling company is worth $100m+.

And what feels the best… Which you CAN’T let go of…

Is bragging to all your SF friends about how your team is doing 9-9-6.

Business is about two things. 

1. Making your customers happy
2. Making more money than you spend

If you keep doing that longer than anybody else, you win. 

Slow and steady wins the race. 

Not 9-9-6.
