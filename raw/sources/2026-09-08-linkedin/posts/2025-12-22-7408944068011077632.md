## Exact post text

I’ve written 1,000 posts about why VC money will destroy your startup, life, and you’d be better of dead. So... here are 8 scenarios where I WOULD absolutely take VC cash (and why it's better than bootstrapping):

1. I was making a physical product

Ryan Babenzien is more psychotic than me about tiny teams. Even he had to raise $2m to get Jolie off the ground. But they got to $50m rev w/ 3 ppl, generate 8-fig profit, and haven’t raised a dollar since.

2. If I was doing a rapid retail expansion

Whole Foods. Sunoco/Stripes filling stations. I’ve heard these two prolific entrepreneurs speak and we wouldn’t have heard of them without VC.

3. If I were playing some sort of lending game

If you are lending money, you need money. I’m talking about everything from Founderpath to JP Morgan Chase to everything in between.

4. If I had both 90% GRR and true 150%+ NRR

I do not know what this feels like. As of now, I only sell high churn product categories to high churn buyer personas. But if I had a business this sticky, depending on the TAM, I would CONTEMPLATE raising money because that business can get huge. But I would try to run it like Klaviyo, and break-even the whole way up.

5. If I had to build a hardware infrastructure to deliver the service

I’m thinking Google, OpenAI, etc. If AWS isn’t going to work for you and you literally can’t keep up with processing power for the demand for your product, you need outside capital.

6. If I were in a capital intensive space (like oil and gas)

If you need hundreds of millions of dollars to buy steel and pull things out of the ground, you probably aren’t bankrolling that yourself.

7. If my business had true network effects

If I were going for users and usage because that improved the experience of the product or improved the data users were consuming, and I didn’t have a pricing model that supported it in favor of user acquisition, VC might be the only way.

8. If I was “post liquidity” (a Sam Parr term)

Until I am really rich, I’m making high probability bets with my own resources that build cash-generating businesses. But “post-liquidity”… Look out. I might YOLO it.

———

LinkedIn LOVES to hate on me for saying bootstrapping’s the only way.

It’s not.

But in SaaS, unless:

I was CERTAIN I had a unicorn on my hands (not delusional like most)

I would not take a dollar of external captial.

Nobody in SaaS realizes that’s a possibility.

Not only is it possible, it’s WAY easier than the 9-figure ARR REQUIREMENT that most series-A and beyond $$$ comes with.

There is a fat middle between a lifestyle business and a unicorn.

If you stick with it long enough, you can have:

- A great product, brand, team
- Make $5-$10m/yr (or more)
- Work on only what you want to work on

The first time I saw it was Ross Andrew Paquette

Bootstrapped $30m ARR w/ 70% total margins.

He doesn’t need a transaction to have a financial outcome.

He wins every year, no matter what.

You can too.

Keep building.
