## Exact post text

Marty Kausas: Why do you hate VCs so much?

Me: How much time do you have?

BACKGROUND:

Marty is a young-buck YC-founder with early traction.

Ed Hallen is the co-founder of Klaviyo, an IPO’d decacorn.

Every Marty is hoping to become Ed someday. Ed has reached the endgame of the life the VC/YC path is selling, he's worth $100’s of mm now, and I spoke to him the other day about this.

Ed: “Most VC-backed founders end up failing. That just sucks for Founders.”

He told me about an article he wrote for the Harvard Business Review about what he calls the “Mighty Middle”.

He concluded sub-VC scale, bootstrapped companies in niche markets had far higher probabilities of creating life-changing outcomes for founders than the VC track...

And therefore are better for the Founders!

But that wasn’t the most interesting part.

When you talk to Ed about Klaviyo, you can tell he doesn’t think of himself and his co-founder as any more capable than their VC-backed peers.

You get the sense that he believes Klaviyo is where it is because of a few breaks that were totally out of their control. Things they NEVER saw coming.

1. Linking themselves with Shopify when it was a $50m valuation, now it’s $150b (a 300x!)

2. Mailchimp got in a fight and killed their integration w/ Shopify in 2018

3. Email is the one positive NRR product in SMB

What would have been the fate of Klaviyo if none of this was true?

We will never know, but my guess is it would be MUCH smaller than the $1.3b run rate (or whatever Klaviyo is now).

The last point Ed made on our call was my favorite.

“Hundreds of millions of dollars has zero utility beyond a number MUCH smaller.”

His point is that most Founders want the freedom to build cool shit, and live the life they want.

A huge amount of money in the bank, and the huge company that young founders think they want, can work AGAINST those goals.

But good luck trying to explain that to a 25yr old paying $3k/mo rent in SF. 'I'll find that out for myself', says the young Founder. Touché.

Last but not least, I always point out the VC path is dependent on transactions with volatile valuations for the Founder to realize the fruits of his labor. 

A decades-long effort of creating a VERY good business most of the time ends up in a very low-paying job with a boss (your investors). Why? Because if you're VC-backed and your business stops growing, your equity is torched. 

Klaviyo was the exception.

Is Pylon?

Only time will tell.

Marty and YC types make the mistake of bucketing Ed’s “Mighty Middle” in the “lifestyle business” category and dismissing us as “playing small”.

It’s not about playing small.

It’s about freedom, and making sure if Marty were to create a great business (and he probably will), the fruits of that labor accrue to Marty (which is unlikely).

So to answer your question, Marty.

Why do I hate VC’s so much?

Because I want YOU to win. And I want you to be free.

But I think by taking $50m, you minimized the odds of both.
