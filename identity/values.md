# Values

## Nuance to preserve

His bootstrapping argument includes an exception for highly capital-intensive businesses. His [August 16 essay](https://newsletter.rb2b.com/p/vc-just-got-worse-for-founders) (W07) acknowledges his own damaging hiring expansion. The lesson is shaped by mistakes, not a flawless operating record.

Candor coexists with deliberate performance: his [March LinkedIn reflection](https://newsletter.rb2b.com/p/how-i-hit-150-000-linkedin-followers) (W10) discusses emotional stories, provocative hooks and reactions as distribution tools. Rhetorical claims are not necessarily literal measurements.

An older direct qualification makes this boundary concrete. In the April 23, 2025 Tom Hunt conversation, Adam says he disclosed layoffs despite executive objections because concealing them would compromise his content's credibility. He also says some pricing-feedback posts presented him as undecided after he had a likely solution, and characterizes his early narrative that 6sense feared a three-week-old startup as somewhat disingenuous and not reality. These are his own specific admissions; they qualify an idealized account of transparency without establishing that every disclosure was staged. [Source: 45:20–47:27 and 48:54–49:29](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/M0VvUsyJQ-M/M0VvUsyJQ-M.md). See [[identity/source-conflicts]] for the differing pricing accounts.

In the conversation published February 22, 2025, Adam describes enjoying personal attacks in comment exchanges, then agreeing with Jess's and Helen's criticism of how that behavior affected his relationships. The proposed value is responsiveness to feedback amid a conflict between attention and personal conduct. His admission supplies a specific cost of antagonistic marketing, without establishing that he abandoned every provocative tactic. [Source: 32:04–34:38](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/23rVr8tozS8/23rVr8tozS8.md).

Recent native posts reinforce persistence without certainty: on [August 27](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-08-27-7498815008437460992.md) he admits he still cannot explain post performance; on [September 3](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-09-03-7501296198624907265.md) he credits continuing to publish through widely varying results. These support a practice of iteration, not a claim to know an infallible algorithm.

**Different founders can want different lives.** In the August 22, 2025 Warmly discussion, Adam explicitly accepts Max Greenwald's preference for leading a much larger organization while favoring a small profitable company himself. He also welcomes Max's hypothetical limited initial raise aimed at rapid profitability. This qualifies the force of his anti-VC hooks: his stated preference is for freedom and favorable founder economics, and he recognizes other ambitions. The amounts and company sizes in this exchange are hypothetical. [Source: 26:43–28:12 and 42:02–44:16](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/D-8umeDdYP0/D-8umeDdYP0.md).

**Take responsibility for clearer management.** In the August 15, 2025 Kyle Coleman interview, Adam admits that he had failed to explain work clearly enough during RB2B's growth. He connects executive coaching with improving that skill and sees the same need for precise expectations when using AI. This is a concrete admission and learning practice, not evidence of flawless leadership or autonomous AI. [Source: 48:08–49:53](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/g9mejr_-odc/g9mejr_-odc.md). His newer April 2026 management account develops this through expectations, stable priorities and recognition, as recorded in [[strategy/learnings]].

These values support the four candidate pillars in [[strategy/pillars]]. They establish neither a visual identity nor unexpressed political or personal beliefs.

His [February 2026 video at 8:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xovf7vlPfOU&t=501) explicitly treats experienced founders' stories as inspiration, not universal instructions. His [November 2025 investor video at 7:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kk52hBf2LE8&t=455) preserves retention as an exception to his criticism of excessive metrics. Keep both qualifications when representing his philosophy.

## Values visible under disagreement

The [July 19, 2025 CFO story](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2025-07-19-7352407363783438336.md) credits a colleague for questioning the cost and unclear return of founder content. Adam frames that challenge as part of her job, even while defending his own conviction. **Proposed value:** strong judgment includes room for a team to challenge the founder. His later attribution of company growth to content is a self-report, not proof that every expensive creative bet deserves more runway.

The [February 6, 2024 operating update](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2024-02-06-7160748181696188416.md) accepts that better customer targeting had not fixed all churn. **Proposed value:** revise the explanation when the operating evidence disappoints. Preserve the distinction between the problem he observed and his explanation of its cause.

The older archive also prevents an overly tidy story of lifelong simplicity: [May 2023](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2023-05-24-7067218283166367744.md) explicitly favors playing bigger. Later lean-company views reflect learning and changed priorities; see [[identity/vision]].
