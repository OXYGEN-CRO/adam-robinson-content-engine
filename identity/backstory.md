# Backstory

## Summary

Adam's public account runs from financial trading to bootstrapped email software, then visitor identification and identity data. He increasingly describes business building as creative expression supporting freedom and a sustainable life. This internal draft combines supplementary web evidence, recent captured LinkedIn posts and historical video evidence. It is not an approved biography. The documentary uploaded March 14, 2025 contains footage explicitly situated in 2023, so its ambitions and circumstances do not supersede the dated 2026 accounts.

## Turning points and interpretation

A recurring arc is learning to build a company that fits its owner: difficult experiments, discovery of a differentiated offer, profitable incubation and lean operations. In [June 2026](https://newsletter.rb2b.com/p/why-i-still-build-startups) (W08), he describes the first seven years as difficult and current company building as creative expression. This arc is editorial interpretation; the events above remain the evidence.

His [August 21 LinkedIn reflection](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-08-21-7496600255719755777.md) connects current excitement to a stronger team, fewer routine operating demands, and moving to Aspen. In [September 4's post](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin/posts/2026-09-04-7501664626724630528.md), he still admits frequent dissatisfaction: outward financial success does not erase the emotional difficulty of building.

## Stories behind the current philosophy

### Robly: profitable but stuck

The company launched in 2014 with a call-center approach to selling a low-price email subscription. A useful prospect list and personal onboarding supported early growth; later, the lead source weakened and the business stalled around $2.5–3m ARR. Adam recalls a 38-person operation in his apartment, layoffs, persistent anxiety and giving too much weight to consultants who said their specialty would fix the problem. The lesson he draws is that activity and purchased expertise cannot substitute for a differentiated product. Broadly date this plateau to the later 2010s; the retellings disagree on its precise endpoints. [P74] [P161] [P172]

### Ross Paquette and meeting Diana

In his May 31, 2026 account, a 2017 Ross Paquette podcast changed what Adam thought possible. He researched former Maropost salespeople, met Diana Ross, and says they decided to build together in 2018. The important personal fact is the change in ambition and the relationship it led to. Ross's revenue and staffing figures vary between Adam's retellings and remain attributed inspiration, not independently verified proof. [P59] [P17]

### An unwanted newsletter product reveals a better offer

Adam describes spending a year and $1m building Lead.com, then discovering at a conference that prospects did not care. Conversations changed when he described identifying visitors who had not completed a form. Later, Robly users tolerated downloading identity data and importing it into other email tools: that inconvenience, alongside strong satisfaction, signaled demand for the feature itself. He separated it into GetEmails and describes an initial $5,000 Facebook test producing $10,000 MRR. The causal lesson is his interpretation; neither this spend/result pair nor the $1m development figure is audited. Conference dates conflict, but the product-learning sequence is consistent. [P164] [P161]

### Robly's buyer relationship and fee regret

In the March 18, 2025 Aye Moah conversation, Adam says Robly's buyer was a customer he met at Affiliate Summit around 2015 and stayed in contact with for years. He regrets agreeing to a large banker fee after already lining up that buyer. The historical amounts are recorded in [[identity/proof]]; the approximate five-year interval does not resolve the disputed sale year. This is his account of one transaction and its costs, not an established scam or proof that trust replaces professional advice. Moah's acquisition is a separate guest case. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/7p-QD-3MtBw/7p-QD-3MtBw|00:28–01:43; 26:18–26:59; 52:55–54:31]]

### Retention.com: a broader name and a hiring mistake

The move from GetEmails to Retention.com also reflected a broader product ambition. In the May 20, 2025 conversation with Nathan Barry, Adam describes wanting a name that represented more than email and says the premium domain changed buyers' perception of the company. That brand effect is his subjective assessment, with no measured return. The historical price and its unresolved public-use boundary are retained in [[identity/proof]], rather than treated as a current company valuation. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/meZrgsAZWos/meZrgsAZWos|02:48–03:13 and 05:27–06:25]]

During 2022–23 he tried to expand aggressively, expecting a larger market than he found. Recent accounts vary between six or thirteen people growing to sixty. He associates the expansion with slow decisions, management demands, a missed growth target and layoffs. In his [March 24, 2026 video at 3:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aaBZ3f5TViQ&t=190), he says publicly admitting the miss and layoffs connected him to other B2B founders. This links the lean-company philosophy to building in public: it grew out of an operating mistake and the response to sharing it. [P68] [P17]

The historical documentary adds the incentive behind that expansion. In its early footage, Adam recalls an attempted sale of the then-current business falling through “last year,” seeing Jasper's rise from a shared office, and a conversation with someone Dave had hired that made his own opportunity seem much larger. He credits Santos Sharan with helping him pursue it. The exact failed-sale date and buyer are not established, and this is not the Robly transaction. Jasper's valuation and growth remain attributed context, not Adam's results. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/JZiUalL7h-8/JZiUalL7h-8|04:49–07:39]]

Later footage shows the cost of mistaking encouraging sales for a fully repeatable system. Adam says he had lost expense discipline and had not focused enough on product, customer success and sales. He pauses expansion and works toward financial resilience. In an offsite reflection he says the $50m ARR goal drove a series of decisions, including assuming sales and onboarding would be easy; he distinguishes velocity from FOMO and asks for optimism grounded in reality. Near the end he explicitly regrets declaring the audacious target and reaffirms a growing, profitable company that treats people well. These are corrections within the film itself, not conclusions inferred solely from his newer lean-company stance. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/JZiUalL7h-8/JZiUalL7h-8|1:16:56–1:19:32; 1:56:37–1:58:04; 2:22:09–2:23:49]]

### B2B discovery makes the founder's own experience relevant

The same historical footage captures the shift toward what would become RB2B. Adam describes testing the idea with people who run sales teams and recognizing demand for a visitor's LinkedIn identity and browsing context. He then changes his content toward sales and pipeline: unlike the day-to-day life of a Shopify merchant, those were problems he personally understood as a B2B founder. He reports that the new content generated beta interest, while the earlier DTC content served a less direct role. The proposed pricing and optimistic reactions are discovery-stage evidence, not achieved conversion rates or a public launch date. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/JZiUalL7h-8/JZiUalL7h-8|1:42:36–1:46:29; 1:51:10–1:54:39]]

This also changes his role. He credits Kyle with the DTC business's in-person sales motion and acknowledges he cannot travel to both DTC and B2B events while preserving home life. His response is to give the established operation capable owners and focus on small experiments until they justify more attention. The handoff is historical evidence for his later emphasis on autonomy through a team, not proof that Retention.com stopped needing people. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/JZiUalL7h-8/JZiUalL7h-8|1:53:33–1:54:39; 2:12:39–2:13:29; 2:15:02–2:15:39]]

### Sobriety, family and Aspen

In April 2026 Adam describes moving from nightly drinking, frequent partying and feeling lost toward sustained sobriety, marriage, two young children and living in Aspen. He calls quitting alcohol his most consequential personal decision of the preceding decade. Record that as his interpretation of his own life, not a health-effect claim or advice to others. The same post says both six and 5.5 years sober; the exact anniversary is unresolved. [P79]

His January 13 post specifically dates the family move to January 6, 2026 and links it to work flexibility, skiing and the team he trusts. The useful brand context is chosen place, family and control over his work; precise home/office location and children's details add little and should stay out of derivative public copy unless specifically needed. [P145]

### The team makes autonomy possible

An earlier engineering story illustrates the distinction between doing technical work and managing people. Adam recalls moving to Argentina to build Robly's team, then seeing better results after a local manager led it. For Retention.com, he says an engineering manager worked alongside Tate before they added engineers. His lesson is to establish management capacity before expanding the team. Dates are unspecified, the claimed improvement is not independently measured, and the story does not establish that geography determined performance. [[raw/sources/2026-09-08-youtube/videos/JaN_78GB638/JaN_78GB638|November 13, 2024 publication, 40:18–42:14]]

His December 31, 2025 retrospective explicitly corrects his own three-people-and-AI storyline by crediting the full 33-person organization. Tate, Diana, Michael, Keleigh, Robb and the data/customer teams make the model work. In April 2026 he credits Jonathan Shapiro's coaching and EOS with clearer expectations, more stable priorities and better management. June hiring of Ebru and Cecilia demonstrates that staying lean still allows adding valuable people. The story is earned delegation and good operating design, not a literal company without human work. [P154] [P84] [P51]

## Contemporaneous LinkedIn anchors added from RB2B

The older posts add dated evidence alongside later recollections. In [July 2023](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2023-07-28-7090775676135362561.md), Adam describes large Shopify stores as Retention.com's ideal customer and presents rapid team expansion as part of going faster when the time is right. In [February 2024](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2024-02-06-7160748181696188416.md), he describes overselling, a sales-leadership departure, a smaller sales organization, reduced expectations and churn that remained difficult despite tighter targeting. These support a change in operating judgment; neither post settles every headcount discrepancy in the later retellings.

The [January 11, 2024 RB2B playbook](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2024-01-11-7151330853724258306.md) is explicitly prelaunch: a free offering, community, founder distribution and future feature gating are plans. It ends by admitting the approach may not work. Keep this prospective account distinct from later launch results and revised pricing.

The [November 2023 Robly/GetEmails story](../raw/sources/2026-09-08-linkedin-rb2b/posts/2023-11-11-7129220348616593409.md) already explains why he shares business setbacks: he wished he could have heard from a founder going through the same thing. Its republication in later years does not make the underlying events newer or create independent corroboration.

Publicly sourced stories are not automatically cleared for new first-person copy. ## Unresolved chronology

Adam recalls writing himself a letter about the lean company and freedom he wanted. A [February 6, 2026 video at 7:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xovf7vlPfOU&t=429) says 2016 and $2.5m ARR; his [June 25 newsletter](https://newsletter.rb2b.com/p/why-i-still-build-startups) says 2015 and $3m ARR. The aspiration is supported, but the precise year and financial plateau need reconciliation. Automatic-caption names also require care: that video's apparent Albacross reference conflicts with the surrounding RB2B narrative and is not evidence he founded Albacross.

The documentary contains another goal statement explicitly introduced as **April 2017**, concerning Lead.com, $2m MRR by his 42nd birthday, product quality, staff and discipline. In the surrounding recording he says that birthday was six months earlier and still targets the following birthday. Keep this as a separately described document; it does not resolve the 2015/2016 freedom-letter discrepancy or establish that the revenue goal was reached.
